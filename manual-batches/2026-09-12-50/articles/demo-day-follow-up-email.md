An investor follow-up email after Demo Day should reconnect the recipient with the conversation, deliver the item or answer they requested, and propose one useful next step. Keep the company description brief, use descriptive links, and include a video only when it directly explains the product question under discussion.

## Reconstruct the conversation before writing

Start with the notes you captured after the pitch or meeting. Record who the investor is, where you spoke, which question they asked, and what you promised to send. These details determine the email's purpose.

If you did not speak directly, say how the connection arose without implying a conversation that never happened. An introduction from a shared contact, attendance at the same event, and a substantive meeting are different contexts.

YC's seed-fundraising guide emphasizes leaving investor meetings with clear next steps. Use the email to confirm that next step in a form both people can refer back to. [YC's guide to seed fundraising](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)

Write the purpose as a verb: send, answer, confirm, or schedule. If the draft tries to do all four at equal length, choose the action that advances the existing conversation and make the others supporting details.

## Match the asset to the actual request

A requested deck should be easy to find. A product question may need a short workflow recording. A request for a definition or figure may need a written answer and a supporting record. Select the smallest asset that makes the response complete.

Use descriptive link text that explains the content. “September product overview” and “Review-and-approval workflow” provide more context than “click here.” If the material is dated, include that date or version in the description.

Check the recipient's access before sending. A correct link can still open a login screen or an obsolete version. Also confirm that the asset is suitable for the person and the forwarding context. Do not encourage broad circulation of material intended only for a specific discussion.

Keep the main answer in the email when possible. The recipient should understand why the asset is relevant before deciding whether to open it. Avoid making a video mandatory viewing just to discover the next step.

## Use a concise requested-material template

The following is an original template. Replace each bracketed field with verified details and remove any line that does not fit the conversation.

> Subject: [Company] — [requested material] after [event]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We spoke at [event] about [specific question]. [Company] helps [user] complete [task].
>
> Here is the [requested asset], covering [what it answers]. The product shown is [current status or version, if relevant].
>
> You asked about [remaining issue]. [Direct answer or a clear statement of what is still being checked.]
>
> Would a short conversation about [specific next topic] be useful? I can do [availability with time zone], or work around your schedule.
>
> Best,
> [Founder]

The template works because each sentence has a distinct job. The first restores context, the second delivers, and the ending proposes a bounded next step. It does not need a lengthy recap of the entire Demo Day pitch.

Before sending, read only the subject and first paragraph. They should make the email recognizable without relying on a clever hook or a claim of urgency.

## Follow up honestly when an answer is still missing

Do not hold every response until you can produce a polished video. A short acknowledgment can clarify what you are checking and when the recipient should expect the answer.

Techstars recommends being honest when a pitch question cannot be answered and following up with the researched response. Apply that principle to the email's wording and internal ownership. [Techstars' guidance on answering pitch questions](https://toolkit.techstars.com/master-your-pitch)

Use this original example for an unresolved technical question:

> Subject: [Company] — follow-up on [technical question]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> You asked whether [specific behavior] works under [condition]. I have confirmed [verified portion]. I am checking [unresolved portion] with [responsible role] before giving you a complete answer.
>
> I will send the result by [realistic date]. In the meantime, [optional approved material] explains the current workflow.
>
> Best,
> [Founder]

Only promise a date the responsible person can meet. If the answer changes the impression created in the pitch, explain the correction directly. Do not hide it behind a new attachment or a vague product update.

## Choose a next step that respects the conversation

A useful next step has a topic and a purpose. “Discuss how the approval workflow fits multi-location teams” gives the investor more information than “find synergies.” Make the requested commitment proportionate to what has already been discussed.

If a meeting was agreed, confirm its date, time zone, and focus. If it was not, offer a concise invitation without treating interest as a commitment. When the investor requested only material, delivering it can be the complete action for now.

Use an internal follow-up log:

| Field | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Contact and context | Who the recipient is and how you connected |
| Exact request | The item or question they asked for |
| Asset version | Which approved material you sent |
| Open issue | What still needs an answer |
| Owner and due date | Who will complete the next action |
| Outcome | Reply, meeting, decline, or no response |

The log supports reliable follow-through. It should not convert every email open or asset view into a claim about investor interest.

## Send a reminder only when it has a clear job

If the recipient gave you a follow-up date, use that agreement. Otherwise, choose a modest reminder based on the context and your team's judgment. There is no need to invent a universal sequence that supposedly works for every investor.

An original reminder could read:

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on the [asset or answer] I sent after [event]. It addresses your question about [topic].
>
> If [proposed conversation] would be useful, I am available [times and time zone]. If the timing is not right, no action is needed.
>
> Best,
> [Founder]

Add new information only when it is real and relevant. Do not manufacture a deadline, suggest a commitment that does not exist, or repeatedly send the same recording under a new subject.

If the investor declines, record the decision and close the immediate follow-up. If they ask to hear about a future milestone, note the actual milestone and communicate when there is a substantive update.

## Run a final pre-send check

Verify the recipient, conversation reference, answer, link permissions, dates, and requested next step. Remove any placeholder that remains in the draft. Read the message aloud once to catch language that sounds more insistent than intended.

Compare the email's product claims with the linked asset. An old demo labeled as current can create confusion even when both the message and file are individually polished. Correct the version or explain the difference.

Then assign ownership for any promise you made. The most useful follow-up email is one your team can fulfill. Keep a copy of the sent material and update the request log when the next action is complete.
