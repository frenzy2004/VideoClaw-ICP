Plan a two-founder investor demo by assigning speaking, product operation, and presentation control separately. Use an explicit handoff cue and a stable product state before the second founder begins. Rehearse the return as well as the transfer, so neither person advances the screen while the other is still explaining it.

## Separate the three jobs before assigning people

Two founders can share a demonstration without both controlling everything. Identify three jobs: explain the current point, operate the product, and control the audience's presentation view. One person may hold several jobs, but the assignments should be deliberate.

For a fictional dispatch product, one founder might explain the dispatcher workflow while the technical cofounder operates the application. The second founder might then explain the technician result while the first retains control of the deck. That arrangement does not require transferring every control at the speaking handoff.

Decide why the second founder appears. Their contribution should clarify a product action, technical boundary, or result that benefits from their involvement. Avoid adding a handoff merely to divide the airtime evenly.

[YC's virtual-event guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/hosting-a-virtual-event) recommends a dry run and clarity about slide control. Apply that discipline to the two people in this demo, keeping the scope to your presentation rather than designing an event-wide production system.

## Choose a handoff state the audience can recognize

Transfer the speaking role at a stable point in the workflow. A completed action, a readable result, or a deliberately held starting screen gives the audience a place to orient themselves while the voice changes.

Do not hand off halfway through an invisible operation unless the change of speaker is necessary to explain that operation. Otherwise, the viewer must track a new person and a changing screen simultaneously.

Write the state in operational terms. “Job J-17 is assigned and the recipient screen is visible” is more useful than “after the product section.” It tells the operator exactly when to stop clicking and tells the next speaker what the audience can see.

Choose a short spoken cue, such as naming the cofounder and the next viewing task. Keep it natural. This is a coordination signal, not a full pitch script. The receiving founder should begin only after the screen has reached the agreed state and the previous speaker has yielded.

## Build a handoff score for the actual controls

Map the sequence with separate columns for voice, product input, and audience view. The following fictional score uses two founders, Leena and Sam.

| Moment | Speaking | Product operation | Audience view |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduce the task | Leena | Sam waits at the starting state | Product window already selected |
| Perform assignment | Leena gives brief context | Sam makes the agreed action | Same product window |
| Hold result | Leena finishes and names Sam | Both stop input | Result stays visible |
| Explain recipient behavior | Sam | Sam opens the planned recipient state | View changes only on the agreed cue |
| Return to company discussion | Sam yields to Leena | Sam stops product interaction | Leena restores the next deck slide |

The score should reveal collisions. If both founders intend to press the next-slide key at the same moment, change the assignment. If the person speaking cannot see the audience's current view, give them a reliable visual reference or simplify the arrangement.

Keep a single version of this score with the final demo file or account-state notes. A rehearsal based on yesterday's sequence can create a confident but incorrect handoff.

## Test slide control separately from product control

A slide-control feature is not general permission to operate a product application. [Zoom's slide-control documentation](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0061986) describes specific presentation modes and focus requirements. Confirm the behavior of your exact configuration before relying on it.

If one founder operates the product locally, keep that operation local unless a remote-control transfer is genuinely needed and explicitly permitted. Moving keyboard or mouse control between people adds another state to manage. It should solve a real demo problem.

For remote cofounders, test what each person sees when the shared window changes. A founder may be watching the meeting's received view while the operator sees a different local arrangement. Ask each person to describe the current screen during rehearsal.

Avoid changing account-wide meeting settings solely for this presentation without appropriate authority. If a feature is unavailable, choose a simpler authorized arrangement, such as one operator and two speakers. The goal is a dependable handoff, not maximum use of platform features.

## Rehearse the seams and one interruption

Run the opening handoff, the product-state transition, and the return to the deck as short separate exercises. These seams often need more attention than the middle of a familiar product workflow.

Have a colleague interrupt at the planned handoff with a product question. Decide who answers based on the question, then explicitly return to the held state. The operator should not continue clicking simply because the original schedule says it is time.

Practice a delayed cue too. The next founder should wait for the actual state, not start speaking at a memorized second. If the result takes longer in this run, maintain a coherent explanation without implying the state has already changed.

Listen for duplicate explanations. The second founder does not need to reintroduce the whole product after a short handoff. A brief reference to the visible result can connect the contributions.

Record the rehearsal if everyone involved agrees, or take notes. Review the moments where voice, input, and view stopped matching. Repair the assignment or cue rather than adding more narration to cover the confusion.

## Lock the simple arrangement and use it consistently

After a successful rehearsal, write down the exact control arrangement: which computer shares, who advances slides, who operates the product, and what state marks each transfer. Include the start and ending screens.

Repeat a short handoff test if the meeting tool, computer, or presentation file changes. A new setup can alter focus, shortcuts, or the audience view even when both founders know their spoken parts.

During the actual demo, let one person own an action until it is visibly complete. The other can ask for a pause but should not compete for the same control. If the investor asks a question, hold the current evidence while the appropriate founder answers.

The work product is a handoff score that lets two people coordinate around the product, not a script that makes them sound rehearsed word for word.
