Match each accelerator application field to a specific viewing job before recording anything. A founder introduction, a product demonstration, and a pitch deck are different assets, even when one application requests all three. Build a requirement-to-file map from current instructions, then test the exact links you intend to submit.

## Start with the destination, not your existing video

Open the application you will actually submit and identify every place that accepts media. Write down the field name, nearby instructions, accepted delivery method, and any stated limits. Keep unknowns visible. An empty box in your notes is more useful than an assumed rule copied from another accelerator.

Treat the application form as the destination and your recordings as candidate inputs. Your launch film may introduce the problem beautifully but never show the founders speaking. A customer walkthrough may demonstrate a workflow while spending almost no time on why this particular team is building it. Neither asset becomes suitable simply by changing its filename.

Separate explicit requirements from your own production decisions. “All founders appear” can be an instruction. “Use matching backgrounds” is a preference. This distinction helps you spend scarce recording time on material that answers the field rather than polishing details nobody requested.

Before scheduling people, resolve instructions that would change the recording: who must appear, whether product footage belongs there, and how long the file may run. Questions about decorative titles can wait.

## Make a field map for each program

Do not assume that similarly named fields mean identical things. [YC's application video instructions](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/) call for a one-minute founders-only video and direct product demonstrations elsewhere. The [Techstars application preview](https://www.techstars.com/application-preview) separately names product-demo, pitch-deck, and team-introduction materials. Check the current form for its detailed requirements.

Use one row per destination, even if two destinations eventually share an asset. The following is a fictional preparation worksheet, not a substitute for either program's instructions.

| Destination | Reader's immediate job | Candidate material | Remove or resolve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YC founder-video field | Meet the people and understand what they are doing | New founders-speaking take | Product montage and promotional opening |
| YC product-demo field | See the product operate | Focused workflow recording | Check current field's delivery rules |
| Techstars team introduction | Understand the team | Team-focused recording | Confirm current field instructions |
| Techstars product demonstration | Inspect the product | Product-focused recording | Do not assume another program's limits |
| Techstars pitch deck | Read the company presentation | Current deck | Reconcile names and product descriptions |

Add two columns when using this privately: “instruction checked” and “final destination URL.” Those columns separate understanding the assignment from successfully delivering the right file.

## Decide what can be reused without changing the answer

Review existing media by its content, not its production cost. Watch the whole file once with only the destination's question in mind. Mark the moment the file starts answering that question and the moment it stops.

A short recording of the founders explaining their work might fit more than one team-oriented destination if the relevant instructions permit it. A product clip might also serve several applications if the product state and context remain current. Reuse is a fit decision, not a promise that every application should receive the same package.

Do not manufacture a founders-only take by surrounding a launch ad with a few seconds of faces. Equally, do not submit a talking-head introduction as proof of a product workflow. The receiving field determines which missing evidence matters.

For a fictional scheduling startup, the reusable product segment might show a dispatcher assigning one job and the technician seeing that assignment. The team recording answers a different question: who these founders are and why they are working on scheduling. Keep both assets focused enough that the reviewer does not have to hunt for the relevant part.

## Record the missing asset with the smallest useful scope

Once the map is filled, record only the gaps. If the product demonstration is suitable but the founder introduction is not, leave the demonstration alone unless its information is stale. Avoid turning a submission correction into a complete media rebrand.

Prepare a small card for each missing asset:

- The destination field and the question it asks.
- The people or product state that must be visible.
- The opening context a first-time viewer needs.
- The final fact or result the viewer should understand.
- The applicable duration and delivery instructions.

Keep this card beside the recording setup, not inside the submitted video. It is a production aid. Use it to stop a founder from drifting into the product tour when recording the team introduction, or from adding a long company biography before the product action.

Record a rough take and route it through the map before polishing. A technically imperfect but correctly targeted take exposes missing information earlier than a beautiful edit built around the wrong job. Fix unclear speech and unreadable screens after confirming that the content belongs in the field.

## Test the exact submission path

A video that plays for its owner may not play for an application reviewer. Copy the intended submission link into a separate signed-out browser session where that is compatible with the required access method. If access is restricted to named viewers, test using a permitted recipient account instead.

Start from the link, not from your media library. Confirm that it opens the intended asset, displays a sensible title, and plays the complete file. Listen to the beginning and ending. Check that processing has finished and that the duration shown matches your intended export.

Then return to the application and compare each populated field against its worksheet row. This catches the surprisingly ordinary mistake of pasting the founder-video URL into the product-demo field. Compare visible content as well as filenames; two files called “application final” are not a useful distinction.

If the portal offers a preview, inspect it without changing unrelated application answers. Record which file version each field points to. Do not assume that replacing a local file changes a hosted link or that replacing hosted media updates a previously uploaded attachment.

## Freeze a submission receipt you can understand later

Keep a compact private receipt containing the program, submission date, field names, final URLs or uploaded filenames, and the version of each asset. This is a memory aid for your team, not an extra attachment unless the application asks for one.

Use meaningful filenames such as “team-introduction-september” and “dispatcher-product-demo-september.” If the portal renames uploads, retain the original-to-portal mapping. That makes later questions answerable without opening a pile of indistinguishable exports.

After submission, stop treating all recordings as interchangeable. An updated product clip may be useful for an interview, but it does not automatically replace what the application contains. Follow the program's current update process when a material change needs to be communicated.
