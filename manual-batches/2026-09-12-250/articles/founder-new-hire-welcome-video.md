Record a founder welcome for a confirmed new hire by greeting them directly, explaining why their role matters, and showing one real working norm. Reassure them about the first day and name a person who can help. Keep the message separate from recruitment persuasion, policy training, and the detailed instructions needed to start work.

## Write for someone who has already said yes

A new employee does not need another pitch about why the company is exciting. They need to understand where they fit, what the founder values in everyday work, and whether asking basic questions is welcome.

Start with the recipient's actual situation. Is this their first day, the week before they begin, or a welcome shared with a group of new colleagues? Do not imply that equipment, access, or a manager introduction is complete unless someone has checked.

[BambooHR's onboarding guidance describes welcome videos as a way to introduce the mission and the employee's contribution](https://www.bamboohr.com/blog/videos-for-onboarding). The founder's particular job is to make that contribution understandable without delivering the entire onboarding program.

Write a simple audience promise: “After watching, this person should know why their work matters and who to ask when they are unsure.” If a paragraph does not support that promise, consider moving it elsewhere.

Avoid language that implies constant availability, unpaid preparation, or heroic sacrifice. A warm welcome should not quietly establish unreasonable expectations.

## Build a five-beat welcome sheet

Use five rows: greeting, contribution, working norm, first-day reassurance, and human contact. Put a concrete sentence in each row.

The greeting identifies the person and role. The contribution connects their work to a real team or customer need. The working norm explains one behavior with an example. The reassurance names what the employee is not expected to know yet. The contact gives them a specific route for questions.

For a fictional founder welcoming a support specialist, the contribution might be: “Your work will help us understand where customers get stuck, not only close individual conversations.” The norm could be: “When an instruction is unclear, we write down the missing context so the next person does not have to rediscover it.”

Do not use a value word alone. “We value ownership” could mean independent judgment, escalation, or working without support. Explain the behavior you actually expect.

Have the employee's manager check the sheet. The founder's message should reinforce the real onboarding plan, not create a parallel set of promises.

## Make the personal opening truthful

A short personal opening can acknowledge the new hire's role or something relevant from the hiring conversation, provided it is appropriate to share. Confirm pronunciation and avoid references that may feel private or overly familiar.

You can record a reusable company welcome and a separate personal opening, but be honest about that structure. “Here is a welcome I share with everyone joining us, and first I wanted to say…” is more straightforward than pretending the entire message was created for one person.

If welcoming a group, address the group. Do not insert one person's name into generic wording that otherwise suggests a different role or team.

Keep personal praise specific and modest. “I appreciated how you explained your approach to documenting a difficult handoff” is more meaningful than “You are going to transform the company,” when the former accurately reflects the conversation.

The opening should make the employee feel recognized, not burdened with a large promise before they have met the team.

## Record a completed fictional welcome

Here is an original example for a fictional company called Harbor:

“Welcome, Sam. I am glad you are joining our customer support team. Your manager will guide your first week, and this message is simply my welcome.

“One thing I want you to know is that a customer's question can reveal something we need to explain better. When an instruction is unclear, we want to improve the shared explanation, not leave the next teammate to work it out alone. For example, if a customer asks who can approve a workspace change, record the missing distinction as well as answering the question.

“You are not expected to know every answer on your first day. Your onboarding contact is listed in your welcome note. If something is missing or confusing, please ask them. We are glad you are here, and I look forward to learning from your perspective.”

The names and company are fictional. In a real version, include the actual contact rather than leaving the recipient to infer who it is.

Record in a calm, ordinary setting. This is a human introduction, not a high-energy recruitment trailer. Leave enough space between ideas that the person can follow the message without already knowing company vocabulary.

## Connect the welcome to a real person and current information

[GitLab's remote-onboarding guidance identifies an onboarding buddy as a friendly point of contact](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/onboarding/). The transferable point is not to copy GitLab's organization, but to ensure the welcome leads to a person who is actually prepared to help.

Confirm that the named contact knows their responsibility and is available during the relevant period. If they will be away, give the new hire an agreed backup. A name in a video is not useful if that person does not know they are the first contact.

Put changing details in the accompanying note: the first meeting, access instructions, contact information, and links to official policies. Avoid embedding information that will become stale in a general founder recording.

Check access from the recipient's actual stage. A pre-start employee may not yet be able to open internal links. Do not ask them to solve an access problem through a channel they cannot reach.

Keep the video optional to revisit rather than treating watching it as proof that the employee understands every expectation.

## Review the welcome for clarity and belonging

Ask a recent hire or colleague unfamiliar with the draft what they learned about the role and where they would go with a question. If they remember only the company's ambition, make the practical parts clearer.

Check that the working norm is real. If the founder says questions are welcome but the onboarding plan provides no route for them, fix the process before repeating the phrase.

Review the final file for correct names, role, and timing. A copied greeting with the wrong name is a small production error with a large interpersonal effect. Save the personal opening with the intended recipient clearly identified.

Update the reusable section when the company norm or onboarding arrangement changes. Keep old versions from being sent accidentally.
