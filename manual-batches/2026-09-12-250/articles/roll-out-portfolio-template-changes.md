Update shared portfolio templates by identifying which company copies depend on the changed master, deciding whether each needs adoption, migration or retirement, and testing a new output before closing the rollout. A release note alone is not enough. Use a template dependency register that distinguishes the reusable source from files already produced with it.

## Name the template family and the change

A portfolio media program may maintain speaker slides, founder quote cards, event introductions or worksheet layouts. Give each reusable family a stable identifier and identify its owner. The identifier should survive a file rename so company copies remain connected to the source.

Describe the change in terms of its effect on future work. A color adjustment, a required attribution field and a new layout structure create different adoption tasks. Avoid calling every revision a “brand refresh” when users need to know exactly what changes in their next output.

[Bynder's template documentation](https://support.bynder.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013873200-Manage-Print-Brand-Templates-and-Presets) describes configurable templates and presets, including fields and styles. It is an example of template dependencies, not a claim that the same controls exist in every media editor.

Separate template revision from approval of a finished asset. A company may have an approved quote card created with an older template. Updating the master does not automatically invalidate that published card or approve a new export. Decide the handling of future work and existing outputs explicitly.

## Inventory copies before announcing a rollout

List the master, company-specific derivatives, shared presets, training examples and downloadable starter packages. Include copies that live outside the main library when the platform knows about them. A template cannot be considered fully adopted merely because the central file changed.

Record the company copy's current revision, local owner, custom fields and relationship to the master. Some copies may be exact duplicates; others may contain legitimate company-specific changes. Do not overwrite local work without understanding what needs to be preserved.

[Canto's version-control discussion](https://www.canto.com/blog/version-control-for-digital-assets/) distinguishes current files and approved sharing context. For template rollout, use that distinction to label the master revision clearly while separately tracking which derivative has been reviewed for use.

Keep unknown copies visible as an uncertainty. Ask company contacts where they obtain the template and which version they currently use. The answer may reveal an old email attachment or a workshop download that remains easier to find than the maintained source.

## Populate a dependency and disposition register

Imagine a fictional quote-card template, QC-04, changing from revision 2 to revision 3. The new revision adds a required source-reference field and changes the text area. Companies have adapted the previous master in different ways.

| Dependent item | Current state | Effect of revision 3 | Disposition | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder quote-card copy | Uses revision 2 with company colors | New source field must be added | Migrate local colors into revision 3 | Test export and company owner check |
| Beacon starter package | Unmodified revision 2 | Can use new master directly | Adopt revision 3 | Download link replaced and checked |
| Cobalt custom layout | Local layout cannot fit the new field | Requires a design decision | Hold migration pending owner review | Approved revised layout reference |
| Old workshop example | Demonstrates revision 1 | Misleads new users | Retire from current learning path | Replacement example linked |
| Already-published Alder card | Final output from revision 2 | Not automatically affected | Review only if its source information needs correction | Separate asset decision if required |

The register prevents a blanket instruction to “replace all old versions.” It distinguishes reusable copies that affect future outputs from completed media that may remain valid in its original context.

Add the change owner, company contact, deadline reason and unresolved question. A row is not complete because the owner received the announcement; it is complete when the specified evidence exists.

## Write an adoption packet people can act on

The packet should include the new master reference, a short change summary, affected fields, migration instructions and one checked example. State whether users should begin from the new master or adapt an existing company copy.

Show what to preserve. In the fictional Alder migration, company colors and approved logo placement remain local choices, while the source-reference field becomes part of the shared structure. A reviewer can then check both requirements without treating every local difference as a defect.

Name what should not be copied forward. An old placeholder quote, retired sponsor mark or example customer name may be part of the previous template file but not appropriate for the new copy. Template migration is an opportunity to remove accidental content dependencies.

Provide a route for exceptions. Cobalt's owner needs a decision about its layout, not repeated reminders to download a file. Keep the exception bounded: which requirement cannot fit, what alternative is proposed and who must approve it.

## Test a real output from each important variant

Ask each affected owner to create a harmless test output using the revised copy. Use fictional content or approved sample material so the check does not introduce a new publication risk. Inspect the export, not only the editable canvas.

For QC-04, the test checks that the source-reference field appears, the quote remains legible, company-specific elements are preserved and no old placeholder content leaks into the output. The template owner verifies shared requirements; the company owner verifies its local facts and presentation.

Check the retrieval route as well. A perfect migrated template is not useful if founders still download the old starter package. Replace or clearly retire current entry points under the platform's control and ask company owners to update their own maintained links.

Do not assume changes propagate automatically. If the application has linked-template behavior, verify what it actually updates and what remains independent. If it does not, the register provides the manual adoption record.

## Close rollout while preserving the history

Mark each dependency adopted, migrated, retired or unresolved, with its evidence reference. Keep the old master available only through the appropriate historical route if the program needs it. Avoid making retired templates compete with current starter files in everyday discovery.

Record why a company remains on an older copy if an exception is accepted. The note should identify the scope and next review trigger, not imply that the entire template family has completed rollout.

Review whether the change process found all important dependencies. Repeated late discoveries suggest that company copies need better registration or that starter packages are being distributed without a maintained source link.

The final work product is a template rollout register with checked outputs and clear exceptions. It helps a multi-company media program change future production inputs without confusing a new master, a migrated company copy and an already-approved finished asset.
