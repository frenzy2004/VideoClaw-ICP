Give the event AV operator a cue sheet that identifies one exact demo clip, one unambiguous start and one agreed return to the presentation. The operator should not have to infer your intention from a filename or a rehearsal memory. Build the handoff around the actual event playback system and verify it together.

## Establish who owns each playback action

Ask whether the operator, founder or presentation software starts the movie. Confirm who advances to the video slide and who returns to the next slide. Those responsibilities may belong to different people, but they must not overlap accidentally.

A founder pressing advance while an operator also launches the clip can create a skipped cue or an unexpected transition. Assign each action to one owner. If the event has a standard procedure, adapt your single-company handoff to it.

[YC’s virtual-event guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/hosting-a-virtual-event) emphasizes coordination and rehearsal around presentation operation. Apply that principle to your particular clip rather than creating an event-wide production plan the operator did not request.

Name a contact who can answer questions about your file before the session. They should know which revision is approved and whether the clip contains narration. Avoid sending conflicting instructions separately from two cofounders; consolidate the final answer into one cue sheet.

## Write the cue around a distinctive spoken landmark

Choose a start cue that the operator can recognize once and act on confidently. “Play when I say demo” is weak if the word appears several times in your introduction. Use a deliberate phrase positioned immediately before the clip.

For a fictional robotics founder, the cue might be: “Let’s watch the arm sort this mixed tray.” Rehearse the sentence at the intended point. Do not insert a second nearly identical sentence earlier in the talk.

Clarify whether the operator acts on the start or completion of the phrase. Include any expected pause after it. The founder should know whether to stop speaking immediately or continue over a silent opening segment.

Keep audience-facing language natural. The goal is not to turn the pitch into a broadcast script full of technical commands. A simple phrase can serve both the story and the operator, provided its meaning and timing are agreed in advance.

## Build the single-company playback sheet

Use this original handoff sheet for one video cue. Replace the fictional entries with your actual event specification and approved asset. Include the sheet in the delivery method the organizer requests.

| Field | Fictional completed entry |
| --- | --- |
| Company and cue | Tray Robotics, product demonstration cue 1 |
| Approved file | tray-sort-review-C.mp4; record actual byte size |
| Runtime | Enter the measured duration of this exact export |
| Start owner | AV operator, after founder finishes the cue sentence |
| Spoken cue | “Let’s watch the arm sort this mixed tray.” |
| First frame | Wide tray view, product and fictional demonstration label |
| Audio expectation | Product narration and mechanism sound; founder remains silent |
| End behavior | Hold the result frame until founder resumes |
| Return cue | Founder says “That was the complete sorting pass.” |
| Next view | Approved next slide, identified by number and title |
| Receipt and rehearsal | Operator name, received revision, test date and result |

If the movie’s last frame cannot remain on screen in the chosen system, agree on a separate result slide or another practical ending. Do not write “hold” on the sheet and assume every player implements that instruction automatically.

Describe the first frame specifically enough to distinguish a wrong revision. A company logo alone may appear on every export. The tray contents, demonstration label or visible starting task can provide a better visual identifier.

## Align software behavior with the cue

If the video lives inside PowerPoint, inspect its start behavior. [Microsoft distinguishes automatic playback on slide arrival from playback in the click sequence](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/play-a-video-automatically-in-a-slide-show). Your cue sheet should match whichever behavior the event actually uses.

For automatic playback, the action that reveals the slide is effectively the movie-start cue. For click-sequence playback, another advance action may be involved. Rehearse the real deck rather than describing an imagined standalone player.

If the operator uses a dedicated playback application, ask how they will preload, start and end the file. Record the answers in operational language the operator recognizes. Avoid demanding controls that belong to a different application.

Confirm the audio relationship too. A narrated movie and a founder speaking over it create different operating needs. State when the founder is silent, when product sound matters and whether any audio change is expected at the return to the deck.

## Rehearse the boundaries, not only the middle

Start the rehearsal before the spoken cue. Have the founder deliver the preceding sentence, the cue and the intended pause. Watch what the operator actually does and what the audience display shows.

Play through the entire clip at least once on the receiving system. Then rehearse the final seconds and return to the deck. Many handoff problems live at the edges: a clipped first word, an unexpected replay or a slide advance while the founder still refers to the result.

Ask the operator to repeat the agreed sequence back: file identity, start action, audio expectation, ending and next view. This is a mutual confirmation, not a test of the operator’s memory. Correct the sheet if either person interprets it differently.

Record the result as an observation: “Revision C tested from preceding slide; narration began after picture; result held; next slide appeared on agreed cue.” Do not mark a rehearsal complete when you only received confirmation that the download arrived.

## Freeze instructions and handle revisions explicitly

Once the cue works, freeze the file and sheet together. A new export can change the runtime, first frame or end hold even when the founder’s spoken words remain unchanged. Treat that as a changed handoff.

If a revision is necessary, send a concise replacement note naming the old and new files and the affected cue fields. Ask for acknowledgment of the new copy and retire the old instruction from active use. Do not assume an attachment in a later email automatically replaces what the operator already loaded.

Keep a founder copy of the final sheet available during rehearsal. It should contain the agreed sequence, not unrelated pitch notes or a long production checklist. The operator needs enough detail to execute one cue cleanly.
