Keep presenter notes private by assigning the audience picture and the founder’s controls to separate surfaces, then testing every transition from the recipient’s view. A presenter-view setting alone is not proof of privacy. Entering full screen, switching to the product and ending the slideshow can change what the investor sees.

## Draw the two views before opening the meeting

Identify what belongs in the audience view: the current slide, the intended product window and any approved video. Identify what belongs only to the presenter: notes, next-slide preview, timing controls and private reminders.

Treat the video-call preview as a third view rather than confusing it with either of the others. A preview may show what you selected, but the recipient remains the best place to verify the delivered picture.

If you also connect to a room projector, add that display explicitly. The remote attendees and the room may receive different surfaces. Correct remote sharing does not establish that the projector is showing the same thing.

Write a simple assignment before rehearsal: “Laptop: private controls. External display: audience slideshow. Call: selected audience surface.” Replace those labels with the actual device and window names once connected. Do not rely on monitor numbering that you have not checked on this setup.

## Configure the application's separate presenter surface

[Microsoft’s Presenter View instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/training/use-presenter-view-in-powerpoint) describe notes alongside slide previews and selecting the monitor for that view. Confirm which display receives those controls before sharing or projecting anything sensitive.

For a windowed Keynote presentation, [Apple’s remote-presentation guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102025) describes a separate presenter window and warns that some conferencing apps still show it when it occupies a shared screen area. Moving the notes window onto the audience surface can therefore defeat the intended separation.

Use the relevant application settings, but verify the result in your actual meeting software. A checkbox is part of the setup, not an assurance that all capture methods exclude private windows.

Prepare harmless test notes before rehearsal. Use a clear marker such as “PRIVATE REHEARSAL NOTE” instead of real investor comments. If the test fails, you can identify the exposure without distributing confidential text just to prove that a setting is wrong.

## Build a display-assignment and transition map

This original map connects the physical displays, application windows and shared surface. Complete it after connecting the actual monitor or projector, because that is when the relevant arrangement becomes observable.

| State | Founder sees | Audience should see | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before sharing | Notes and meeting controls | Meeting view only | Recipient confirms no desktop |
| Slideshow active | Presenter surface | Current slide only | No private test marker |
| Product handoff | Product controls and private notes | Intended product window | Correct account and task visible |
| Return to slides | Presenter controls restored | Correct current slide | No next-slide or notes exposure |
| Slideshow ended | Editing application | Agreed neutral view or stopped share | No notes or editing canvas |
| Display reconnected | Reassigned windows if changed | Nothing until rechecked | Repeat audience confirmation |

A useful diagram in your private notes is only three arrows: slide window to audience surface, notes window to founder surface, audience surface to the chosen share target. Add the projector as a separate destination if present.

Do not assume that moving a window visually changes what an application-specific share captures. Conversely, do not assume a whole-display share follows only the window you meant to show. Record the selected capture type and inspect its actual behavior.

## Rehearse the product handoff with a second viewer

Join the rehearsal from another authorized device or ask a colleague to observe as a recipient. Keep its microphone and speakers arranged so the test does not create feedback. The purpose is to see the audience picture, not to judge your speaking style.

Start the share, enter presentation mode and read one slide. Ask the viewer whether they can see the private test marker, next-slide preview or editing controls. Give them concrete things to look for rather than asking whether the screen seems fine.

Switch to the product exactly as you will during the investor discussion. Open the intended panel, return to the slide and end the slideshow. Record which transitions change the shared picture and whether a pause or explicit share-target change is necessary.

If the audience sees the wrong surface, stop the share before rearranging sensitive windows. Correct the assignment and repeat the complete transition. A passing still image after the repair does not establish that the handoff itself is now clean.

## Handle limited-screen setups deliberately

A founder with one display may still be able to use separate application windows, but there is less physical separation between notes and audience content. Keep the chosen share target explicit and avoid spreading private windows underneath a whole-screen share.

Consider using a small printed cue card or a separate private device for brief reminders if that makes the setup simpler. This is a presentation choice, not a requirement to buy additional equipment. Test whether you can still operate the product naturally.

If the meeting requires whole-screen sharing for a necessary interaction, remove private notes from that surface before starting. A clean audience desktop is easier to reason about than several overlapping windows whose capture behavior you have not tested.

Plan for a display disconnect or application restart. Those events can alter window positions or end presentation mode. The safe operational response is to stop showing content, restore the intended arrangement and verify it again before resuming—not to keep narrating while guessing what remains visible.

## Freeze the arrangement for the investor session

Save the final map with the application mode, display arrangement and share target you tested. Keep it short enough to reconstruct during setup. A screenshot of harmless rehearsal windows can supplement the written labels, but should not contain real private notes.

Before the actual meeting, connect the same displays and repeat the initial audience check. If the environment has changed, repeat the affected transitions. Do not treat a successful rehearsal on a different laptop layout as proof of this one.

At the end of the demonstration, stop sharing before returning to private notes or unrelated work. Confirm the meeting state before opening any follow-up material. Ending the slideshow and ending the share are distinct actions in many presentation workflows; rehearse the sequence you need.
