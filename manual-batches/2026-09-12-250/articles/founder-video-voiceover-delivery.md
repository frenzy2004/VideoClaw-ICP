Record a natural founder voiceover by speaking to one imagined listener, dividing the script into complete thoughts, and choosing emphasis that explains the meaning. Rehearse aloud, record contrasting takes, and match pauses to the visuals. Keep a short pickup list so you can repair weak passages without flattening the whole performance.

## Choose a listener and a reason to speak

Before recording, describe who needs this explanation and what they already know. “A facilities manager who understands missed maintenance requests but has never seen our product” gives you a clearer speaking task than “the internet.” Imagine answering that person’s practical question.

Choose an intention for each passage: explain, acknowledge, contrast, or invite. These are performance directions, not emotions you must exaggerate. A founder explaining a design compromise might sound thoughtful. A founder announcing an available feature might sound direct. Neither needs an artificial commercial-announcer voice.

Read the first passage once without recording, then say its meaning in your own words. Notice which words receive natural emphasis. Return to the approved text and carry that intention into the read. If the script requires phrasing you would never say, propose a small wording change before the recording is treated as final.

Keep the distinction between script content and delivery clear. You may need to preserve an approved product limitation exactly while still saying it conversationally. Natural delivery is not permission to drop a qualification, soften a warning, or imply a stronger product claim.

## Mark thought groups, not every syllable

A thought group is a short unit of meaning that a listener can follow before the next idea begins. Mark boundaries where the idea changes, not simply wherever a line happens to wrap on the page. Keep connected phrases together so the sentence still sounds like speech.

[Descript’s guide to reading naturally](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/learn-to-read-without-sounding-like-youre-reading) recommends reading aloud and marking delivery cues, including emphasis and pauses. Use a minimal annotation system: a slash for a short pause, a double slash for a full thought break, and a note identifying the word that carries the contrast.

Do not mark every word. An over-annotated page creates another task to perform while speaking. If a sentence needs five different instructions, simplify the sentence or split it into two complete thoughts.

Read the passage with one emphasis choice, then another. Emphasizing “you” in “You choose the owner” identifies who makes the choice; emphasizing “owner” identifies what is being chosen. In your working copy, underline the contrast word. Listen for whether the audience would understand the distinction without seeing the annotation.

## Build an annotated passage with visual cues

Here is an original fictional voiceover passage for a founder introducing a maintenance-request product. The marks are instructions for the performer, not words to read aloud. The product behavior is invented for this exercise and is not a claim about any real tool.

“Every request starts with a person / who needs something fixed. // At first, that request may arrive as an email or a conversation. / The problem comes when nobody knows who owns the next step. // In this example, the site manager assigns an owner. / The owner sees the request and records what happens next. // We are not diagnosing the equipment. / We are making the handoff visible. // Let’s follow one request from the initial note / to an assigned owner.”

Use this cue sheet beside the passage:

| Thought | Emphasis | Visual cue | Delivery check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A person needs help | Person | Request appears | Sound specific, not grandiose |
| Ownership is unclear | Who owns | Two possible recipients | Let the question register |
| Assignment occurs | Assigns | Owner field changes | Name the action when it is visible |
| Product boundary | Not diagnosing | Hold the request view | Keep the qualification audible |
| Walkthrough begins | One request | Return to starting state | Invite attention without rushing |

The sheet links meaning to the picture. It also shows which pauses exist for comprehension and which allow the viewer to inspect a screen change.

## Record two genuinely different attempts

Give each attempt a purpose. In the first, prioritize clear explanation to the imagined listener. In the second, keep the same factual wording but make the transitions more conversational. Do not merely repeat the passage while hoping something improves.

[Descript’s script-reading advice](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-read-a-script-like-a-pro-while-recording) describes recording in segments as a way to reset and adjust delivery. Use complete thoughts as your segments. Leave a little space before and after each so the editor can join them without cutting into a word or breath.

Listen to the two attempts without watching the waveform or reading the text. Write one concrete observation for each: “The ownership contrast is clear,” “The limitation sounds like an afterthought,” or “The invitation is rushed.” Avoid a vague preference such as “take two has better energy.”

Keep the take that makes the intended meaning easiest to follow. A slightly imperfect but understandable sentence may be preferable to a polished read with the wrong emphasis. If both attempts share the same weakness, change the direction or annotation before recording again. More identical takes will not resolve an unclear performance choice.

## Fit narration to the visuals without racing

Place the chosen narration against the actual sequence. At each cue, ask whether the viewer sees the relevant object when you name it and has enough time to understand the change. A natural voiceover can still feel confusing if it describes an action before the action appears.

In the exercise, the phrase about assigning an owner should coincide with the owner field becoming clear. If the cursor takes longer than expected to reach the field, consider shortening that visual action or moving the pause. Do not accelerate the whole narration to compensate for one slow screen movement.

Distinguish picture timing problems from performance problems. If a complicated screen is visible only briefly, extending the shot may solve the issue. If the voice groups two different ideas into one rushed phrase, rerecording that thought may be better. Avoid changing both at once without knowing which problem you are solving.

Review the product-boundary sentence especially carefully. Qualifications should not disappear under music, overlap a distracting transition, or be spoken so quickly they become inaudible. Finish this pass by listening with the picture hidden, then watching with the narration restored. Both meaning and synchronization should remain clear.

## Make a pickup list and preserve the conversational read

A pickup is a replacement recording for a specific passage. Keep the list narrow: passage identifier, first and last words, problem, new direction, and the adjacent thought needed for a natural join. This avoids rerecording an entire video to fix one weak transition.

For the fictional passage, the list might contain: “P4, from ‘We are not’ through ‘handoff visible’; limitation is too quiet; keep the contrast firm but matter-of-fact; include the preceding sentence about recording the next action.” Record that surrounding context even if only the middle passage will be used.

Match your speaking position and general delivery to the selected take. Listen to the sentence before the replacement before you begin. The goal is continuity, not a standalone performance that sounds dramatically more energetic than everything around it.

After the edit, listen at ordinary playback speed from the beginning. Check for abrupt changes in tone, missing breaths that make the speech feel cramped, and edits that alter meaning. Save the annotated script, selected takes, and completed pickup list together. Your final handoff should tell the editor which performance choices are deliberate, including pauses that must remain for the visuals to make sense.
