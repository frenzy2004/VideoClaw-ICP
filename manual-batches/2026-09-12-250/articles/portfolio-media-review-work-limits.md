A portfolio review bottleneck improves when the team controls what enters review, makes blocked decisions visible and finishes existing work before admitting more. Define a review item as a specific company decision on a specific version. Set a provisional work limit from actual reviewer availability, then adjust it using observed flow rather than hoped-for production speed.

## Find the queue that is actually stuck

A large content backlog is not automatically a review bottleneck. Separate work awaiting a company decision from work awaiting editing, missing source material or a platform distribution slot. If all those states are called “in progress,” the team may ask editors to work faster when the real constraint is an unavailable approver.

Inspect a small set of unfinished items. For each, ask what decision would move it forward and who can make that decision. “Feedback needed” is not specific enough. “Company must confirm whether the quoted customer result can remain in version three” identifies a decision. “Editor should improve the pacing” identifies production work instead.

[Atlassian's marketing service template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/templates/marketing-service-management) includes tracking intended to reveal bottlenecks. For a portfolio team, the useful view is the decision queue across companies, not simply a count of assets in production.

A reviewer may have authority over one company's facts but none over another's. Therefore, count available decisions by their required reviewer, not as one interchangeable pool of review hours.

## Define entry and exit for review

An item enters review when the named reviewer receives the exact version and a bounded request. Specify whether the reviewer is checking factual accuracy, approving a quotation, choosing between alternatives or accepting the final release. Different questions may need different people.

An item leaves the queue when that decision is recorded and the next action is clear. Comments alone do not necessarily finish a review. “Looks mostly fine” may leave the editor uncertain about an unresolved claim. Ask the reviewer to distinguish accepted content from changes that must happen before release.

The [Kanban Guide](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/) defines work in progress between a workflow's start and finish and requires explicit controls. Here, those boundaries prevent review work from disappearing whenever someone sends a reminder or opens a new file version.

Keep an item visible if it is blocked. Removing it from the board may make the limit look healthy while the commitment still exists. Show the blocker and decide whether to pause the commitment formally, reduce its scope or obtain the required decision.

## Set a provisional limit by reviewer availability

Choose a limit small enough that the people responsible can actively manage the queue. Base the first choice on their real availability and the complexity of the decisions, not a general rule about how many assets an editor can produce.

A reviewer who can assess two short factual changes may not be able to assess two long interviews in the same window. You can separate simple factual checks from substantial narrative decisions, provided the categories remain understandable. Avoid assigning artificial point values unless the team can apply them consistently.

Use the limit as a conversation rule: when the queue is full, a new item cannot enter without identifying what will finish, move or be explicitly displaced. The rule does not forbid urgent corrections. It makes their consequences visible.

In the first cycle, record what made items wait. Was the packet unclear, the reviewer unavailable, the question outside their authority or the draft still changing? Changing the limit will not solve every cause. Sometimes a better review packet is the smallest useful intervention.

## Walk through a fictional board

Suppose a platform sets a provisional limit of three active company-review items for one coordinating editor. This is a fictional teaching example, not a capacity recommendation.

| Item | Current decision | State | Next useful action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder profile v2 | Confirm revised product description | Active | Company contact answers two factual questions |
| Beacon interview v4 | Accept the selected customer quotation | Blocked | Customer permission confirmation needed |
| Cobalt event intro v1 | Approve founder role and company name | Active | Company approver checks the supplied wording |
| Delta feature draft | Choose a central audience | Not admitted | Wait for a slot or use office hours |
| Ember correction | Remove an outdated published claim | New urgent request | Assess whether an interruption is required |

The editor does not start Delta merely because its draft is available. First, Cobalt may be resolved through one precise question. Beacon needs a permission confirmation rather than another editorial pass. Alder needs factual answers, not a redesigned profile.

If Ember requires an interruption, record which active item is paused and notify its company. The board should still show the total outstanding commitments. Moving Beacon into a hidden folder would not create genuine capacity.

After Cobalt's decision is recorded, Delta can enter with an appropriately bounded audience question. The point is to finish decisions, not keep all five documents visibly busy.

## Reduce avoidable review work

Prepare a decision packet that lets the reviewer understand the change without reconstructing the whole project. Include the version, the requested decision, the changed passage or relevant timestamp and the deadline reason. Link to supporting material where it is necessary.

For a returning review, distinguish previously accepted material from new material. Ask the reviewer to focus on the changed facts or unresolved questions. If the story has substantially changed, say so; a narrow review label should not conceal a complete rewrite.

Avoid sending different reviewers competing versions at the same time. When one company contact asks for a factual correction, identify whether that change affects another reviewer's decision before circulating a new release candidate.

Create a separate route for questions the named reviewer cannot answer. A founder may know the product but not have authority to clear a customer quotation. The coordinator should identify that dependency rather than repeatedly reminding the wrong person.

Some review queues shrink when the team stops asking for unnecessary decisions. Do not require every company contact to review an internal file label that cannot affect the public result. Retain the checks that change the outcome.

## Use queue observations to improve the next cycle

Review a few completed and blocked items with the people who handled them. Compare active review time with elapsed waiting time where that distinction can be observed reliably. Do not turn incomplete time records into precise productivity claims.

Look for recurring causes that have a specific remedy. An unnamed substitute approver suggests a continuity problem. Repeated factual changes suggest the source record was not settled. A flood of last-minute requests may indicate that the release calendar is invisible to companies.

Keep the work limit provisional until the team has useful experience. Raise it only when reviewers can handle the additional active decisions without making existing work age unnecessarily. Lower it when too many simultaneous items make priorities unclear.

Describe changes in plain language: “We will admit final reviews only after the company confirms the source facts,” or “Each company will nominate a backup for dated event assets.” A small explicit policy is more useful than a dashboard that records a growing queue without changing how work enters it.
