Track expiring portfolio media by recording the dates and use restrictions already confirmed by the company or rights owner, linking them to affected assets and scheduling the required operational actions. Keep uncertain terms unresolved. An expiry calendar implements supplied instructions; it does not determine whether a licence permits a use or create permission to renew it.

## Begin with confirmed instructions

Collect the instructions that accompany the asset: the permitted context, any ending date, the contact responsible for clarification and the source of the instruction. A date in a filename is not sufficient evidence of a use restriction. Nor should a coordinator infer a licence period from the date an image was purchased.

Ask the company or rights owner to resolve ambiguous wording before scheduling an action that depends on it. The operator can flag the question and identify the affected media. Interpretation of an agreement belongs with the people authorized to provide it.

Keep the operational instruction short and traceable. “Remove from the public resource portal at the supplied ending time” is actionable when its source is clear. “Rights expire soon” does not tell the team which use ends or what should happen next.

Do not assume every asset needs the same treatment. A stock image, a customer interview and an event photograph may arrive with different instructions. Your calendar should preserve those differences rather than flattening them into one global expiry policy.

## Connect the instruction to actual assets

An instruction becomes useful when the team can identify every affected asset and destination. Record the source media, relevant versions and known derivatives. A licensed image might appear inside a video, in its thumbnail and on the article page promoting it.

For each destination, identify who can change it. The platform may control its own portal but need a company or partner to update another page. That distinction affects the follow-up required when a use period ends.

[Canto's expiry documentation](https://support.canto.com/hc/en-us/articles/23001802813841-Set-Expiration-Dates) describes a portal-level expiry behavior. It is a useful example of a system action, but a setting inside one library should not be assumed to remove copies already published elsewhere.

Include the supplied time zone when the instruction includes a time. Ask for clarification if a date-only instruction requires an operational cutoff. Do not silently choose a time zone based on the coordinator's location.

Separate the final action date from the earlier review reminder. A reminder creates time to seek clarification or prepare a replacement; it does not extend the permitted use.

## Build an expiry calendar

The following example is fictional. Dates and actions would come from the actual company's confirmed instructions.

| Asset | Supplied instruction | Affected destinations | Review action | Ending action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder interview excerpt | Use ends on the company's specified date | Fund resource page and company link | Ask whether replacement is planned | Remove or replace as instructed |
| Beacon campaign image | Limited to the named campaign | Campaign page and thumbnail | Identify a cleared replacement | Update affected placements |
| Cobalt event recording | Company asks for review before further reuse | Internal library | Request updated direction | Hold new distribution pending response |

Notice that Cobalt's instruction is a review requirement, not necessarily an expiry date. The calendar should not invent a deletion event. It can show a hold on additional use while the appropriate person clarifies the next step.

Add the source instruction, responsible contact, time zone, reminder date and completion evidence to the working record. Keep a field for unknown destinations. That uncertainty matters if the asset has been downloaded or shared outside the controlled library.

Use a stable asset reference so renamed files do not disappear from the calendar.

## Decide among renewal, replacement and retirement

Before the ending date, ask whether the company wants the asset to remain in use. If it does, identify who can supply renewed or changed instructions. An unanswered request does not renew anything.

Prepare a replacement when continued use is important but the original asset may become unavailable. Check whether replacing one component affects the rest of the media. Changing an image inside a video can require a new export, updated captions if wording changes and another company review.

Retirement can mean removing the asset from active distribution while preserving an appropriate internal record. It does not automatically mean deleting all files. Follow the organization's actual retention and recordkeeping instructions rather than inventing a universal period.

[Bynder's archiving guidance](https://support.bynder.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013931059-How-To-Mark-Assets-As-Archived) distinguishes archived access from normal asset availability. This illustrates why archive status and public use are separate operating concepts. Your chosen system may handle them differently.

Record the decision before performing it. A replacement plan should identify the new asset and the destinations to update, not simply say “swap creative.”

## Verify the action at each destination

At the ending point, check what actually changed. Confirm whether the old asset is unavailable through the normal route, whether the replacement opens and whether descriptions or thumbnails still reference the retired material.

For destinations controlled by another party, request confirmation and record its status. Mark an unresolved external copy honestly. Do not report a complete withdrawal merely because the platform's own portal was updated.

Check direct links as well as navigation. Removing an asset from a collection may leave its URL accessible, depending on the system. The operational action should match the supplied instruction and the available controls.

Keep evidence proportionate to the task: a destination record, a confirmation message or a dated check may be enough. Avoid retaining unnecessary personal or sensitive information merely to prove that an update happened.

If the action cannot be completed as planned, contact the responsible owner with the specific limitation. A clear unresolved exception is more useful than a green status based on partial work.

## Maintain the calendar as media changes

New derivatives, renamed campaigns and moved pages can make an old expiry record incomplete. Connect the calendar to the release process so new uses inherit the relevant source instructions and destination references.

Review upcoming actions with the people who can perform them. An asset requiring a revised video export needs more preparation than a simple portal removal. Use observed dependencies to plan the work rather than assigning the same lead time to every item.

After each cycle, examine missed reminders and unresolved uses. Determine whether the issue was an unclear instruction, a missing destination, an unavailable owner or a system limitation. Change the specific part of the process that caused the problem.

Keep the distinction between facts and decisions visible. The source provides the instruction; the calendar records the team's planned implementation; the destination check shows what actually happened.

That separation makes the calendar useful across many companies without turning the platform into an interpreter of every media agreement. The practical goal is to carry confirmed instructions through to real, verifiable actions.
