Republish a portfolio article only within an already-authorized scope, using a written instruction sheet for source attribution, allowed edits, update responsibility and technical indexing review. Keep the original page and approved copy connected. Clear attribution helps readers understand provenance, but it does not itself grant permission or guarantee how search engines handle duplicate content.

## Confirm the approved copy and scope

Start with the authorization already obtained by the responsible people. Record the source article, approved revision, receiving publisher and permitted use. If permission is missing or unclear, stop this workflow and ask the appropriate owner to resolve it rather than drafting your own licence terms.

Distinguish a full copy, an excerpt, a translation and a substantially edited adaptation. Approval for one does not automatically cover the others. Identify which form the receiving publisher will use and whether images, diagrams and quotations are included in the approved package.

Keep the source URL separate from the production file. A shared document may contain the approved text, while the original published article lives elsewhere. The receiving editor needs both references so the published copy can point readers to the maintained source.

Record any restrictions or required instructions as supplied, without reinterpreting them. A media coordinator can carry a confirmed attribution line into production; questions about rights, contract scope or legal obligations belong with the responsible owner or advisers.

## Write the attribution and editing instructions

Specify the exact source credit the parties have approved, the link destination and the intended placement. The reader should be able to recognize that the article originated elsewhere without searching through an unrelated footer.

List permitted edits concretely. Formatting for the receiving site's layout may be allowed while changing quotations or product claims requires review. If the receiving editor wants a different headline, ask whether that change falls within the approved scope and who checks the resulting framing.

Separate article text from attached media. A photograph may have its own supplied credit or use instruction. Do not assume that permission to republish the article includes every image visible on the original page. Use the approved package and keep missing media questions visible.

Explain how publication dates and update notes should appear. A republished historical article should not look like a new account merely because the receiving site uses today's date. Preserve the relevant original context in the agreed presentation.

## Populate a republication instruction sheet

Consider a fictional operator article about preparing a company media package. The company has authorized a full copy on the portfolio program's resource site, subject to the recorded instructions.

| Field | Filled instruction for this fictional copy | Check owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Company article ART-18, approved revision 2, maintained source URL recorded | Company editor |
| Receiving destination | Program resource article REP-06 | Platform editor |
| Visible attribution | Approved line names the company publication and links to ART-18 | Platform editor |
| Allowed edits | Layout and heading styling only; factual text changes return for review | Company editor |
| Media | One approved diagram; original article photographs excluded | Media coordinator |
| Updates | Company editor sends corrections; platform editor records applied changes | Both editors |
| Indexing | Site owners review the intended treatment before release | Technical publishing owners |

The actual sheet should contain the exact approved attribution wording and real destination URLs. This illustrative table uses record references so it does not invent a real publisher relationship.

Suppose the platform editor wants to shorten a paragraph that explains an important limitation. The instruction sheet directs that change back to the company editor. It does not allow a meaning-changing edit to pass as formatting.

## Separate visible credit from indexing controls

A source link is useful to readers, but it is not the same as a technical instruction to a search engine. [Google's canonical documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) describes methods for expressing a preferred URL among duplicate or similar pages. The receiving site's owner should decide the appropriate implementation for its situation.

Cross-site syndication needs particular care. [Google's canonicalization troubleshooting guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting) says canonical links are not its recommended way to avoid syndication duplication and points to partner indexing controls. Do not turn “add a canonical” into a universal republication rule.

Record the intended indexing outcome as a question for the technical owners: should the receiving copy be eligible for Search, and how will the agreed treatment be checked? Blocking indexing affects discovery, so it should be an explicit publishing decision rather than an editor's unreviewed workaround.

Do not promise that the source will rank first, retain all traffic or receive a particular search benefit. Even a correctly implemented preference does not establish a guaranteed display outcome. The article's operational deliverable is an accurate handoff, not an SEO performance assurance.

## Review the actual receiving page

Compare the receiving preview with the approved revision. Check body text, headline, dates, source credit, links and media. A copy can preserve every paragraph while introducing misleading meaning through a new heading or image caption.

Test the visible source link and confirm that it reaches the intended article, not a homepage or an outdated duplicate. Check the receiving page's own links as well; copied relative links may lead to different destinations on another site. Ask the technical owner to verify the agreed indexing treatment independently.

Keep the approval reference attached to the preview version. If the receiving site changes the page after review, identify whether the change needs another check. Do not treat approval of a document as proof that the final published page matches it.

For REP-06, the media coordinator confirms that the excluded photographs are absent and the approved diagram carries its supplied description. The company editor checks that the limitation paragraph remains intact. These are specific completion tests, not a general “content approved” checkbox.

## Maintain the relationship after publication

Record the live receiving URL and date checked. Add it to the source article's republication register so future corrections have a known destination. If a second receiving publisher is authorized later, create a separate row with its own scope and owner.

When the source changes, classify whether the change affects the republished copy. A corrected factual statement should be routed promptly through the agreed process. A new section added to the source may require a separate decision about whether the copy should incorporate it.

Track the correction request and observed result. “Sent update” is not the same as “receiving page checked.” If the partner cannot be reached or the instructions are disputed, keep the issue open and escalate through the responsible relationship owner.

The finished work product is a copy-specific instruction sheet and maintained destination record. It keeps authorization, attribution, editing, indexing and updates connected without pretending that any one of those elements automatically resolves the others.
