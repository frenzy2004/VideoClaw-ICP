Host a founder AMA by offering a specific area of firsthand knowledge, agreeing on community rules, and making room for difficult as well as friendly questions. Prepare a way to route uncertainty and private account issues. The useful outcome is a clearer conversation, not a rehearsed pitch disguised as participation.

## Offer expertise a community can use

Start with a sentence that explains why this founder can answer these questions. A funding announcement establishes a company event, not expertise about every startup problem. “I build tools for maintenance coordinators and have been investigating contractor handoffs” offers a more concrete basis for discussion.

Choose the community because that experience intersects with its interests. Read its recent conversations and current participation rules. Do not infer permission from seeing another founder’s old appearance.

[Reddit’s AMA hosting guidance](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002427523-What-is-an-AMA-and-how-do-I-host-one) directs prospective hosts to community moderators and emphasizes authentic participation across varied questions. Its rules apply to Reddit; other communities and video platforms need their own checks.

An AMA can be written or spoken. Decide that before preparing assets. A live video session needs a visible question queue and someone watching the chat; a written thread needs clear replies attached to the questions. Neither requires a slide deck about your funding round.

## Fill a facilitation sheet with actual boundaries

The following sheet is an original fictional example for Fieldnote’s founder, Priya. It is a planning artifact, not an account of a real AMA.

**Audience:** Facilities coordinators comparing ways to hand repair work to outside contractors.

**Firsthand expertise:** Designing Fieldnote’s assignment workflow and hearing customer explanations of incomplete handoffs.

**Not covered:** Individual customer disputes, legal advice, or unsupported claims about competing products.

**Opening introduction:** Priya identifies her role, explains the workflow problem she works on, and invites questions about the choices and limitations involved.

**Format:** A moderator-approved written session, with an announced start, end, and time zone.

**People:** Priya writes her own answers. A teammate tracks unresolved questions and locates already-approved factual material. A community moderator handles rule enforcement.

**Close:** A final update separates answered questions, accepted follow-ups, and topics that cannot be addressed publicly.

Confirm the moderator’s current verification process rather than improvising a public identity-document upload. [Reddit’s community-event guidance](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/15484368382612-Hosting-events-large-and-small) discusses verification, moderator help, and clear scheduling. Follow the specific community’s instructions for what is required and where it belongs.

## Prepare answer ingredients, not a performance

Keep a small reference sheet with current product boundaries, public documentation, and examples you are permitted to discuss. This prevents searching through internal messages while a question waits. It is not a library of canned answers to paste regardless of context.

Prepare to distinguish four kinds of statements:

- What you observed directly.
- What the company currently does.
- What you infer from limited experience.
- What you do not yet know.

For example, Priya can describe why the fictional team separates an assignment’s location from its responsible contact. She cannot claim that every facilities business needs the same setup. A useful answer names the conditions under which her approach might fail.

Rehearse the mechanics separately from the content. Confirm who can post, how questions reach the speaker, and how the end of the session will be marked. If hosting on video, do a short microphone and screen check. Keep the current question near the camera without requiring the founder to read a prepared response word for word.

A [first media-interview preparation sheet](/blog/first-founder-media-interview-preparation) can help organize verified facts, but an AMA requires answering the community’s actual questions rather than steering every exchange toward a message.

## Route real questions without hiding the hard ones

Use a simple routing sheet during the session. Record the question, its route, and the response status. The following examples illustrate decisions; they are not questions claimed to come from a real community.

**Practical question:** “How do you decide what information the contractor receives?” Route it to a direct answer. Explain the decision criteria and one limitation, using only verified current behavior.

**Critical question:** “Doesn’t another tool already handle this?” Route it to a substantive comparison of the problem. Acknowledge that existing tools may work well, then explain the particular situation your team is addressing. Avoid unsupported superiority claims.

**Ambiguous question:** “Why is assignment broken?” Ask what the person means. They may be describing your product, their process, or a different system. Do not defend the company before establishing the question.

**Private account issue:** “Here is our customer list; which permissions should we change?” Ask the person not to expose further account information and direct them to the appropriate private support route. Do not diagnose sensitive details in public.

**Unknown:** “Will you support our approval structure next quarter?” State what is known now and whether the team can investigate. Do not turn interest into an unapproved release commitment.

Track skipped questions for a second pass. Favoring only friendly prompts can happen accidentally when the founder reads the easiest answers first.

## Replace promotional answers with useful reasoning

Compare two fictional responses to a question about small teams.

Promotional answer: “Fieldnote is the best choice for modern facilities teams. Book a call and we’ll show you how much time it saves.”

That does not explain the decision and invents an outcome the example has not established.

Useful answer: “If one person coordinates all repairs and everyone already sees the same information, a shared list may be enough. The problem we are working on appears when an assignment crosses an organizational boundary and the next person lacks context. I would first map that handoff before deciding whether another tool is necessary.”

The second response contributes a decision rule that remains useful without a purchase. The founder should formulate the live answer personally in response to the actual question and follow the host community’s authorship rules.

Do not have teammates pose as unrelated participants or manufacture favorable questions. If a moderator uses an opening prompt to start discussion, its origin should be clear. Preparation should make genuine conversation easier, not create a fictional audience.

## Close with an accurate account of what remains

Before the scheduled end, identify any commitments the founder has actually accepted. Replace “we’ll get back to everyone” with specific, achievable follow-ups.

Priya’s fictional closing note might say: “Thank you for the questions about handoff context. I answered the workflow questions I could address from direct experience. I will check whether our documentation explains the difference between location and responsibility. I cannot discuss individual accounts here, and I have not committed to a new approval feature.”

Update a promised answer in the agreed place when it is ready. If the investigation remains unresolved, say that rather than quietly letting the commitment expire. Preserve the original question’s meaning when summarizing it internally.

Recurring strategic input may belong in a [customer advisory board invitation](/blog/startup-customer-advisory-board-invitation), but participation in an AMA is not consent to join one.
