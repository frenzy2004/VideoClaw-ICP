Choose individual or team editing licenses by mapping people to the tasks and controls they need. Editing access, review access, billing administration, and company asset continuity are different requirements. Buy confirmed entitlements for named users, then test what happens when an editor joins, changes role, or leaves the business.

## Count people and tasks, not computers

List everyone involved in production and what they actually do. One employee may edit daily, another may open projects occasionally, a founder may only review exports, and an operations colleague may manage payment.

Do not begin with the number of laptops or assume a single shared workstation means a single shared identity is acceptable. [Adobe's account-sharing policy](https://helpx.adobe.com/download-install/apps/licensing-activation/activate-deactivate-apps/account-sharing-policy.html) states that both individual and team accounts are for the assigned person and must not be shared.

That is a provider-specific policy to respect in the proposed arrangement. Other software may have different licensing models, so compare the applicable terms rather than importing assumptions from one product.

Ask each person for the smallest set of capabilities they need. “Collaborates on video” is not enough to determine whether someone must edit a timeline, leave comments, download an approved master, or administer subscriptions.

## Build the person-to-entitlement map

Use this original worksheet before requesting a quote. Replace role labels with named people or approved vacancies, and keep uncertain access types explicit.

| Person or role | Required action | Candidate entitlement | Question to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary editor | Create and export projects | Named editing user | Which applications and services are included? |
| Occasional editor | Make authorized timeline changes | Separate editing access | What commitment fits intermittent use? |
| Business reviewer | Comment on a review copy | Review or guest access if supported | Can they comment without editing rights? |
| Finance administrator | Manage invoices and payment | Billing role | Does this require a paid creative seat? |
| Technical administrator | Assign access and manage departures | Appropriate admin role | Can this be separated from daily editing? |
| Replacement editor | Resume a company project | Reassigned or new entitlement | What happens to the previous user's assets? |

Add the start date, expected duration, business asset location, and person authorizing purchase. The map should distinguish an entitlement already held from one merely assumed to be included.

Include contractors who already hold their own subscriptions in the discussion. Ask whether the company needs to provide any additional entitlement or only project access, and verify that answer against the proposed collaboration method.

Reviewers do not automatically need full editing access, but do not promise free guest access without checking the actual product and plan. Test the intended review action using the supported role before buying unnecessary seats.

## Compare administration as a purchased capability

The difference between individual and team offerings may include more than application features. Ask how the business assigns products, sees unused entitlements, manages billing, and handles changes in personnel.

[Adobe's team license instructions](https://helpx.adobe.com/business/teams/users-and-licenses/manage-users/assign-licenses.html) describe assigning products to named users through its administration tools. That is a concrete management capability to compare with separately reimbursing individual subscriptions.

Ask who controls the organization and who can make purchases. An editor should not have to become the sole billing administrator simply because they installed the software first. Likewise, administrative access should not be granted broadly just to make review invitations easier.

Compare the precise offer available to your business, including eligibility and purchasing route. A feature described for one enterprise or direct-purchase plan may not apply to every reseller agreement or individual subscription.

## Test departure and reassignment separately from file delivery

Imagine the current editor leaves and another person must continue the work. Ask which license can be reassigned, who performs the change, and whether there are conditions or timing restrictions.

[Adobe's team-management documentation](https://helpx.adobe.com/business/teams/users-and-licenses/manage-users/manage-team-in-the-creative-cloud-desktop-app.html) describes assigning, removing, and reassigning licenses, while pointing separately to recovery of a removed user's assets. That separation matters: changing an entitlement is not the same as handing over a usable project.

Ask where company projects, source media, shared libraries, and cloud documents live. Identify which are covered by the plan's administration features and which remain on a local drive or in another service.

Do not casually remove a real user to test the process. Request a vendor demonstration or use an authorized test account and disposable project. Record what the business can recover, what requires user cooperation, and what has to be delivered through a separate handoff.

## Compare the complete offer without inventing seat economics

Obtain actual prices and terms for the mapped users. Include the commitment period, renewal conditions, taxes where applicable, storage or service limits, support, and any add-ons the workflow requires.

A lower displayed monthly figure does not necessarily mean a lower commitment, and an unused entitlement is not automatically refundable. Ask what happens when the team shrinks or a project ends before the contract does.

Keep capability value separate from arithmetic. Central administration might be useful because staff changes are frequent; it may add little for a stable single-person business with simple local files. State the actual problem it solves instead of assuming a team plan is always the mature choice.

If you model hypothetical growth, label the scenario and use reader-entered quotes. Do not invent a vendor discount, price, or break-even seat count. Show which assumptions would change the decision, such as another permanent editor joining or review access requiring an additional paid role.

## Approve a small, maintainable licensing arrangement

The final purchase record should name the plan, assigned users, required capabilities, administrators, commitment dates, and asset-continuity method. Attach the entitlement map and the vendor answers that resolved uncertain rows.

Before committing broadly, verify that the primary editor can work and the reviewer can complete the intended review without borrowing credentials. Confirm that an authorized business administrator can locate the subscription and manage access.

Schedule a review around real personnel or contract changes rather than creating an elaborate governance system. The relevant question is whether the purchased entitlements still match the people doing the work.

Choose individual licensing when it satisfies the actual arrangement and responsibilities. Choose team licensing when its confirmed controls solve specific business needs. Neither label substitutes for a clear record of who can do what and how the company keeps access to its work.
