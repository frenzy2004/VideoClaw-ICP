Fit a product clip into a fixed Demo Day slot by budgeting the whole media sequence: introduction, playback start, clip runtime, result hold, and return to the deck. Measure those transitions in rehearsal. Cut secondary footage before rushing the decisive action or making the product appear faster than it is.

## Establish the event's actual timing boundary

Confirm where the event clock starts and stops. It may cover the entire founder appearance rather than only the spoken presentation. Ask whether the introduction, AV transition, and exit are included, and whether the allotted time is a hard stop or a target.

Do not borrow a slot length from another accelerator's historical advice. Use the current event instructions. Keep that constraint separate from the amount of time your product clip happens to occupy today.

Identify who starts the movie and what action triggers it. A founder clicking a slide, an AV operator responding to a cue, and a remotely shared file each introduce different transition behavior.

The task here is media arithmetic, not rewriting the pitch story. Start with the agreed presentation sequence and calculate what the clip can occupy without swallowing the surrounding explanation. If the arithmetic does not work, make a visible editorial choice rather than assuming you will speak faster on stage.

## Write the time budget as an equation

Use this original planning equation:

Available clip time = total slot − non-demo speaking − entry transition − result hold − exit transition − contingency allowance.

Every term should have a meaning in the actual presentation. The contingency allowance is a planning choice, not a prediction of failure or an industry benchmark. Keep it small enough to be realistic and large enough to reflect the transitions you observed.

For a fictional 180-second slot, suppose the agreed non-demo speaking occupies 105 seconds. Allocate 5 seconds to enter playback, 8 seconds to hold the result, 4 seconds to return to the deck, and 8 seconds as a planning allowance. That leaves 50 seconds for the movie itself.

These are illustrative numbers, not recommended event timings. Replace them with the current slot and your measured rehearsal durations. If the movie is 70 seconds, the example has a 20-second gap that must be resolved before handover.

## Separate product time from presentation time

The movie's runtime is not necessarily the elapsed time of the product operation. A clip may contain a disclosed time jump, a narrated still, or a result hold. Track those components separately so the shortened presentation does not create a misleading speed claim.

Keep the decisive action at a pace the audience can follow. Removing an unrelated settings tour may save time without harming the evidence. Accelerating the action that establishes the result may make the demonstration shorter but less useful.

If processing time is relevant, retain enough context to explain it and keep an uncut run available. Do not remove a manual intervention and count the saved seconds as harmless dead time.

Use a simple scene ledger:

| Scene type | Keep, shorten or remove? | Timing reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting state | Keep enough to identify the task | Establishes context |
| Decisive action | Preserve inspectable playback | Carries product evidence |
| Unrelated navigation | Usually remove from this clip | Does not answer the chosen question |
| Processing interval | Disclose any compression | Separates playback from elapsed time |
| Result | Keep readable | Lets the audience inspect the outcome |

The ledger makes the cut decision about evidence, not merely duration.

## Measure the playback route in rehearsal

Use the exact deck, movie, and control arrangement planned for the event. Start the rehearsal clock at the event's defined boundary, then record the actual times at entry to the clip, first moving frame, final result, and return to the next slide.

[Keynote supports recorded presentation playback](https://support.apple.com/en-mide/guide/keynote/tan81813d552/mac), which can help inspect an assembled sequence. If you use another tool, measure the same transitions in its actual presentation mode.

For a remote demo, [Zoom's video player displays elapsed and total playback time](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0064733). Treat those controls as the movie clock, not the clock for the entire founder slot.

Run more than one rehearsal because a single clean transition can hide an awkward cue. Note why a run differs: a delayed operator response, an extra click, or the founder continuing narration after the result. Fix the sequence before allocating arbitrary extra seconds.

## Cut with an evidence-first order

First remove footage that answers a different product question. A second workflow, a menu tour, or a long account setup may be useful elsewhere but unnecessary in this slot.

Next simplify the path through the chosen task. Prepare the starting state so the clip does not spend time locating a fictional record, while preserving enough context that the preparation is not misleading. Do not imply that preconfigured work happened during the visible run.

Then shorten repeated explanations. If the founder says what the audience just watched, retain the sentence only when it adds interpretation or a necessary boundary. A result hold can be silent while the audience reads.

Use disclosed time compression only where it fits the claim. If the remaining evidence still exceeds the budget, choose a narrower task or negotiate the presentation plan with the event contact. The answer is not to hide a meaningful step or assume the audience can inspect unreadable fast motion.

## Freeze a timed cue strip for the final version

Create a short cue strip with the intended entry cue, movie runtime, result hold, and exit cue. Pair it with the exact filename and presentation version. This gives the founder and operator the same timing reference.

For the fictional budget above, the cue strip might read: “Enter movie after task introduction; play the 50-second file; hold its result for the planned explanation; return to the next slide on the agreed cue.” Replace the prose with your actual tested cues and times.

After any edit, rerun the whole segment. Adding a title card or changing a narration pause can alter the budget even if the product footage is unchanged. Do not send a revised file under an old runtime note.

Keep the timing result bounded: the sequence fits the tested slot under the rehearsed arrangement. It does not guarantee an identical live performance.
