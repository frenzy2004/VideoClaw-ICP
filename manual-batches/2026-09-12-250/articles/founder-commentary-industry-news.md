Add founder commentary to industry news only when you can connect a verified development to a customer decision through relevant firsthand knowledge. Separate the event from your interpretation, disclose uncertainty, and pass when you have nothing useful to add. A timely explanation should help people understand the change, not merely attach your startup to it.

## Ask whether this story belongs in your expertise

A newly funded founder may feel pressure to have an opinion on every industry announcement. Resist the assumption that visibility requires constant reaction. Your useful territory is the intersection of what happened, what you know directly, and what customers now need to decide.

[David Meerman Scott's newsjacking excerpt connects participation in a news story with a legitimate relationship to it](https://www.davidmeermanscott.com/hubfs/documents/Newsjacking-free-excerpt.pdf). That relationship is a starting point, not evidence that a comment will receive coverage.

Write down your connection without mentioning the funding round. “We have implemented this type of integration for our own product” is more relevant than “We recently raised capital.” The first can support a practical observation; the second establishes no particular knowledge of the event.

Also identify your boundary. You may understand integration design without knowing the announcing company's internal reasons. Do not substitute industry familiarity for access you do not have.

If your only contribution is that the news is exciting, concerning, or a game changer, pause. Those adjectives do not yet give a reader a decision aid.

## Verify the event before interpreting its implications

Read the original announcement or other primary material. Record its date, scope, availability conditions, and unresolved details. A social post summarizing the news may omit precisely the condition your commentary depends on.

Create two separate notes: what the source explicitly says and what you infer. Keep them separate throughout drafting. “The platform announced a new export option” is different from “Customers will be able to leave more easily.” The latter depends on what the export contains and how another system can use it.

[PRSA's ethics code emphasizes accuracy in public communication](https://www.prsa.org/professional-development/prsa-resources/ethics). Apply that standard before adding a confident founder voice to incomplete information.

Do not invent a customer reaction to make the story feel immediate. If you have not spoken with customers about the announcement, say what they may want to examine rather than claiming they are already demanding a solution.

If the news involves legal, financial, security, or safety questions beyond your competence, narrow the commentary to your actual area and obtain appropriate review where needed.

## Fill out a commentary decision canvas

Use six fields: verified event, direct expertise, affected customer decision, original contribution, unknowns, and relevance deadline. Complete all six before choosing a format.

For a fictional ecosystem change, imagine a platform announces a new way to export project records. The event field records only that announcement. The expertise field says your team has previously moved its own test data between systems. The customer decision is whether to evaluate the new export for a future migration.

Your original contribution might be a distinction between exporting records and preserving the relationships between them. The unknowns include which fields and attachments the announced option actually supports. The relevance deadline is when detailed documentation is expected to resolve those questions.

The publish-or-pass decision follows from those fields. Publish a clearly qualified checklist if it helps customers evaluate the documentation. Pass on declaring the end of vendor lock-in, because your evidence does not support that conclusion.

This canvas is an original editorial tool. It is not a validated predictor of press interest or audience demand.

## Write a comment that adds a usable distinction

Here is an original fictional example, not commentary on a real platform announcement:

“The announced export option is worth examining, but an export file and a usable migration are different things. From moving our own test records, the question I would ask first is whether relationships, attachments, and ownership information survive the move. The announcement does not yet answer those questions. I would compare a small representative sample with the receiving system before making a migration plan.”

The comment does four jobs: it names the development, identifies the founder's experience, adds a practical distinction, and preserves uncertainty. It does not claim knowledge of customer adoption or the announcing company's motives.

For a short founder video, place the verified event in the first spoken sentence and your contribution immediately afterward. Do not spend the opening praising your own company. If a visual helps, use an original conceptual diagram rather than an unrelated screenshot that could imply access to unreleased functionality.

Keep your company's connection visible without making it the destination of every paragraph. A disclosure of relevant commercial interest can be more credible than pretending to be a neutral observer.

## Challenge the draft before the news window closes

Ask a colleague to underline every sentence that sounds like a fact about another organization. Can you point to the original material for each one? If not, rewrite it as a clearly bounded inference or remove it.

Then ask whether the comment adds anything beyond the announcement. A paraphrase with your founder title attached is still a paraphrase. Add a useful question, trade-off, or concrete evaluation step grounded in your experience.

Check for opportunism. A tragedy, personal harm, or unresolved allegation is not a convenient stage for a promotional lesson. If the connection to your product feels forced, pass.

Finally, inspect the source again immediately before release. Fast-moving news can change while you record. If the missing documentation is now available, incorporate it instead of publishing an obsolete uncertainty as though it remains unresolved.

The deadline is not simply “before someone else comments.” It is the point at which your contribution stops being accurate or useful.

## Keep ownership of the interpretation after publishing

Save the event source and your reasoning notes with the published version. If the facts change, update the commentary or explain why the original conclusion no longer applies. A founder's responsibility does not end when the recording is uploaded.

Track the questions people ask in response, but do not treat a few reactions as proof of market-wide demand. They may reveal an unclear phrase or a useful follow-up topic. Keep that distinction explicit.

If a journalist contacts you, restate what you know firsthand and what remains an interpretation. Do not upgrade your certainty because the conversation has become an interview.
