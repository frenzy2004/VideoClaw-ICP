Match portfolio marketers for peer help by translating a broad request into one answerable operating question, finding someone with relevant experience and asking both people before making an introduction. Use a match card with a bounded purpose and private closure note. The service should resolve a practical question, not manufacture networking activity.

## Turn a broad request into a working question

A request such as “I need help with content” is difficult to match. Ask what the person is trying to decide, what they have already tried and what kind of experience would help. Keep the intake brief enough that asking for help does not become a project of its own.

For example, “How do other teams collect product-review feedback?” can become “How can one marketer obtain a single factual decision from several company reviewers before exporting a tutorial?” That narrower question points toward someone who has managed a similar workflow rather than someone with the most senior marketing title.

[Together's Colleague Connect documentation](https://help.togetherplatform.com/hc/en-us/articles/22171219164315-What-is-Colleague-Connect) describes skills- and goals-based peer matching without a required seniority gap. The useful distinction is relevant experience, not hierarchy. A recent practitioner may have a more applicable answer than an experienced executive working in a different context.

Record the desired output. The requester might want a revised review request, a sample handoff checklist or a decision about which step to simplify. That output makes it possible to close the match without requiring an ongoing mentoring relationship.

## Identify useful experience without exposing private work

Ask potential helpers what they have done and what they are comfortable discussing. A public role description may indicate a broad area of expertise, but it does not prove experience with the specific problem. Invite a short confirmation rather than assuming fit.

Keep the matching record focused on capabilities and conditions. “Has coordinated tutorial reviews with product and customer-success teams” is useful. Copying a private customer's name or an unpublished internal failure into the roster is usually unnecessary for deciding whether to introduce two people.

[First Round describes private resources and structured mentorship](https://founders.firstround.com/template-of-sheet). A portfolio peer-help service can similarly keep introductions within an agreed community, while defining its own narrower scope. This is not a claim that every network member is available or that an introduction guarantees useful advice.

Check for company overlap and sharing constraints before proposing a match. If the question would require revealing sensitive positioning to a competitor, help the requester narrow it to a generic operating problem or choose another route.

## Fill a question-to-expert match card

Consider a fictional marketer at Alder who needs to improve factual review of tutorial videos. The platform identifies three possible peers.

| Candidate | Relevant experience | Constraint | Match assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beacon content lead | Recently introduced a single decision packet for tutorial reviews | Can share a blank template, not customer footage | Strong fit for the process question |
| Cobalt demand-generation lead | Runs paid campaign experiments | Limited tutorial-review experience | Broad marketing fit, weak task fit |
| Delta product educator | Coordinates release documentation and product demos | Available next month | Useful alternative if timing permits |

The match card names the requester, question, desired output, relevant context, proposed helper and sharing boundary. It also states the requested commitment: one initial conversation and an optional follow-up only if both people want it. This is a fictional service design, not a prescribed meeting length.

The platform asks Beacon's lead privately whether the question fits their experience and whether they are willing to help. If they decline, the requester receives a neutral update. The operator should not share the helper's personal explanation or imply that declining harms their standing in the community.

## Make a two-sided introduction

Send each person enough context to make an informed choice. The requester should know the helper's relevant experience and the limits of what they can share. The helper should know the actual question, the desired output and any preparation expected.

After both agree, make an introduction that preserves the bounded purpose. “Compare a proposed tutorial-review request with a blank decision-packet example” is specific. “Help Alder improve marketing” invites an undefined advisory relationship.

Name who arranges the conversation and how the match will close. The platform can offer a suggested next step without controlling every interaction. Do not schedule a recurring series unless the participants have agreed that it is useful.

Avoid framing the helper's experience as a universal prescription. The introduction can say that Beacon has tried a relevant process, not that its process will work for Alder. Differences in staffing, approval authority and product complexity may matter more than the surface similarity of the task.

## Help the pair produce a usable next step

Suggest that the requester bring a sanitized example of the current review request and one point of confusion. The helper can bring a blank or approved example rather than exposing another company's work. This gives the conversation something concrete to examine.

For the fictional match, the pair notices that Alder asks reviewers for “feedback” without naming the decision. They draft a revised request identifying the exact version, factual questions, responsible reviewer and next action. That is an original illustrative outcome, not an observed result from a real peer program.

The requester decides whether to use the suggestion and checks it with the relevant company stakeholders. Peer advice does not transfer approval authority or replace professional advice where that is needed. The platform should not present an informal conversation as a certified solution.

If the question proves mismatched, close or redirect it gracefully. A helper may discover that the real problem is unavailable company approvers rather than an unclear review packet. Record the new understanding and identify an appropriate next route instead of extending the conversation indefinitely.

## Close privately and improve future matching

Ask the requester whether the original question was addressed and what action they intend to take. Ask the helper whether the brief accurately represented the request and whether they want similar introductions. Keep the closure lightweight and within the agreed privacy boundary.

A useful note might read: “Question clarified; requester will test a decision-specific review packet; helper is open to similar process questions.” It does not need a transcript, named customer details or an assessment of the requester's competence.

Separate match completion from business outcomes. An introduction, a conversation and an applied change are different events. Do not claim revenue impact or portfolio engagement improvement from a small set of closure notes.

Use recurring mismatches to improve intake questions and expertise descriptions. If many requests are too broad, offer an example of an answerable question. If helpers repeatedly receive unsuitable introductions, narrow their profile. The service succeeds operationally when it makes a relevant, consented connection around a concrete question and leaves both people clear about the next step.
