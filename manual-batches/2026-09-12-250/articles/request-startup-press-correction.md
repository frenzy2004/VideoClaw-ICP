Ask for a press correction by identifying the exact factual error, providing a verifiable replacement, and sending the evidence to the reporter or the outlet’s published correction contact. Keep the request focused and courteous. Distinguish a wrong fact from an unfavorable interpretation, and correct your own source materials if they contributed.

## Decide whether the problem is factual

Start by writing the disputed statement exactly as it appears, along with the article URL and publication date. Then write the fact you believe is correct.

The difference should be testable. “The product is available to every business” and “The product is available only to invited pilot customers” are incompatible factual statements. “The company faces an uphill battle” is an assessment that may be unwelcome without being a factual error.

Some cases involve a misleading implication rather than a wrong number or name. Explain the concrete impression a reader would take away and the fact that conflicts with it. Avoid calling every omitted detail a correction.

This distinction helps you make a request an editor can evaluate. It also protects the relationship from becoming an argument about whether the article was sufficiently enthusiastic.

If several people on your team disagree about the correct fact, resolve that internally before contacting the outlet. A correction request should not introduce another unverified version.

## Assemble the smallest persuasive evidence packet

Use a compact packet rather than a long narrative about everything the company does.

| Packet element | What belongs in it |
|---|---|
| Location | Article URL, date, and relevant paragraph |
| Disputed fact | The specific statement that is wrong |
| Correct information | A precise replacement or factual clarification |
| Evidence | The strongest dated source establishing the correction |
| Origin | Whether company material or an interview supplied the error |
| Requested action | The correction you want the outlet to consider |

This is an original working template. It is designed to help an editor compare two factual versions quickly.

Prefer evidence that directly establishes the point. A current availability page may show today’s status but not what was available when the article appeared. If timing matters, provide a dated announcement, a contemporaneous document, or another appropriate record.

Do not bury the key evidence in a folder containing dozens of unrelated files. Name the relevant page or section and explain what it establishes. Share only information suitable for the intended recipient.

## Understand the outlet’s correction process

Find the outlet’s own published policy or contact route. Some direct requests to the reporter; others provide an editorial inbox or form. Follow that process instead of guessing which senior person will create the most pressure.

The [Associated Press’s standards](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/) describe clearly labeled corrections, including for audiovisual material. That is an example of a newsroom’s approach, not a guarantee that every outlet uses the same procedure.

Identify the format involved. An article, video caption, social post, and syndicated copy may have different correction routes. A changed article does not automatically update every place the original sentence appeared.

Ask for the factual correction without demanding editorial approval rights. The journalist remains responsible for the reporting. Your role is to supply accurate information and make the error easy to assess.

If a recorded founder statement contains the relevant fact, point to the timestamp and provide the underlying factual reference. Do not require the editor to watch a full interview to discover the disputed detail.

## Write a request that stays on the point

Here is an original fictional example:

> Subject: Factual correction request for the Fieldnote article
>
> The article published on [date] says Fieldnote is available to every US business. The product was available only to invited pilot customers at the time of publication.
>
> The attached dated availability notice states that limitation, and the current access page continues to describe the invitation process. Could you please review the sentence and correct the availability information? I can answer any questions about the pilot scope.

The example uses fictional facts and placeholders. Replace them with the actual evidence, not a preferred marketing phrase.

Notice what the message leaves out: complaints about tone, references to the funding round, threats to withdraw access, and requests to add unrelated favorable information. Those additions would make a straightforward factual request harder to process.

If the correction requires nuance, propose the minimum accurate explanation rather than an entire replacement paragraph. State what is wrong and why. Allow the editor to choose wording consistent with the outlet’s style.

## Repair the source if your team supplied the error

Review the material the journalist received. Check the email pitch, press page, product description, founder recording, and any interview notes available to you.

If your own copy was wrong, say so directly. A useful message might begin, “Our briefing document incorrectly described availability. The correct scope is…” That helps the reporter understand how the mistake occurred and what else may need checking.

The [PRSA ethics code](https://www.prsa.org/professional-development/prsa-resources/ethics) calls for correcting erroneous communications for which the practitioner is responsible. Apply that principle to your own public destinations as well as the material sent privately.

For a video, identify whether the error is spoken, shown on screen, or present in the description. Correct the relevant location using the publishing system’s available process, and make the updated factual reference easy to find.

Avoid quietly replacing a source in a way that makes the history confusing. Keep an internal record of the old statement, the correction, and when the change was made.

## Check the outcome without widening the dispute

When the outlet responds, compare the result with the specific request. Did the corrected sentence now state the fact accurately? Was the error also present in a headline, caption, or related post?

If the main article is corrected but another outlet reproduced the original version, identify that separate copy and follow its own process. Do not assume the first journalist controls every downstream use.

If there is no response, send a concise follow-up through the published route. Add the original request and the essential evidence. Repeating a broader complaint will not improve the factual basis.

Close the matter when the error is resolved. Record any useful prevention step, such as adding an availability boundary to future materials or checking a frequently misunderstood term before interviews.
