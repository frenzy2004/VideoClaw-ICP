Choose a backup editor by testing whether they can resume an authorized copy of the current project without relying on the incumbent's memory. Pay for a bounded cold-start exercise, record the help required, and request one small change. Then agree realistic activation and availability terms; passing a test alone does not reserve capacity.

## Define the interruption the backup must cover

State what the business needs if the primary editor becomes unavailable. It may need a final export, one factual correction, completion of a nearly approved cut, or several weeks of continued editing.

These are different backup assignments. A person who can render an existing project may not be ready to make creative decisions on unfinished material. A strong editor may still need time to learn the business's terminology and approval history.

Identify the activation trigger and the person authorized to make the call. Avoid a vague expectation that the substitute is always watching for trouble.

Shortlist for technical and collaborative fit. [Upwork's hiring guidance](https://www.upwork.com/hire/video-editors/how-to-hire/) includes matching portfolio work and communication to the assignment. Use that for candidate selection, then gather project-specific evidence through the restart exercise.

## Prepare a safe copy of the current handoff

Work with the incumbent to create an authorized copy of the project package. Include the latest approved or current cut, source media, version notes, essential assets, and a short record of unresolved decisions.

Do not disrupt the live project to manufacture a test. If you want to test a missing dependency, use a clearly agreed exercise copy and a recoverable, non-sensitive example.

Confirm that the backup may access the material and third-party assets. Provide appropriate project permissions without sharing personal passwords or assuming the incumbent's licenses can be transferred.

Technical dependencies can block even a competent substitute. [Adobe's XML export guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/render-and-export/export-files/export-a-project-as-a-final-cut-pro-xml-file.html) documents linked-media and translation limitations. If the backup uses a different tool, resolve the interchange route separately so the drill can distinguish software loss from missing human context.

## Run the cold-start exercise

Give the candidate a small, paid task with a clear time allowance and permission to report blockers. Ask them to identify the current version, locate the relevant sequence, explain unresolved issues, and complete one representative change.

Use this original observation sheet.

| Restart step | Evidence requested | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify current work | Name the latest cut and source project | Was the correct version found? |
| Reconstruct context | Summarize approved and pending decisions | What information was missing? |
| Open and link | Show usable media and dependencies | Which links, fonts or plugins blocked work? |
| Resolve a test issue | Explain the safe recovery approach | Was help requested appropriately? |
| Make one change | Deliver a small agreed revision | Did unrelated parts remain intact? |
| Hand back the result | Name version, changes and remaining risks | Can the business continue from it? |

Record elapsed restart time and active work time separately. Waiting for a missing permission is a different problem from difficulty understanding the timeline.

Before the exercise, agree what the candidate may change and where the test result should be saved. Require a separate output version so the original remains available for comparison. A continuity test should demonstrate careful recovery and communication, not create another ambiguous project copy that the business might accidentally treat as final.

Tell the candidate how to ask for help. The purpose is to discover realistic dependencies, not to reward silent guessing or conceal information necessary for safe work.

## Evaluate judgment as well as speed

A fast restart is useful only if the candidate selects the right version and preserves important decisions. Compare the result against the current reference cut and the requested change.

Look for appropriate restraint. Did the backup avoid redesigning approved sections? Did they identify uncertainty before replacing licensed assets or changing a factual claim? Did they explain what could not be verified?

Classify assistance required. A missing project note suggests a handoff improvement. An unavailable license suggests an entitlement issue. Confusion about ordinary editing operations may indicate a skill mismatch. Do not combine all three into a single failure score.

Ask the candidate to describe what they would need for a real activation. Their answer may reveal a dependency the exercise did not cover, such as access to an audio specialist, an unavailable motion-graphics file, or a decision-maker in another time zone.

## Buy availability explicitly

Once the candidate passes the relevant drill, discuss how backup work is activated. Ask about notice, staffed hours, conflicting commitments, maximum scope, and whether any capacity is actually reserved.

A paid test does not automatically create a retainer, and a friendly agreement to “help if needed” is not guaranteed availability. Record the arrangement in terms both parties understand.

Request actual fees for any readiness work, reservation, or activation. Avoid inventing standard backup premiums or assuming the substitute can match the incumbent's price when taking over at short notice.

Agree a communication route and a response expectation. Also define what happens if the first backup is unavailable: the business may need another qualified option or a reduced delivery scope, not an unsupported promise of uninterrupted production.

## Keep the backup ready with minimal maintenance

Fix the handoff gaps discovered in the exercise, then repeat only the affected part where useful. If a missing font prevented the test, establish the lawful access or substitute and verify that the package now opens.

Keep the backup's context current when the tool, project structure, or business requirements materially change. Do not send every routine message or expose unrelated company information merely to maintain familiarity.

Before a known absence, confirm availability and provide the latest authorized package. During activation, specify the current priority and decision authority rather than expecting the backup to infer them from a large archive.

After the interruption, reconcile versions and return control deliberately. Identify which project is now current, what changed, and what remains unfinished.

The purchase succeeds when a named person has demonstrated the relevant restart and the business understands the conditions under which they can do it again. That is concrete continuity evidence, not simply a second editor's contact details in a spreadsheet.
