Hire an editor in another time zone only after comparing their staffed hours with your actual review and decision windows. Overnight production can help, but an unanswered question can erase that advantage. Use a dated weekly overlap grid, include holidays and clock changes, and rehearse one blocked decision before committing.

## Identify who must be available together

List the interactions that genuinely need a shared window: kickoff, live edit review, ambiguous feedback, technical troubleshooting, or final approval. Many other tasks can proceed asynchronously if the brief is complete.

Name the people involved, not merely the offices. A vendor may have an account manager online when the assigned editor is absent. Your company may have staff at work while the only person authorized to approve a claim is unavailable.

Ask which hours are staffed for production and which are only support hours. [Video Husky's service FAQ](https://www.videohusky.com/best-video-editing-service/) publishes Philippines-based weekday working hours. Confirm the current arrangement for the quoted service rather than treating that page as a guarantee about a particular editor.

Write your own review availability honestly. If the founder never reviews before late afternoon, a nominally overlapping business day may not provide a usable decision window.

## Use dates and named zones

Record both locations with a date-aware time-zone name, such as America/Los_Angeles and Asia/Manila. Avoid relying on an abbreviation or a fixed offset copied from an old comparison table.

[NIST's daylight-saving guidance](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/popular-links/daylight-saving-time-dst) identifies US seasonal clock changes and exceptions. A recurring meeting's relationship to another country's workday can therefore change during the year.

Use an up-to-date calendar to convert the actual dates, and have both parties confirm the result. Include the local date as well as the hour; one person's Monday evening may be the other's Tuesday morning.

Check at least one representative week now and one after a relevant clock change. Do not assume every location changes on the same date or changes at all.

## Build the seven-day overlap grid

The following is an illustrative calculation, not a vendor commitment. Assume a Los Angeles buyer reviews Monday through Friday from 17:00 to 19:00, and a hypothetical Manila supplier is staffed Monday through Friday from 09:00 to 18:00.

For the week beginning September 14, 2026, those supplier hours run from 18:00 on the preceding Los Angeles date to 03:00 on the supplier's matching calendar date.

| Los Angeles date | Buyer's review window | Relevant Manila workday | Shared window in Los Angeles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday, September 14 | 17:00–19:00 | Tuesday, September 15 | 18:00–19:00 |
| Tuesday, September 15 | 17:00–19:00 | Wednesday, September 16 | 18:00–19:00 |
| Wednesday, September 16 | 17:00–19:00 | Thursday, September 17 | 18:00–19:00 |
| Thursday, September 17 | 17:00–19:00 | Friday, September 18 | 18:00–19:00 |
| Friday, September 18 | 17:00–19:00 | Saturday, September 19 | None under these assumptions |
| Saturday, September 19 | Unavailable | Sunday, September 20 | None |
| Sunday, September 20 | Unavailable | Monday, September 21 | None for this buyer |

Add actual holidays, planned leave, and the availability of the named decision-maker. Replace every hypothetical window before using the sheet for a hiring decision.

The grid exposes something an offset alone misses: a Friday review request may have no shared window until the next working week. It also shows where changing the buyer's availability could help without requiring the editor to work outside the agreed schedule.

## Rehearse one blocked question

Choose a realistic question that cannot be answered by the editor alone: which product screenshot is approved, whether a claim can remain, or which of two conflicting feedback notes takes priority.

Place the question at a specific date and time in the grid. Find when the buyer sees it, when the buyer can decide, and when the editor next resumes work. Record the total wait as a scenario, not a promised delivery time.

Then improve the brief or authority arrangement and repeat. Could the buyer supply a decision rule in advance? Could a named colleague approve the issue? Could the vendor continue another part of the job while waiting?

Do not solve every mismatch by expecting permanent evening or weekend availability. Ask what schedule the people can sustainably commit to and what exceptional support actually costs.

## Separate routine overlap from escalation

Agree the normal response route, the live-review window, and the procedure for genuinely urgent problems. A chat application being installed does not mean anyone has agreed to monitor it at all hours.

Confirm whether the advertised hours belong to the assigned editor, a shared support desk, or the company's headquarters. A geographically distributed team can provide several kinds of coverage, but the buyer needs the person who can resolve the specific issue. Ask the supplier to name that role for each critical window.

Ask who covers leave and local holidays. Determine whether substitute staff have the context and authority to help or can only acknowledge the message.

For recurring work, identify a cutoff for sending consolidated notes before the supplier's next shift. Make clear that the cutoff supports the agreed schedule rather than guaranteeing every requested change fits into one shift.

Keep escalation proportionate. A missing file that blocks production is different from a new creative preference. Name the kinds of issue that justify an urgent contact and the person authorized to initiate it.

## Choose the supplier against the working pattern

Compare candidates using the same dated scenario. One may offer more overlap; another may work effectively with a clear asynchronous brief. The better fit depends on how the business actually makes decisions.

Ask both sides to read one proposed calendar invitation aloud in their own local date and time. That simple check can catch a mistaken day before a recurring review is booked.

Record the accepted staffed hours, review windows, holiday process, escalation arrangement, and any seasonal change requiring another check. Include the complete quote if a different service level is needed.

For the illustrative Los Angeles and Manila pattern, winter offsets change the overlap. Recalculate rather than extending the September table unchanged into another season.

Finally, try the working pattern on a bounded paid assignment. Observe unanswered questions and decision delays, not just export speed. Hiring across time zones works when both parties understand when decisions can happen and the production brief lets useful work continue between those windows.
