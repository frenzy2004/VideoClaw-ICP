Allocate a shared portfolio media cost by separating company-specific charges from common work, choosing an explicit basis for the shared portion and reconciling the result to the original total. Use a cost-allocation worksheet with traceable inputs and exceptions. This is an operational explanation of consumed resources, not a pricing guide or accounting policy.

## Define what the allocation is for

A portfolio platform may coordinate a shared recording session, a cohort media package or a common resource library. Before dividing its cost, decide whether the worksheet is for internal visibility, program planning or an authorized company charge. These uses require different approvals.

An internal view of resource use does not automatically authorize invoicing companies. If the program has not agreed that companies will pay, label the output as an internal allocation view and keep it separate from billing. Route accounting, tax and contractual questions to the responsible finance or professional advisers.

[The FinOps Foundation's allocation framework](https://framework.finops.org/framework/capabilities/allocation/) distinguishes shared from dedicated costs and calls for a documented approach. Its setting is technology spending; the media worksheet below is an editorial adaptation, not a claim that cloud allocation rules govern portfolio production.

Define the cost period and the source documents included. A recording-day invoice and a later editing invoice may belong to the same project but represent different work. Do not combine them merely because they arrived in the same month.

## Separate direct work from common work

Identify costs attributable to a specific company before allocating the shared remainder. A company-specific edit can be assigned directly when the source record supports that attribution. Common setup, shared capture or a platform-wide resource may need a separate basis.

Do not force every cost into a company bucket. Some work exists because the platform chooses to run the program. It may be appropriate for the platform to retain that cost under its approved policy rather than distributing it to companies that did not request or use it.

Record the evidence behind each classification: invoice line, work log, session record or approved project scope. If a line combines direct and shared work without a defensible split, mark the uncertainty and ask the responsible owner for clarification.

Keep the allocation basis separate from the expense description. “Shared recording setup” says what was purchased. “Allocated by confirmed capture blocks” says how the worksheet divides it. A reader needs both to understand the result.

## Choose a basis that the inputs can support

Possible bases include an equal split among eligible participants, a fixed agreed proportion or a variable measure of documented use. [FinOps guidance on shared costs](https://www.finops.org/wg/identifying-shared-costs/) discusses these different approaches and the need to explain them to stakeholders. None is automatically fair for every media program.

Use a basis related to the shared work and simple enough to verify. Confirmed capture blocks might be relevant to a shared recording session. The number of final clips may be a poor basis when one company needs many short exports from the same material and another needs one long cut.

Avoid treating noisy activity as precise consumption. A library download count may include repeated downloads, previews or staff testing. If the data cannot support a usage-based split, use an approved simpler method or retain an unallocated amount rather than inventing precision.

Write the rule before calculating company shares. State the eligible participants, measurement period, excluded work, rounding method and treatment of missing inputs. Changing the basis after seeing which company pays more undermines the worksheet's explanatory value.

## Work through a fictional allocation

Suppose an internal cohort recording worksheet contains 120 fictional cost units. These units are teaching values, not currency, vendor rates or market benchmarks. The source record attributes 20 units directly to an Alder-only edit and identifies 100 units as common recording work.

The approved internal rule divides common work by confirmed capture blocks. Alder used two blocks, Beacon one and Cobalt one. Four blocks therefore form the denominator.

| Participant | Confirmed blocks | Share of 100 common units | Direct units | Total allocated units |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder | 2 | 50 | 20 | 70 |
| Beacon | 1 | 25 | 0 | 25 |
| Cobalt | 1 | 25 | 0 | 25 |
| Total | 4 | 100 | 20 | 120 |

The calculation is common pool multiplied by the participant's blocks divided by all eligible blocks. The totals reconcile: 70 plus 25 plus 25 equals 120. This establishes arithmetic consistency, not that the selected basis is appropriate for every program.

Attach the block log reference and the approval for the internal allocation rule. If Beacon disputes whether its block occurred, the worksheet should show where that fact can be checked. A formula cannot resolve an uncertain input.

## Handle exceptions without hiding the remainder

Imagine Cobalt cancelled before its planned session and no capture block occurred. Do not silently count the original booking as usage. The responsible program owner must apply the agreed cancellation treatment or decide how to classify the unused shared capacity.

The worksheet might retain that portion with the platform, use a previously agreed booking-based rule or await clarification. Each is a different policy choice. Record the chosen basis and approval rather than presenting an after-the-fact redistribution as a mathematical necessity.

If inputs are missing, show an unallocated line or a provisional state. State what evidence would resolve it and who owns the next action. Do not spread an unexplained remainder across companies simply to make every line appear final.

For real currency amounts, apply the organization's approved rounding treatment and reconcile any remainder explicitly. This article does not prescribe a finance policy. The operator's responsibility is to make the calculation and its assumptions inspectable by the people who own that policy.

## Review the worksheet before using the result

Check the source total, direct classifications, shared pool, participant list, measurement inputs and formula independently. A correct formula with an incomplete denominator still produces a misleading allocation. Confirm that no company-specific cost has also been included in the common pool.

Share the relevant company view with enough context to explain its share, while protecting other companies' confidential work details. A company may need the rule and its own inputs without needing another company's private production brief.

Record the review state: draft, inputs confirmed, rule approved or finalized for the stated internal purpose. If the worksheet will support an authorized charge, finance should apply its own process before any invoice is issued.

Keep the final sheet with its source references and exception notes. It provides a transparent account of shared media resources already consumed. It does not establish vendor prices, prove cost savings or turn an internal allocation into an obligation to pay.
