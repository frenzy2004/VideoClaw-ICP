Before posting a Demo Day recording publicly, review the exact offering language in its picture, sound and accompanying text. The original event audience and the proposed public audience may be different. Prepare a timestamped media packet for qualified counsel rather than assuming the event label or a quick edit settles the distribution question.

## Separate event participation from the proposed publication

Describe the original setting: who organized the event, who could attend, whether participation was in person or virtual and what distribution was understood at the time. Preserve the organizer’s actual instructions rather than reconstructing them from memory.

Then describe the proposed publication separately. Is the recording going on an unrestricted website, a public video channel or another destination? Will the whole pitch appear, or an excerpt with a new title, thumbnail and caption?

The [SEC’s general-solicitation guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/general-solicitation) describes the determination as fact-specific and explains that qualifying Demo Day treatment has conditions. It does not provide a blanket answer for every founder recording or later publishing decision.

This article is a media-preparation workflow, not legal advice or an exemption analysis. Company-specific questions belong with qualified counsel who understands the offering and communication history. Your job is to give that reviewer an accurate account of the proposed asset and audience.

## Inventory every place round details appear

Watch the complete recording with sound and mark references to an ongoing or planned offering, the kind of securities, amounts, terms, proceeds or an invitation to discuss investing. Do not limit the review to the obvious fundraising slide.

Inspect the opening introduction and discussion after the pitch. A host or participant may mention round details while the screen shows only a company logo. Include those moments in the packet even when the founder did not say the words.

Read the captions and any transcript independently. Automated text can preserve a statement removed from the audio or introduce a misleading transcription. The public description, thumbnail and post copy also belong in the review because they change the communication surrounding the video.

Do not collect or send unnecessary personal information. Give counsel the relevant asset through an appropriate private channel and identify the passages that need attention. A public test upload is not necessary to show the reviewer what you intend to publish.

## Build a timestamped distribution-review packet

Use this original worksheet for one exact proposed release. The entries below are fictional flags for review, not a list of automatically prohibited statements or a recommendation about what your company may say.

| Location | Observed content to capture | Publication question for counsel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:42–00:49 audio | Founder mentions an ongoing round | Does the proposed public use fit the company's circumstances? |
| Slide visible at 01:18 | Offering amount or terms appear | What treatment, if any, is needed for this destination? |
| 02:03–02:11 discussion | Host invites investment inquiries | Does this change the analysis of the excerpt? |
| Caption track | Same language or a transcription difference | Does the final text accurately match the reviewed communication? |
| Thumbnail and title | Round-focused label | Does the surrounding presentation change the review? |
| Proposed destination | Unrestricted public page and accompanying post | Is this audience and distribution method appropriate? |

Attach the exact video revision, the full transcript, proposed text and an audience note. Identify any earlier public or private distribution you know about. Do not omit an inconvenient context because it complicates the review.

Write precise questions rather than requesting a vague “legal check.” For example: “Please review revision D together with this title and caption for the proposed unrestricted page.” That gives the adviser a defined communication to assess.

## Understand why the Demo Day label is insufficient

The [SEC’s Rule 148 adopting release](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2020/33-10884.pdf), in its Demo Day discussion, explains the limited intended scope of offering-related communications and restrictions on virtual participation. This supports caution about treating every audience as interchangeable; it is not a ruling on your recording.

Do not turn the article’s source summary into a self-service compliance checklist. The relevant facts can extend beyond what is visible in the clip, including the company’s offering arrangements and other communications. A media editor will not necessarily have that context.

Likewise, do not infer that a recording is impermissible merely because the original event does not fit one set of conditions. Ask counsel to evaluate the actual circumstances and applicable pathway. The editorial task is to identify the issue accurately, not to issue a legal conclusion in either direction.

Keep the product demonstration distinguishable from the offering discussion in your private edit map. That can make possible alternatives easier to review, but it does not establish that a product-only excerpt is automatically appropriate for public posting.

## Apply the reviewed treatment across all media surfaces

Once the responsible reviewer provides direction, translate it into specific production actions. Identify the source interval, required treatment, affected caption text and revised surrounding copy. Keep the instruction tied to the exact asset rather than applying it loosely to every company video.

If a segment is removed, inspect the resulting transition for accuracy. Do not splice a qualification away from the statement it limits or make a host’s words appear to describe a different topic. A legal-review edit still needs an honest, coherent presentation.

Regenerate or correct affected captions and transcripts, then compare them against the new export. Check the thumbnail, title and post copy again. Removing a visible number while leaving it in the spoken track is not a completed media revision.

Send material changes back through the agreed review process. Do not assume approval of one revision extends to a new caption that reintroduces the original offering language. Keep the source, proposed copy and reviewed release distinguishable in your private notes.

## Approve a specific release, not a reusable assumption

Record who reviewed the proposed publication, which revision and destination they reviewed, and any conditions or limits they communicated. Keep that record private and avoid presenting it as a public certification.

Before release, compare the actual export and accompanying text with the reviewed version. Check the first and last frames, the flagged time ranges and any discussion included after the formal pitch. Confirm that no older caption file or thumbnail has been attached accidentally.

If the distribution plan changes, revisit the affected question. A different audience, excerpt or message may require another review. Do not reuse a prior decision as a universal rule for every future Demo Day recording.
