Place a founder-camera overlay only where it does not cover the product action, result, captions, or necessary context. Map those exclusion areas scene by scene before editing. If no safe position exists, remove the camera for that interval or give the founder a separate shot rather than shrinking the proof.

## Decide what the founder's face adds to this moment

A camera overlay can introduce the person speaking, make an explanation feel direct, or clarify that narration comes from the founder rather than the product. It does not need to remain visible during every second of a product demonstration.

Watch the planned sequence without the camera layer. Identify the moments where seeing the speaker adds useful context. An opening introduction may benefit; a dense result screen may not. Make those decisions before choosing a permanent corner.

Do not assume a larger face creates a better investor demo. The product evidence has a job too. If the investor cannot inspect the result because the overlay occupies the useful area, the layout is failing the task.

[YC's design guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck/) emphasizes obvious, legible meaning. For an overlay, that means preserving the hierarchy between the explanation and the specific screen evidence being explained.

## Draw exclusion zones on representative frames

Select a still frame from each materially different screen state. Mark where the decisive action occurs, where the result appears, and where supporting labels or captions will sit. Include temporary panels and menus, not just the uncluttered opening screen.

Treat these as exclusion zones for the camera. A region that looks empty before a click may become a confirmation message afterward. Review the full action before declaring a corner safe.

For a fictional scheduling app, the lower-right area might contain a save button, the upper-right might hold the account and status information, and the bottom band might be needed for captions. The apparently harmless default corner could cover exactly what the investor needs to see.

Keep enough surrounding context for the crop to make sense. An overlay can obscure the relationship between a label and its value even when it does not directly cover either. Assess the entire meaning-bearing region, not just the letters.

## Choose among four layout treatments

Use a stable corner when the same area stays unimportant throughout the scene. Resize the overlay only enough to preserve a useful face view without taking excessive room from the product.

Use a reserved side panel when the product can remain readable in the reduced main area. This is a tradeoff: moving the face outside the product picture may prevent overlap while making all interface text smaller. Test the actual delivered size.

Remove the overlay during a decisive action or dense result. The founder's narration can continue if it remains clear who is speaking. A brief absence is often less distracting than a camera tile moving repeatedly around the screen.

Use a separate founder shot for a transition or explanation that does not need simultaneous product evidence. Then return to an unobstructed product view. Do not present a staged reaction shot as if it were a simultaneous response to a live event when it was recorded separately.

[Apple's iMovie documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/imovie/mova1aaa682b/mac) describes positioning, resizing, and timing picture-in-picture clips. Use those controls, or equivalent ones in your editor, to implement the layout decision rather than letting a default position decide it.

## Build a scene-by-scene overlay plan

Make the plan specific enough that an editor can follow it without guessing which UI details matter. This fictional example demonstrates a job assignment:

| Scene | Product exclusion zone | Founder treatment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduce the task | Selected job in center | Small stable corner | Establish the narrator |
| Open assignment panel | Panel and confirm control on right | Move to a planned left-side safe area at the scene cut | Keep the action visible |
| Show recipient result | Job identifier and status occupy most of frame | Camera absent | Let the investor inspect the result |
| Explain a limitation | Result remains available as context | Separate founder shot, then return | Avoid covering the evidence with both face and label |

The movement should happen at a clear transition, not chase the cursor. If several moves are required in one short scene, reconsider whether the overlay belongs there at all.

Add the caption region to the plan. A camera tile can avoid the product yet still cover the text a muted viewer relies on.

## Test the composition at the investor's viewing size

Export a short draft with the overlay and captions in place. Play it at the intended size, whether that is a video-call share, a deck window, or a laptop player. Do not evaluate the layout only in a large editing canvas.

Ask a viewer to identify the action and read the result. If they move the player controls or enlarge the window to see around the camera, note that as a layout problem. Your preferred viewing conditions may not match theirs.

Check the overlay's own framing too. A face cropped through the mouth or a gesture repeatedly leaving the tile can become a distraction. Keep the crop stable and natural, but do not enlarge it at the expense of the product proof.

Watch transitions in motion. A dissolve or animated movement can temporarily pass through an exclusion zone even if both its starting and ending positions are safe. Inspect the entire transition, especially around brief result notifications.

Listen while watching. If the founder says “here” or “this number” while the camera covers the target, change the composition or make the spoken reference more precise.

## Preserve the layout when the demo changes

Tie the overlay plan to the product build and final edit. A redesigned interface can move a button into a previously safe corner. A new caption style can consume more of the picture. Reusing the old layout without checking can reintroduce obstruction.

Keep an unobstructed product capture whenever possible. That gives you room to revise the camera treatment without rerecording the entire workflow. Do not permanently combine layers too early if your editing arrangement allows them to remain separate.

For the final pass, watch once with attention only on the product and once with attention on the founder. The first pass checks evidence; the second catches distracting framing or inconsistent appearances. Neither replaces the other.

The useful deliverable is an exclusion-zone map and a composition that respects it, not a universal recommendation to put the face in one corner.
