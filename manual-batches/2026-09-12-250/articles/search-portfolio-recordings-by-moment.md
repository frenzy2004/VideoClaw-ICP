Make portfolio recordings searchable by connecting accurate transcript text and useful audience questions to specific moments in approved videos. Preserve the recording, speaker context and access boundary behind each result. Test names, acronyms and ambiguous terms manually so a promising search result takes a founder to the right explanation rather than an unrelated or misleading passage.

## Define the moment a reader needs

A recording title often describes a session broadly: founder interviews, marketing hiring or customer onboarding. The useful answer may occupy only a few minutes. Start with the questions people bring to the library, then identify where the recordings actually address them.

Distinguish a mention from an answer. A speaker saying “we struggled with onboarding” does not necessarily explain how the problem was solved. A search result should describe what the passage contains, not turn a passing reference into a promised lesson.

Use questions that a portfolio operator can recognize. “How did the team decide which customer to interview?” is more useful than an internal session code. Keep the wording faithful to the content rather than optimizing it into a claim the speaker never made.

This task creates navigation within existing approved material. It does not authorize new publication or produce a short clip. The original recording and its context remain the destination.

## Build the transcript and visual index together

Start with the available transcript and check passages that will become search entry points. Confirm names, product terms and numbers against the recording. Mark unclear words instead of guessing.

[Panopto's video-search description](https://www.panopto.com/features/video-search/) explains searching spoken content and jumping to a matching moment. That describes a useful retrieval pattern, but it should not be read as an accuracy guarantee for your recordings.

Some useful information appears on screen rather than in speech. A slide may show the steps while the speaker says “use this sequence.” Record the visible context needed to understand the answer, and distinguish it from words actually spoken.

[Panopto's editing documentation](https://www.panopto.com/features/video-editing/) also describes indexing spoken and displayed words and exposing generated transcript text. When using any comparable system, verify the exact material that is searchable and whether users can correct it.

Do not put private slide notes or company-only material into a broader search index merely because the technology can extract it. Search visibility should follow the underlying content's agreed access.

## Create a moment index

Use a small index that connects the reader's question to the source passage. The following entries describe fictional recordings.

| Reader question | Recording and moment | Confirmed content | Context needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How did Alder choose interview participants? | Customer research session, 08:40 | Speaker explains selection criteria | Early-stage customer research |
| How did Beacon review a first marketing hire's work? | Operator discussion, 17:10 | Speaker describes a draft-review routine | Small team with founder involvement |
| What belongs in an onboarding walkthrough? | Product education session, 05:25 | Demonstration of the first user task | Product version shown on screen |

Add the company or speaker identifier, transcript excerpt location, access state and last check. The index should let another editor verify that the result points to the intended passage.

Choose a start point that includes the question or enough setup to make the answer intelligible. Jumping directly to “that was the mistake” may save seconds while removing the meaning.

If a passage covers several questions, create separate entries only when the answers are materially distinct. Do not fill the index with keyword variations pointing to the same vague segment.

## Test difficult searches deliberately

Create a short retrieval test set using terms likely to fail. Include a company name that resembles an ordinary word, an acronym, an alternative spelling and a question whose language differs from the speaker's.

For a fictional example, a transcript renders the company name “Alder” as “older.” The company-name search fails even though the passage is present. Correct the transcript or add a verified alias in the index, then repeat the search. Do not change the speaker's wording beyond correcting the transcription error.

An ambiguous term requires a different fix. “Activation” may refer to customer onboarding, an event sponsor or a software setting. Add enough description to the result that a reader can choose the relevant meaning.

Test from the intended user's access level. A coordinator may retrieve a private recording that a general portfolio member cannot open. A result that advertises an inaccessible private passage can still reveal information and frustrate the user.

Record whether the result was correct, understandable and accessible. A keyword match alone is not a successful retrieval.

## Keep timestamps attached to versions

Editing a recording can shift timestamps. If an introduction is removed or a new segment inserted, the old moment links may lead to the wrong content.

Tie each index entry to a recording version and review affected entries when that version changes. A small technical re-export may preserve timing, while a content edit may not. Verify rather than assuming.

Check companion changes too. A transcript corrected after company review may alter the searchable text without changing the video. Record the correction so another operator understands why a search behaves differently.

Where a system supports stable chapter or segment references, evaluate whether they preserve the intended destination through your editing process. Do not promise that every link will survive a change merely because the platform offers chapters.

For retired recordings, remove or clearly mark active search recommendations under the agreed archive arrangement. A current-looking result should not send a founder to obsolete product instructions without context.

## Use the index to improve the library

Review failed searches and requests that still reach the platform team. Some failures indicate missing labels or transcription errors. Others reveal that the library does not contain an answer at all.

Keep those causes separate. Better tagging cannot create a lesson the recording never covered. Record the unanswered question as a content gap for future programming rather than stretching a marginal passage into a misleading result.

Ask a few company operators to find a real answer and explain what they would do next. If they reach the right timestamp but cannot understand the lesson without watching the whole session, improve the entry's context or choose a different passage.

Maintain a small set of known-good retrieval tests as the library grows. Re-run them after significant index or recording changes. These tests protect useful paths without requiring a complete manual review of every search result.

The output is a trustworthy route from a practical question to a specific explanation. The library remains the source of context, while the moment index helps founders reach the part that matters.
