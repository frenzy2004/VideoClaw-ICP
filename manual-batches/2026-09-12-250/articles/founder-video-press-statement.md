Record a founder video statement for the press as a dated, self-contained account of a verified company development. Identify yourself, explain the customer consequence, state important limits, and provide a monitored contact route. Test likely excerpts before release so a sentence remains accurate even when a journalist uses it outside the full recording.

## Define the statement's purpose and authority

A prepared statement gives the company's position on a specific development. It is not a substitute for an independent interview, supporting evidence, or answers to follow-up questions. Decide what it can establish before writing it.

Name the development in ordinary language. “We are changing the availability of our pilot” is clearer than “We are entering an exciting new chapter.” Identify who has authority to confirm the relevant facts and which teams must check customer instructions.

Create a dated fact sheet with the development, affected audience, current status, known exceptions, and contact owner. Mark anything still under review. Do not allow a polished recording to make an unresolved question appear settled.

The founder should know which parts are firsthand knowledge and which have been verified by responsible colleagues. If a question falls outside that scope, the statement can name the limitation without guessing.

For matters involving legal obligations, active investigations, or safety, obtain the appropriate specialist review. This workflow is an editorial preparation method, not legal or crisis-management advice.

## Write the verified development and its consequence together

Keep conditions close to the claims they qualify. “Access is open” followed much later by “for existing pilot customers only” is vulnerable to misunderstanding. Say “Existing pilot customers can request access” as a complete thought.

[CDC's spokesperson guidance advises acknowledging uncertainty instead of over-reassuring](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/media/pdfs/CERC_Spokesperson.pdf). Its context is public-health emergencies; the startup application here is narrower: distinguish what you know from what you have not yet confirmed.

Build the script from five beats: identity and date, verified development, customer consequence, limits, and follow-up. These are an original structure for this communication job, not a required newsroom format.

Remove promotional adjectives that do not help someone understand the event. A statement about a service change should not turn into a funding pitch. If you include a reason, make it specific enough to be meaningful and supported enough to withstand a follow-up question.

Read each beat aloud. A journalist should not need to untangle several subordinate clauses to identify what actually changed.

## Use a fictional script to test the structure

Imagine a fictional company, Harbor, moving an optional pilot into a limited release. This example contains invented details for illustration only:

“I am Lena Ortiz, cofounder of Harbor. This statement reflects our position as of the date shown with this recording. Existing pilot workspace owners can now request the shared-calendar view. It will not switch on automatically, and members' current view remains unchanged.

“We are opening requests in this limited group so we can confirm the setup process before offering it more broadly. We have not announced availability for other accounts. Customers in the pilot can use the contact route in their workspace, and journalists can reach our named press contact through the accompanying statement page.”

Before using a structure like this, replace every condition with verified facts about your own company. Do not retain the example's limited-release rationale unless it is genuinely your reason.

Notice what the script avoids: a universal availability claim, an unsupported quality claim, and an implied date for a wider release. The statement's usefulness comes from a precise boundary, not from sounding triumphant.

## Record a simple shot that preserves the meaning

Use a stable direct-address frame and intelligible speech. Record the statement in complete sections, but preserve enough context that editing does not detach a qualification from its claim.

Keep names, dates, and conditions in front of you if necessary. Accuracy matters more than appearing to improvise. If you make a factual error, stop and record the complete affected sentence again rather than trying to correct it with an ambiguous aside.

Avoid unrelated B-roll, dramatic music, or visual effects that compete with the statement. If you show supporting material, label its date and status. Do not use a prototype image to illustrate a statement about current availability without making the distinction clear.

[AP's standards allow limited technical improvements to video without changing its content](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/). Those are AP's newsroom rules, not a promise that a supplied video will be accepted. They are a useful reminder to keep the source recording straightforward and preserve the underlying meaning.

Retain the original recording and the approved script for reference.

## Run an excerpt-integrity review

Make a two-column worksheet: likely excerpt and interpretation when heard alone. Choose the sentences most likely to be quoted, especially the opening claim, the reason for the decision, and the next step.

For the fictional Harbor statement, “We are opening requests” fails by itself because the audience boundary is missing. “Existing pilot workspace owners can now request the shared-calendar view” is more self-contained.

Also test pronouns. “They can contact us” may be clear in the full video but ambiguous in a short excerpt. Replace it with the relevant audience when precision matters.

Check time words such as today, tomorrow, and next week. Pair the recording with a visible date and consider using explicit dates in sensitive instructions. If the video is encountered later, the viewer should be able to recognize its time context.

Ask a reviewer who has not seen the fact sheet to summarize the development. Compare their interpretation with the approved facts. Repair the script if the mismatch comes from your wording rather than blaming the reader.

## Deliver the video with a usable context package

Provide a transcript that matches the final recording, the statement date, the speaker's correct name and role, and a monitored press contact. Include links to the supporting company material a journalist can inspect.

Keep the statement at a stable destination with a clear way to find updates. If facts change, publish a dated update and make the relationship between versions understandable. Do not quietly replace a statement in a way that leaves old quotations unexplained.

State any relevant usage information plainly, and do not imply that sending the file guarantees coverage or editorial control. A journalist may quote only a small part, seek other sources, or decide the development is not newsworthy.
