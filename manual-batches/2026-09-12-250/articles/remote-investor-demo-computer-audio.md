Make remote investors hear a product demo by routing the application's sound into the meeting separately from your microphone, then testing both from a recipient device. Hearing the sound on your own computer is not enough. Check the exact live-app or recorded-video sharing path before the meeting begins.

## Draw the two audio paths before changing settings

Identify the sounds the investor needs to hear. One path is your explanation through the microphone. Another is the product's output: a voice response, notification, media playback, or other sound generated by the application.

Write those paths in plain language. “Founder microphone to meeting” and “product audio to shared content” are enough. Add a third path only if the demo genuinely uses another source, such as a phone or external device. Do not assume that a sound entering your headphones also enters the meeting.

Use a private call with a colleague or a second permitted device to test the arrangement. Keep the recipient device from creating an audible feedback loop in the same room. If it is nearby, use headphones and mute its microphone.

Make the test specific. Ask the recipient to identify a spoken sentence and a distinctive product sound. “Can you hear me?” verifies only the founder microphone and leaves the product path untested.

## Choose the route that matches the demo material

For a live application, share the relevant screen or window and explicitly include computer sound where the meeting tool supports it. [Zoom documents its Share Sound option](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0063608) for this separate audio route.

For a prerecorded movie, use the video-file sharing route appropriate to the platform and file. [Zoom's built-in video player](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0064733) provides file playback with controls hidden from attendees. Test the actual movie rather than assuming a successful live-app test covers it.

Do not switch routes casually in the meeting. Moving from a live window to a local player may change what is shared and how its sound reaches the audience. Rehearse the transition if both appear in the same demo.

Check the requirements and controls in your installed version. If an option is unavailable, resolve that in rehearsal rather than improvising with a speaker aimed at a microphone. The latter creates a different and often less clear sound path.

## Run an isolation test for each source

Test the founder microphone alone first. Say a normal sentence at the position you will use while operating the demo. Turn your head as you will during the real interaction; a microphone test conducted straight toward the screen may not represent your working posture.

Then stop speaking and trigger the product sound. Ask the recipient to describe it, not merely confirm that some noise occurred. If the sound is a spoken response, ask them to repeat its meaningful phrase.

Next, mute the founder microphone temporarily while leaving the intended product share active. If the product becomes inaudible, it may have been reaching the meeting acoustically through your microphone rather than through the intended digital route. Inspect the configuration and repeat.

Finally, test the combined state you will use in the meeting. Speak briefly before the product event, pause for it, then resume. Confirm that neither source disappears and that the product sound does not make the narration uncomfortable to hear.

## Use a recipient audio test card

Keep the test short enough to repeat after a device or sharing change. The following fictional card is for a voice-enabled scheduling app:

| Test | Founder action | Recipient must report | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Voice only | Explain the next action | Complete sentence is clear | Check selected microphone and position |
| Product only | Trigger the spoken confirmation | Exact confirmation is understandable | Check product output and shared-sound route |
| Mic muted | Repeat the product event | Product remains audible | Investigate accidental acoustic pickup |
| Combined | Introduce, pause, play, resume | Clear separation between sources | Adjust source levels and speaking timing |
| Route change | Move from app to movie if planned | Movie audio arrives correctly | Test the new share method independently |

Record the tested devices and route privately. Do not turn the card into a promise about every investor's connection or hardware.

A useful pass result is concrete: “Recipient heard the confirmation while the founder microphone was muted.” That is much stronger than “audio checked,” because it tells you which path was actually verified.

## Resolve echo and missing sound at the correct point

If the recipient hears a delayed duplicate, inspect duplicate meeting connections and open speakers before changing the product. A phone and laptop joined together can create multiple audible paths. Keep only the intended speaker route active.

If they hear your voice but not the product, check the application's output, then the meeting's shared-sound setting. Confirm that the application itself is producing the expected sound. A muted browser tab or a different output device can be the upstream problem.

If they hear the product but not you, return to the microphone selection and mute state. Do not raise the movie volume to compensate for missing narration. These are separate sources and should be diagnosed separately.

If both work alone but become confusing together, change the timing of your speech. Let the product finish a meaningful phrase before explaining the result. This is often a communication problem rather than a need for more audio processing.

After any repair, repeat the recipient test. Your local meters or headphones are useful clues, but the investor's received sound is the relevant result.

## Rehearse the start and stop of the shared sound

Begin the demo with the product in a known quiet state. Tell the investor when the next sound is the product speaking or responding. This avoids confusion between your narration and the system's output.

Keep unrelated applications quiet while computer audio is shared. A message alert or browser video may travel through the same path. Use a prepared demo environment and close unnecessary audio sources before the call.

At the end, stop the shared audio when you stop the demonstration. Verify that the meeting returns to ordinary conversation without a looping clip or stray application sound. Rehearse this ending as deliberately as the start.

Save the test card with the exact demo setup so you can repeat it after changing a headset, computer, meeting application, or file.
