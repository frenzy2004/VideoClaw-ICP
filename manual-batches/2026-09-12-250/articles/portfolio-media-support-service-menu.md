A portfolio media service menu tells companies which help they can request, what they must supply, and what they will receive. Build it around recognizable founder problems, then connect each entry to a bounded output and a clear request route. Keep unavailable services separate from work your team can actually accept.

## Start with requests founders already make

Collect a small sample of recent requests from the channels your platform already uses. Preserve the requester's language: “We need someone to review this interview” is more useful than “editorial enablement.” Note the immediate problem, the requested deadline, the material available and the result the company expected. You are discovering the shape of a service, not counting phrases to justify an expansion.

Group requests only when they can be answered through substantially the same work. A factual review of an existing company profile differs from writing that profile from scratch. Restoring access to an approved video differs from creating a new video. If those requests share one entry called “content support,” the founder cannot predict what happens next.

The [service-catalog explanation from Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/service-request-management/service-catalog) explicitly includes marketing among services that can be organized into a catalog. For a portfolio team, the useful adaptation is to describe the help in company language. A founder should recognize a situation before needing to understand how the platform is staffed.

## Define the smallest useful result

Write the output before naming the service. “Annotated feedback on one supplied draft” describes something the recipient can inspect. “Stronger storytelling” describes an aspiration. Your menu may explain the purpose of an output, but the purpose must not replace the deliverable.

Then describe the boundary around that result. State which source materials are included, how the company participates and what ends the request. A review might end when the platform returns its comments; the company still decides what to change. A library retrieval request might end when the requester confirms that the correct released version opens.

Avoid making a different menu entry for every format if the work is identical. Equally, do not hide substantially different services behind one label. The test is whether the same inputs, effort decisions and completion evidence apply. Where a request can expand, give the founder a choice between a small defined service and a separate scoping conversation.

Write exclusions as useful directions. “We do not arrange customer participation; please secure the customer's involvement before requesting interview review” explains the missing dependency. A flat rejection leaves the company guessing how to become ready.

## Draft a six-entry menu

The following is a fictional menu for a platform that already provides limited editorial assistance. It is an example to adapt, not a description of any vendor's services.

| Request | Bring this | Receive this | Separate work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Review a company profile | Current draft and approved facts | Annotated factual and clarity review | Original research or complete rewrite |
| Find an approved asset | Company, intended use and approximate subject | Link to an identified released version | New editing or additional permissions |
| Shape an interview outline | Audience question and participant context | A focused conversation outline | Recruiting interview participants |
| Check a media package | Files and the company's release instructions | Missing-item receipt | Deciding legal rights |
| Get editorial office-hour help | One decision and relevant material | A documented next step | Ongoing campaign management |
| Request a fund-channel feature | Public story and proposed audience | An editorial selection decision | Guaranteed placement or reach |

For each entry, add an eligibility line and the request destination. If alumni and current companies have different access, explain the difference alongside the service. Do not make eligibility depend on knowing a particular partner.

Completed fictional entry: “Review a company profile” is available to current cohort companies with a named company approver and an existing draft. Submit through the platform's Media Help form, selecting Profile Review; the editorial coordinator owns triage. Alumni use the Alumni Help route to confirm availability first. If the form is inaccessible or the request is misrouted, contact the platform support lead with the request reference. This names eligibility, intake and escalation without promising a turnaround time.

Also name the response the requester should expect. An acknowledgement confirms receipt. A scoping response confirms that the team understands the work. Acceptance confirms that the platform has agreed to perform the defined service. These are different events; a catalog should not collapse them into a promise of completion.

## Make the request route match the menu

The request form should ask only for information needed to decide whether that service can proceed. A company profile review needs the draft, source of company facts and the approver. An asset search needs enough clues to find the item and understand its intended use. Requiring both groups to complete a large generic creative brief creates unnecessary work.

[Atlassian's marketing service template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/templates/marketing-service-management) separates request types and connects them to a service portal and tracking. You can apply that idea with a simple form and register; the operating choice is more important than the application.

Give every request a reference the founder can use in follow-up. Return the selected service name, supplied materials, outstanding questions and next expected communication. If a staff member redirects a request, retain the original reference and explain the change. Otherwise, a company may submit again and create two apparent commitments.

Place the human-help route beside the self-service options. A founder who cannot classify a request should be able to describe the problem briefly. That route should lead to classification, not become a hidden premium service available only to persistent callers.

## Test the menu with realistic situations

Ask several people who were not involved in writing it to choose an entry for a realistic task. Include a first marketing hire, a founder and an experienced company operator. Give them the situation without suggesting the service name.

For example, “Your product description changed, and the fund still has the old wording” should lead to a correction or profile-review route. “You have an interview recording but no approved customer quotation” should expose whether your menu distinguishes editing from obtaining permission. Record where people hesitate and which words they use instead.

Do not ask merely whether the menu looks clear. Ask the participant what they expect to receive and what they think happens after submission. If their expected output exceeds your defined service, revise the entry before adding explanatory footnotes.

Keep a confusion log with the scenario, chosen entry, expected result and proposed correction. A recurring mismatch usually indicates a problem with the service boundary or label. More training is not automatically the answer.

## Maintain a menu the team can honor

Assign a review trigger to each entry. A staff change, a recurring excluded request or a backlog that prevents acceptance may require an update. Temporarily unavailable services should have a visible state and an alternative next step, rather than continuing to collect requests nobody can fulfill.

Review completed requests against their menu descriptions. Did the company provide the stated inputs? Did the delivered result match the promise? Did staff routinely perform hidden extra work? Use those observations to refine scope, not to criticize founders for reading the menu literally.

Keep a short change history so company contacts can tell when a service changed. For a material change, update the request form and acknowledgement together. An accurate menu makes the next conversation easier: the company knows what to ask for, and the platform can explain exactly what it has accepted.
