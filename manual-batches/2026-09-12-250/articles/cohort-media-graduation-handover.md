Hand cohort media back to companies with a company-specific delivery manifest, a tested receiving destination and a clear record of what the platform will still maintain. Graduation should close defined service responsibilities, not leave founders guessing which files are final or when links may stop working. Confirm receipt before marking each package complete.

## Define what the company is receiving

Start with the media service's agreed closeout scope. Identify which final outputs, source materials, captions, credits and instructions belong in each company's package. Do not assume that every file produced during the cohort is included or suitable for transfer.

Separate company-specific work from shared program assets. A founder interview may belong in a company package under the agreed use instructions, while a group event recording may remain in the platform's shared archive. A company appearing in a recording does not automatically mean it should receive unrestricted copies of the entire event.

Give each deliverable an identifiable state: final approved output, editable source where included, reference material or unresolved working item. Avoid folders titled “final” that contain several competing exports without explanation.

Write the closeout boundary in plain language. The handover may end routine editing support while leaving the platform responsible for a public event page or correction requests. Those continuing responsibilities should be named separately from the files being delivered.

## Confirm the receiving owner and destination

Ask each company to nominate the person responsible for accepting the package and the destination it controls. A temporary personal account may not be the intended long-term home. The company should make that choice through its own authorized process.

[Google distinguishes individually owned files from team-owned shared-drive files](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/7212025?hl=en). That illustrates why sending a link and transferring a maintained package are different actions. Check your actual system and destination; do not assume that a copied file or new share link transfers ownership or every permission.

Record whether the handover uses downloads, an approved copy process or another agreed transfer method. Keep account administration with the appropriate administrators. A media closeout checklist should not require sharing credentials or broad access to a company's storage.

Confirm any constraints that affect delivery, such as file formats the company can open or whether editable project files are included. Resolve these before the final day rather than discovering that the recipient can view a preview but cannot use the supplied material.

## Populate a company handover manifest

Imagine a fictional cohort closeout for Alder. The package contains final company media plus references to shared program material.

| Item | Package state | Delivery or reference | Company acceptance check | Continuing owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder interview | Final approved export INT-08 | Company-controlled destination | Video opens and matches approved version | Company for its future copies |
| Caption file | Matched to INT-08 | Included alongside video | Timing and language correspond to export | Company for later adaptations |
| Founder portrait | Approved image PHOTO-14 | Included with supplied credit | Correct person, crop and credit record | Company media contact |
| Editable title card | Included under agreed scope | Source file plus preview | Company can open the file and identify dependencies | Company after receipt |
| Cohort recap | Shared program asset | Maintained platform link | Link works for intended audience | Platform event editor |
| Unfinished optional cut | Not a final deliverable | Exception record EX-02 | Company understands unresolved scope | Named closeout decision owner |

Add the company ID, package revision, destination, recipient, date sent, receipt evidence and unresolved questions. The manifest should describe the exact package delivered, not a list of everything the platform once planned.

The unfinished cut remains an exception. It is not hidden inside a final folder or silently counted as completed because the cohort ended.

## Check completeness and relationships

Compare the package with its manifest before sending it. Confirm that every listed file is present and that captions, thumbnails, descriptions and credits correspond to the correct output. A complete file count can still conceal mismatched companions.

[Canto's implementation guidance](https://www.canto.com/glossary/digital-asset-management-implementation/) treats migration as involving metadata, permissions and user readiness. A company closeout uses those concerns on a smaller scale: the recipient needs context and usable relationships, not merely a pile of files.

Check editable sources for dependencies that the recipient needs under the agreed scope. A project may refer to fonts, graphics or media that cannot be included or require separate arrangements. Record that fact instead of promising that every source project will reproduce identically in another environment.

Remove unrelated company material from the package through the normal review process. Inspect contact sheets, shared folders and project references for accidental cross-company inclusions. A handover is a company-specific delivery, not an export of the whole cohort workspace.

## Verify receipt with an acceptance exercise

Ask the receiving owner to perform a small practical check: open the approved interview, find its matching caption file, identify the supplied portrait credit and locate the shared recap. The exercise tests whether the company can use the package without asking the original editor to reconstruct it.

Record the result for each check. “Link received” is not sufficient if the company cannot download an included file or cannot identify which export is current. Keep access problems and missing companions assigned until they are resolved or an explicit alternative is agreed.

For Alder, the recipient may find that the title-card source opens but a referenced graphic is absent. The coordinator returns to the agreed scope and either supplies the permitted dependency or documents the limitation and accepted alternative. Do not invent a replacement asset without checking its intended use.

Keep the acceptance record proportionate. It needs the recipient's confirmation, checked package revision and exceptions, not a lengthy technical audit of the company's systems. The goal is usable delivery within the media service's scope.

## Close responsibilities without breaking ongoing references

After acceptance, record which services end, which shared links remain maintained and where future correction requests should go. State any actual access transition communicated by the program owner; do not invent a universal grace period.

Check platform-owned pages that link to company media. If the handover changes the maintained destination, update those references through the appropriate publishing process. A transferred package does not automatically repair public links or partner copies.

Keep unresolved work separate from routine closeout. An accepted final interview can be complete while an optional cut awaits a decision. The exception record should name its owner and next action so graduation does not erase it.

The completed work product is a verified company package, a receipt record and a responsibility map for shared material. It gives companies usable media at the end of the cohort while making the platform's remaining commitments explicit rather than relying on personal memory or indefinite access assumptions.
