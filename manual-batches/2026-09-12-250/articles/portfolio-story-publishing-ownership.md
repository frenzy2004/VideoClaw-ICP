Choose a portfolio story's originating publisher by matching the audience to the organization that owns the facts and can maintain the page. Assign other channels clear amplification roles instead of publishing competing copies by default. Use a publishing-ownership sheet that names the source destination, correction owner, supporting channels and unresolved permissions before production begins.

## Separate the story from the distribution opportunity

A company may have a useful story while the fund has a larger or different audience. That does not automatically mean the fund should host the full article. Start with the reader's question and the organization best placed to answer it accurately over time.

A product explanation may belong with the company that maintains the product. A cross-company operating lesson may fit a platform resource. A cohort announcement may belong on the program's own site. These are editorial choices to test against the actual story, not universal rules about who deserves visibility.

[a16z distinguishes owned channels from portfolio services](https://a16z.com/what-is-new-media-in-2026/). For a publishing decision, keep support separate from ownership of the destination. The platform can help prepare a story without becoming its originating publisher.

Clarify what “originating publisher” means in your working sheet: the agreed home of the full maintained story. It does not settle copyright ownership, legal rights or every search-engine decision. Those questions may require separate instructions or specialist review.

## Evaluate audience, facts and maintenance together

Ask three questions. Who needs the complete story? Who can confirm its central facts? Who will correct or update the page when those facts change? A destination that fits only the first question may create a maintenance problem later.

Consider the expected life of the content. A product walkthrough may change with releases, while a dated event report can remain a historical account. A founder reflection may need a clear date rather than ongoing rewriting. The publishing owner should understand the kind of maintenance the story requires.

Check the practical destination. Can the organization publish an accessible page, preserve a stable link and receive correction requests? Do not choose a channel solely because it has a convenient empty slot. A social post may amplify a story well without serving as its maintained home.

Also consider the company or participant's preferences and the agreed use of source material. If a private workshop lesson has not been authorized for public publication, choosing a public destination is premature. Resolve that boundary before discussing channel reach.

## Fill a publishing-ownership decision sheet

Imagine a portfolio editor considering four fictional stories. Each receives a different source destination and supporting role.

| Story | Primary reader job | Proposed originating publisher | Correction owner | Supporting channel role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder product setup guide | Understand the current product workflow | Alder resource page | Alder product-content lead | Fund newsletter links with a short contextual introduction |
| Cohort media-submission guide | Follow the platform's shared process | Platform resource library | Program operations owner | Company contacts share the maintained link |
| Beacon founder's dated reflection | Understand a specific personal experience | Beacon's chosen company blog | Founder plus company editor | Fund channel quotes only approved material and links |
| Cross-company event recap | Understand the program event as a whole | Program event page | Platform event editor | Participating companies amplify the recap within agreed use |

Add the approved source material, intended publication date, audience boundary and decision reference. The sheet records a proposal until the relevant owners confirm it.

For Alder's guide, the fund does not need to host a duplicate tutorial merely to support the company. A short introduction can explain why the guide is useful to the newsletter audience and point to the maintained company page. That preserves a clear route for future product corrections.

## Assign amplification without creating duplicate ownership

Define what each supporting channel will publish: a link, a short introduction, an approved excerpt or a separately authorized full copy. These formats create different review and maintenance needs. Do not call all of them “sharing the story.”

A short newsletter introduction should accurately describe the destination and avoid adding unsupported claims. A social post should not imply that a company's product solves a problem more broadly than the source page supports. Amplification is still editorial work even when it contains only a few sentences.

For a full second copy, create a separate republication instruction sheet covering attribution, permitted edits and update responsibilities. Choosing an originating publisher does not answer those details automatically. Keep that second-copy workflow distinct from the initial ownership decision.

Search behavior is another separate concern. [Google's canonical URL guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) describes signals expressing a preferred URL for duplicate or similar pages. A declared editorial source is not a guarantee that a search engine will display that page. Route technical indexing decisions to the site owners without promising rankings.

## Test the correction path before release

Use a hypothetical correction to check whether the ownership sheet is complete. Suppose Alder changes a setup step a month after publication. Who updates the guide, who tells the fund about any affected excerpt and which destinations need a review?

If the answer is “the person who wrote it will probably remember,” the maintenance assignment is not ready. Name a role or maintained contact route that survives staff changes. Keep the current source link in the supporting channel record.

For the program event recap, a company may report an incorrect founder title. The platform editor owns the event page correction, while each company owns changes to its own posts. The sheet should make those boundaries visible rather than implying that one edit updates every copy.

Distinguish a correction from a new story. A factual error needs the appropriate correction process. A later product development may justify a new article or an update note. The originating publisher should choose the treatment with the relevant fact owner.

## Record the decision and revisit it when the story changes

Before production proceeds, confirm the source destination, fact reviewer, publishing owner, correction route and amplification roles. Keep unresolved permissions or ownership questions as holds rather than burying them in a general project note.

Revisit the decision if the story changes materially. A company interview may evolve into a cross-company analysis, or a public announcement may become a private operator resource. The original destination may no longer fit the audience or source boundaries.

After publication, record the actual source URL and supporting destinations. Check that introductions point to the intended maintained page and that no unplanned full copy has appeared in a platform-controlled channel.

The completed publishing-ownership sheet makes one story's home and upkeep clear across organizations. It helps a portfolio team support distribution while preserving a dependable source, a responsible correction owner and an understandable role for every participating channel.
