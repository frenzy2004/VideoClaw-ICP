Record remote cofounders in one shared call with a deliberate layout, clear turn cues, and a short audio test. Verify the saved recording before attempting the application take. The goal is a coherent conversation in which every founder can be seen and heard, not a montage of separately polished performances.

## Make one call the recording room

Choose a time when all founders can participate without another meeting ending the session abruptly. Leave room for setup, a test recording, the application take, and playback. A calendar invitation for exactly the final video's duration creates pressure to accept avoidable technical mistakes.

[YC explicitly allows a recorded video call](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/) when founders cannot gather physically. It also asks for all founders and spontaneous speech from bullet points. Use the relevant program's current instructions to set your content and duration.

Designate one person to start and stop recording. Everyone should know when the test ends and the real take begins. If several people record backups, give those files distinct names and agree which recording is the candidate submission. Otherwise, each founder may assume someone else's copy contains the complete group.

Keep the call founders-only during the application take. A helpful friend can review the test afterward without occupying a video tile or unexpectedly becoming the active speaker. Close unrelated collaboration tools so a notification does not become an accidental production cue.

## Establish the picture that will actually be saved

Do not judge the result only by what appears on each founder's screen. [Zoom's recording-layout documentation](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0062314) distinguishes computer recordings that follow the recorder's view from cloud recordings governed by host settings. Test the route you will actually use.

For a small founding team, a stable group view makes it straightforward to see everyone. Check the saved file rather than assuming a gallery view on your own laptop guarantees that outcome. Avoid changing layouts during the take unless you have a clear reason and have already tested the transition.

Ask each person to frame their face at a similar apparent size. They do not need identical rooms, cameras, or lighting. They need enough light on their face, a steady device, and a position that does not drift out of frame when they speak.

Remove empty participant tiles where your chosen setup permits it. Review displayed names for clarity and appropriateness. A nickname left over from a personal call can distract more than an ordinary home background.

## Solve the audio path before practicing the words

Have each founder say a normal sentence while the others listen quietly. Then test a handoff: one person finishes a sentence and the next begins. Listen for the quieter voice disappearing, a delayed echo, or the start of a sentence being clipped.

Headphones can help separate received sound from microphone input. Whatever equipment you choose, test it in the exact arrangement used for recording. Moving from a laptop microphone to a headset after the rehearsal changes the sound path and deserves another short test.

Do not compensate for a bad microphone connection by asking someone to shout. Check the selected input, cable or wireless connection, and distance from the microphone first. Keep fans and other steady noises away from the recording position where practical.

Mute unrelated devices that have joined the same call. If a second device is present only to inspect the image, it should not create a second audible route. A successful test means every founder's normal speaking voice remains intelligible when played back at one comfortable listening level.

## Use a remote take sheet instead of a shared script

Prepare a small coordination sheet that describes turns rather than complete sentences. It should help people cooperate across latency, not make them read in unison. The following fictional example is for three founders; adapt the number of turns to your team and the program's limit.

| Person | Speaking job | Handoff cue | During other turns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maya | Introduce the company and immediate problem | Finish with Arun's name | Look toward the camera and listen |
| Arun | Explain what the team is building | Finish the thought, then yield to Jo | Keep hands away from recording controls |
| Jo | Add relevant founder context and close | Complete the last sentence, then pause | Watch for an accidental interruption |
| Recorder | Start the agreed take and save it | Silent start signal before speaking | Keep the tested layout unchanged |

The named handoff is a suggestion, not a requirement to insert a theatrical introduction. A natural sentence ending and a brief pause may be enough. Practice whichever cue the team can use without talking over one another.

Keep bullet points near the camera if you need reminders. Do not arrange a long document below the screen and spend the take visibly reading it. Each founder should understand their contribution well enough to finish the thought in their own words.

## Capture one complete attempt and inspect its seams

Start recording before the first spoken word so the beginning is not clipped. Give everyone a moment to settle, then begin using the agreed cue. Let the final speaker finish before anyone reaches for the stop control.

If two founders overlap briefly, continue when the meaning remains clear. If an entire sentence is lost to an audio dropout, mark that attempt as unusable and record another complete take. Do not spend the session debating tiny imperfections while a more important communication problem remains unresolved.

Watch the saved attempt together. Focus first on the transitions: does one founder appear to interrupt another, does the image jump unexpectedly, or does a voice begin before the previous point is understandable? These are coordination problems, not reasons to rewrite the company story.

Check the entire group remains visible when required. Listen through the ending. If you trim setup silence, verify that the trim did not remove the first consonant or the last word. Preserve a natural conversational result instead of assembling a falsely seamless exchange from unrelated statements.

## Choose the usable take and deliver that exact file

Select a take using a simple order: correct content, intelligible audio, complete founder participation, then presentation polish. A relaxed, accurate take is usually a better candidate than one with elaborate production and a missing founder's contribution.

Give the chosen file a unique name and share it with all founders for a final factual check. Identify it by its content and duration as well as its filename. Delete nothing merely because another export exists; keep private source recordings until the submission is settled.

Upload through the intended destination and play the resulting asset. Hosting can introduce a new processing or access step, so a local playback test is not the end of delivery. Confirm the first frame, all voices, and the ending in the actual submitted version.
