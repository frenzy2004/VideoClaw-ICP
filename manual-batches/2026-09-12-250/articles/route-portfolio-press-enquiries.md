Route a portfolio press enquiry by identifying the company involved, clarifying the journalist's request and deadline, and securing one accountable response owner. The platform coordinates the handoff; it does not speak for every company. Use an enquiry receipt and ownership log so an urgent message cannot disappear between shared inboxes.

## Recognize the kind of enquiry

A message from a journalist may ask for a company comment, a factual correction, a photograph, an interview or background information. Start by recording what is being requested without rewriting it into the story the platform would prefer to tell.

Identify the outlet, journalist, contact route, relevant company, topic and stated deadline. If the request came through a forwarded message, preserve the original wording and timestamps where appropriate. Ask for missing context through a verified channel rather than guessing what the sender wants.

A dedicated route makes the handoff easier to explain. [Chatham House's contact page](https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/contact-us) directs media enquiries to a press office. A portfolio team can similarly define an intake route while keeping company response authority separate.

Do not confuse acknowledgement with a promise to comment. A receipt can say that the enquiry has reached the platform and is being routed. It should not promise a spokesperson, an interview or a publication-ready answer until the relevant company has agreed.

## Separate platform coordination from company authority

Create a contact map with a primary company media contact, an agreed substitute and a platform coordinator. Record when the company last confirmed those roles. A founder's old email address in a directory is not enough to establish current response ownership.

For questions about the fund's own program, the platform may be the appropriate respondent. For questions about a company's product, customers or plans, route the request to that company's authorized contact. If a story concerns several companies, each may need a separate decision about participation.

[a16z distinguishes portfolio services from owned media activity](https://a16z.com/what-is-new-media-in-2026/). In an enquiry workflow, the corresponding operational distinction is support versus speaking authority. Coordinating a response does not authorize the platform to answer company-specific questions from its own assumptions.

Keep internal context out of unnecessary forwards. The receiving company needs the journalist's request and relevant routing history, not private observations about other portfolio companies. If one email concerns competitors, create separate handoffs with only the information each party needs.

## Complete an enquiry receipt and handoff log

Consider three fictional requests arriving at a shared portfolio inbox. The operator's first job is not to draft quotations; it is to make response ownership explicit.

| Enquiry | Requested material | Deadline context | Response owner | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PR-201, Alder | Comment on a product announcement | Journalist supplies date, time and zone | Alder's confirmed media lead | Ask whether the company will respond |
| PR-202, Beacon | Approved founder portrait | Magazine layout deadline | Beacon's media contact | Confirm the intended use and supply an approved asset if authorized |
| PR-203, fund program | Explanation of a cohort workshop | No deadline supplied | Platform communications lead | Clarify timing and the precise question |

The full log adds received time, original request reference, acknowledgement, owner acceptance, current state and closure evidence. Use separate states for routed, accepted, preparing, responded, declined and closed without response. “Forwarded” is an action, not proof that somebody owns the next step.

For PR-201, the coordinator sends Alder the request and asks for an explicit acceptance of ownership. If the media lead is unavailable, the agreed substitute receives the handoff. The platform records who accepted, not merely who was copied.

## Handle time pressure without guessing facts

Confirm the deadline exactly when it matters. “This afternoon” depends on the sender's location and publication schedule. Record the date, time and zone as stated, along with any later revision. Do not convert an ambiguous deadline silently.

If ownership is unconfirmed, follow the company's agreed escalation route. Tell the journalist what you can truthfully confirm about the handoff without exposing private availability details. If no authorized response is ready, the platform should not fill the gap with an improvised quote.

Separate simple asset retrieval from factual response preparation. Supplying a portrait still requires checking the correct person, current approved version and intended use. Answering a product question may require a company subject-matter reviewer. Treating both as “press help” hides different dependencies.

When a request includes an allegation, sensitive claim or unfamiliar legal issue, preserve the request and route it to the company's designated communications or professional advisers. This workflow does not decide what the company should say or whether a response is legally required.

## Keep one current response path

Once the company accepts ownership, confirm who will communicate with the journalist. The company may respond directly, with the platform assisting on logistics. Alternatively, it may authorize the platform to transmit specified material. Record that narrow instruction rather than assuming general permission to speak.

Avoid parallel drafts moving through different contacts without coordination. If a founder and marketer both receive the enquiry, ask the company to identify the final response owner. The operator should not choose between conflicting company statements based on which arrives first.

For PR-202, the company might approve one portrait and a short identification line. The coordinator sends only that package and keeps a reference to the approved asset. A broader biography, previous interview quote or customer logo should not be added because it seems helpful.

If the journalist changes the question, return the new request to the response owner. An earlier approval may not cover a different topic or format. Keep the current question and current response together so the log does not imply that an old answer resolves a new enquiry.

## Close the enquiry without claiming coverage

Record what actually happened: the company responded, supplied an asset, declined, requested more time or did not provide a response before the deadline. Include the response reference and any unresolved follow-up. Do not mark “coverage secured” unless a publication has actually appeared and been checked separately.

If an article later publishes, hand its link to the coverage-record process. That is a different job from routing the original enquiry. A response can be handled correctly even if no story appears, and a story can appear without a prior enquiry.

Review failures at the handoff level. Repeated unaccepted forwards suggest an outdated contact map or unclear ownership. Missing deadline zones suggest an intake problem. Conflicting responses suggest that the company needs a clearer internal decision route.

The finished work product is a traceable enquiry receipt with an accountable response owner and a documented outcome. It lets the platform provide reliable coordination without turning a shared inbox into an unofficial spokesperson for every company.
