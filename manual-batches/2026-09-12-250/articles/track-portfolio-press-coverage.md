Track portfolio press coverage with a company-resolved clipping register that distinguishes original reporting, repeated copies, company announcements and false name matches. Verify each item before summarizing it. The immediate work product is a reliable record of what appeared and where, not a claim about media program performance or the business value of coverage.

## Decide what belongs in the record

Define the coverage scope before collecting links. You may want editorial articles, broadcast segments, trade-publication mentions or a separate record of company announcements. Keep those categories visible rather than combining every appearance into one undifferentiated count.

A company newsroom post and an independently reported article can both be useful records, but they answer different questions. A wire release repeated on several sites also differs from several separately reported stories. The register should preserve those distinctions even if a later report groups them for a specific purpose.

[Meltwater's coverage guidance](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/how-to-measure-press-coverage) discusses different mention types and keeping historical records. This article uses that as context for building a checked clipping register, not for adopting reach estimates, valuations or business-impact claims.

Specify the collection period and known gaps. A register assembled from accessible web results does not establish complete coverage of print, broadcast, paywalled or local-language media. Mark unverified leads and unavailable sources separately from inspected items.

## Resolve the company before accepting a match

Portfolio companies may have common names, renamed brands or products whose names overlap unrelated organizations. Maintain a small identity reference with the stable company ID, current public name, former names where relevant, website and a few confirmed disambiguating facts.

Use those facts to inspect a candidate mention. An article about “Alder” may concern a tree species, a different company or the portfolio business. The headline alone may not settle the identity. Open the source and look for a confirmed product, founder, website or other reliable context.

[Google Alerts can email matching new Search results](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/4815696?hl=en), with settings such as language and region. Treat an alert as a discovery lead, not a verified clipping or a guarantee of complete monitoring. The operator still needs to inspect the result.

When identity remains uncertain, keep the item in a review state. Do not assign it to the most familiar company simply to empty the queue. Record the missing evidence and the person who can help resolve it.

## Populate a company-resolved clipping register

Consider a fictional review of five web results. The names and article references below are invented to illustrate classification, not actual coverage.

| Record | Candidate item | Company check | Coverage class | Group / status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CL-301 | Trade reporter's interview with Alder's confirmed founder | ALD-01 matched by founder and product | Original editorial reporting | Story group G-11; verified |
| CL-302 | Regional site reproduces the same interview with attribution | ALD-01 matched | Reprint of editorial story | G-11; verified copy |
| CL-303 | Beacon announcement distributed through a wire | BCN-02 matched by company website | Company-issued announcement | Release group R-04; verified |
| CL-304 | Another site carries the same Beacon release | BCN-02 matched | Wire redistribution | R-04; verified copy |
| CL-305 | Article about an unrelated Alder business | Identity does not match | False match | Excluded, with reason |

The working register also includes source URL, outlet, author if stated, title, publication date, date checked, brief factual mention note and any access limitation. Keep source date and discovery date separate: a result discovered today may have been published earlier.

For G-11, retain both URLs because they represent different destinations, but label the shared story relationship. A later summary can truthfully say that the register contains one original story and one verified reprint, rather than implying two independent reporting efforts.

## Check repeated copies without assuming identity

Compare the actual content and attribution before grouping items. Similar headlines do not prove that two articles are copies. One outlet may independently report the same announcement, add interviews or publish a substantially different analysis.

Record the basis for a grouping decision: identical text, explicit source attribution, a shared wire identifier or another inspectable relationship. If the relationship is uncertain, mark it as suspected and keep the items separate until reviewed. Do not guess which outlet originated a story solely from the order in which search results appeared.

Preserve updates to a story as part of its history where appropriate. A corrected headline at the same URL is not automatically a new clipping. A follow-up article at another URL may be a distinct story even if it covers the same company and event.

For a page with inaccessible content, record only what you could actually verify. A search snippet may establish that a candidate exists but not support a full summary or duplication judgment. Do not bypass access restrictions or reproduce an entire article to make the register convenient.

## Write notes that support later use

Keep mention notes short and factual. “Founder quoted about the product's onboarding workflow” is more useful than “strong positive coverage” when the operator has not defined a sentiment method. Link to the source so a later reader can inspect the context.

If a company asks to use the mention in a newsletter or media kit, route it through the normal editorial and rights checks. A clipping register is not permission to reproduce an article, photograph or publication logo. Store references and limited notes rather than copying large passages.

Record corrections and disputed identity checks. If CL-305 was initially assigned to ALD-01, the exclusion note should explain the corrected company match so the same false positive is not repeatedly re-added. Keep the correction factual, not a judgment about the source.

Use company IDs to connect records across rebrands. The displayed company name can reflect the article's historical wording while the stable ID preserves the relationship to the current directory record.

## Summarize only what the register establishes

At the end of the collection period, report the categories and limitations actually supported by the checked records. Separate original reporting, reprints, company-issued material, unresolved candidates and excluded matches.

Do not convert the number of URLs into audience reach or business impact. A story appearing on several sites does not establish how many distinct people saw it. Likewise, a quote in an article does not prove that the platform caused the coverage.

Keep this process separate from evaluating the overall media program. The register supplies cleaner inputs for later interpretation, but it does not decide whether a program improved fundraising, sales or company visibility.

The completed work product is a traceable clipping record with company identity, source relationships and review states intact. It lets a portfolio operator answer “What actually appeared?” before attempting the more difficult question of what that coverage means.
