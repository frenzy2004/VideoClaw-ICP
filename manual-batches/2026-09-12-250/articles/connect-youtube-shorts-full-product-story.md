Connect a YouTube Short to a longer product video by choosing a destination that answers the next question the Short creates, then adding it through YouTube’s related-video feature. Match the verbal invitation, destination title, and opening context. Test the published link instead of relying on a pasted description URL.

## Decide what the Short finishes and what comes next

A useful Short can deliver a complete small insight while leaving a sensible next question. It does not need to hide the answer to manufacture a click. Identify the boundary between the two pieces before choosing a longer destination.

For example, a Short might show how a reviewer spots a request with missing information. The longer video can explain the complete clarification workflow. The first piece answers “What should I notice?” and the second answers “What happens after I notice it?” That is a coherent progression.

Avoid linking every Short to the same company overview. A general introduction may not answer the specific question raised by a workflow clip. Choose the closest useful continuation, even if another video is newer or more polished.

Write two sentences in the planning sheet: what a viewer learns from the Short, and what they can learn next. If they say essentially the same thing, the destination may only repeat the excerpt. If they describe unrelated topics, the handoff needs another destination or a clearer explanation.

## Use the related-video control, not a text workaround

[YouTube’s related-video instructions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157) let eligible creators select a video from their channel after uploading a Short. The selected item must be public or unlisted, and the feature requires advanced feature access. Check those conditions in the channel you actually use.

Open the Short’s details in YouTube Studio, select the related destination, and save. Treat that as a publishing setting distinct from the video’s captions, description, or spoken ending. An editor can render a perfect invitation while the publisher forgets to attach the destination.

[YouTube’s link guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639?hl=en) says URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable. Do not build the viewing path around a pasted URL that looks like a link only in your draft document.

This feature connects to eligible YouTube content, not directly to an arbitrary external product page. If the eventual journey includes your website, first make the longer video useful on its own. Avoid asking viewers to navigate several unexplained hops before receiving the answer promised in the Short.

## Write a destination matching worksheet

Here is a fictional plan for three Shorts extracted from an existing product walkthrough. The worksheet makes each link a content decision rather than a default setting.

| Short promise | Next question | Related destination | Opening check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spot incomplete requests | How do I ask for clarification? | Clarification workflow demonstration | Names the missing-information problem |
| See who should act next | How are responsibilities assigned? | Reviewer assignment walkthrough | Defines the roles before showing settings |
| Recognize a completed decision | Where can I review the outcome? | Decision history tour | Shows the record being discussed |

Add a final column for the exact published video selected. A working title in a planning document is not enough to distinguish an old recording from its replacement.

Notice that these paths do not all lead to a full webinar. A shorter focused walkthrough may be the better continuation. The destination should earn its additional viewing time by answering the next question with appropriate context.

If no existing video fits, do not pretend one does. Publish the Short as a standalone explanation or prepare a suitable continuation before adding an invitation that cannot be fulfilled.

## Make the invitation specific and natural

Replace a generic “watch more” ending with a short statement of the next benefit. “The related video shows how to send the clarification request” tells viewers why to continue. Keep the wording accurate to what the destination demonstrates.

Avoid hard-coded instructions that depend on an interface location you have not checked. A spoken reference to the related video is more durable than a detailed claim about where a button appears on every device. The official help page describes the link below the channel handle; still verify the experience your audience will encounter.

Let the Short finish its small answer before the invitation. Cutting away just before the result may make the excerpt feel incomplete. Conversely, an extended outro can overwhelm a useful short demonstration. Keep the invitation proportionate to the piece.

Check captions and visual text alongside the audio. The destination promise should remain understandable with sound off. Do not obscure the product detail that makes the Short useful, and do not add several competing requests to follow, comment, subscribe, and visit a website in the same closing moment.

## Test the whole viewing handoff

Use a normal viewer session to open the published Short and follow its related-video link. Confirm the destination, visibility, and opening context. A saved setting in Studio is not a substitute for testing the viewer’s path.

Watch the destination’s beginning as if you had seen only the Short. Does it explain the example, or does it open with event housekeeping? Can the viewer tell that they arrived at the promised answer? Add or improve navigation on the longer recording when appropriate, but do not assume the related link jumps to your preferred timestamp.

Check that the destination remains available outside your team’s permissions. An unlisted video can still be reachable through its link, so choose visibility deliberately. Do not use a customer-restricted recording merely because it appears in the same channel library.

Document failures precisely. “The link opens last quarter’s interface walkthrough” is an asset-selection problem. “The right video opens but the promised topic is hard to locate” is a content handoff problem. Fixing the title alone may solve neither.

## Maintain the relationship as videos change

Keep a small register of Short, destination, promise, and last checked version. This is especially useful when a product walkthrough is replaced. The Short may still show a valid action while its related destination has become outdated.

When revising the destination, revisit the invitation as well. If the longer video now answers a different question, the original wording can become misleading even though the link still works. Either restore the intended answer, select another destination, or revise the Short when appropriate.

Evaluate the path with the analytics your channel actually provides and with viewer questions. Do not equate a Short’s total views with people who continued to the longer video. A high-level count cannot tell you whether the linked answer was found or understood.

The finished deliverable is a tested relationship between two assets. Keep the first useful, make the second relevant, and ensure the link genuinely connects them. That turns repurposing into a coherent viewing journey instead of a collection of excerpts pointing toward an unrelated flagship video.
