Export a narrated investor deck by treating the recording as a timed sequence, not a pile of slides converted into pictures. Check when each visual appears, what the founder says over it and how the last frame behaves. Approve the finished video only after watching the actual export from beginning to end.

## Freeze the deck version you intend to narrate

Identify the approved deck and make the recording against that revision. A late change to a result label can leave the narration describing a different value. Changing slide order can be even harder to detect when the spoken transitions still sound plausible.

List the slides with embedded product clips, staged builds or unusually long explanations. These need particular attention because their internal timing may differ from the time spent on the slide. A chart appearing halfway through a sentence is not the same as a static slide held under narration.

Use neutral revision names for the deck and export. Record the relationship privately: deck review-C produced video review-C-1. Avoid overwriting the only known-good file while experimenting with a new recording.

Before recording, remove material that should not appear in the delivered video. Check visible notes or overlays included by your chosen recording method. Do not assume that every recording workflow captures exactly the same surfaces as a conventional slideshow export.

## Create a visual-and-voice timing ledger

Build this original ledger from the approved deck. It is not a new pitch script; it connects existing narration to the visual evidence the investor must see.

| Slide or segment | Visual readiness | Spoken landmark | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product context | Task name fully visible | Founder identifies the task | Viewer has context before interaction |
| Demonstration | Input state visible | Founder names the selected example | Actual result has appeared |
| Result explanation | Final value readable | Founder explains the observed change | Qualification has finished |
| Closing frame | Contact and next step visible | Final invitation complete | Intentional end hold |

Add actual slide numbers and rehearsal timecodes in your working copy. “Slide 4, result appears at 00:47; narration says ‘confirmed’ at 00:49” exposes a mismatch that “timing looks fine” conceals.

If a build requires a click, record who or what triggers it during capture. Decide whether the exported video should preserve that recorded timing. If a slide contains a movie, allow enough time for both the movie and any explanation that follows it.

Do not fill every silence automatically. A short hold can let an investor inspect a result after the founder stops speaking. The relevant question is whether the hold serves a visible purpose, not whether continuous narration sounds more polished.

## Record narration with the actual builds

Rehearse the sequence in the same application and mode used for capture. Speak over the real visual progression, not a separate reading of speaker notes while imagining when the slides will move.

Make a short trial that includes one build and one embedded product segment. Play the saved result before recording the full deck. Confirm that the microphone, visual sequence and product audio are present in the relationship you intended.

If you make a narration mistake, use the recording workflow appropriate to your application, then inspect the affected area. [Apple’s Keynote recording documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-mide/guide/keynote/tan81813d552/mac) describes rerecording from a selected point. That is a separate workflow from repairing an already rendered movie.

After a repair, check the transition into the next slide and the end of the recording. A corrected sentence can alter the duration needed for a build or leave a later explanation starting before its visual. Listen for meaning, not merely clean audio.

## Select the export timing deliberately

In supported Windows PowerPoint versions, [Microsoft’s video-export instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/turn-your-presentation-into-a-video) provide a choice to use recorded timings and narration. Select the setting that matches your finished recording rather than accepting an unexamined default.

Record the chosen export options privately. Include whether recorded narration and timings were used, the output format and the quality selection. These notes help explain a later mismatch without turning the investor-facing description into a technical report.

If you intentionally use fixed slide durations instead, treat that as a different presentation. A fixed duration does not know when the founder finishes a qualification or when the product clip reaches its result. Rebuild the ledger around that choice and test every affected slide.

Wait for export completion before copying or uploading the movie. Then open the actual file in a separate player. A correct preview inside the authoring application is useful, but it is not the delivered artifact you are approving.

## Find timing defects by their visible symptoms

Watch once without pausing. Mark where a sentence refers to something that has not appeared, where a slide disappears before its explanation ends, or where the recording seems to finish unexpectedly. These are viewer-level defects that can get lost in detailed timeline inspection.

Then inspect the marked intervals. A fictional ledger might show slide 5 changing at 01:12 while the founder’s qualification ends at 01:15. Decide whether to extend the slide, shorten the wording or rerecord the transition. Do not repair the problem by deleting a material qualification.

Check embedded product media separately. Confirm the visible first frame, the action, the actual result and the transition back to the deck. If an element did not survive export, investigate the specific object and workflow instead of assuming all presentation media behaves identically.

Watch the final frame through the player’s end behavior. Give the viewer a deliberate conclusion, without letting an accidental blank interval resemble a failed export. Check that the final sentence is complete and that any contact information remains readable for its intended hold.

## Release the movie as its own reviewed artifact

Approve the export only after a complete playback with sound. Record the exact filename and duration you reviewed. A later export with the same title but a different revision needs its own check, especially after narration or build changes.

If the video will be embedded in another deck or played during a call, run it through that path as well. Confirm that a second layer of playback does not crop the frame, mask a subtitle or introduce a confusing handoff after the closing slide.

Keep the approved source deck and narration arrangement with your private production materials. Send the investor the intended movie or agreed presentation package, not every intermediate take. Maintain a clear distinction between the editable source and the delivery copy.
