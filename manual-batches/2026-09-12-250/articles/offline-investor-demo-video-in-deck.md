Make a pitch-deck demo work offline by using locally available media, confirming whether it is embedded or linked, and testing the transferred presentation with network access disconnected. A video thumbnail inside a slide does not prove the movie is inside the file. Verify playback from the exact delivery package.

## Identify where the movie actually lives

Open the presentation and inspect each video used in the demo segment. Classify it as embedded local media, a link to a local file, or an online video. These categories can look similar on a slide while depending on very different resources.

[Microsoft distinguishes embedded video from linked local files](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/insert-and-play-a-video-file-from-your-computer). Embedding increases the presentation's size; linking leaves a separate dependency that can break when files move.

Do not infer the category from the image shown on the slide. A poster frame may remain visible even when the underlying media is unavailable. The only useful inventory identifies the actual file or online destination and how the presentation references it.

Use the desktop presentation application for the offline workflow you intend to test. A browser tab that happened to remain open during a rehearsal is not proof that the full presentation can be launched and played without connectivity.

## Replace online dependencies with authorized local media

An online movie is not made offline merely by inserting its URL into a slide. [Microsoft states that website-hosted videos need an internet connection](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/training/insert-a-video-from-youtube-or-another-site) for playback.

For your own demo, obtain the original approved export from your media files and insert that local asset. Do not download someone else's protected material or bypass a service's restrictions just to remove a network dependency. Use media you are entitled to include.

Confirm that the local file is complete and playable before adding it to the deck. A cloud-storage placeholder may look like a file while still needing a download. Make it available locally through the storage application's supported process, then test it after disconnecting.

If the demonstration itself depends on a live service, distinguish that from the prerecorded movie. Making the movie local does not make the product offline-capable. Introduce it as a recording when that is what the audience is seeing.

## Choose an embedded or packaged local arrangement

For a modest demo clip, embedding can simplify delivery because the presentation carries the media. Use the insert option for a local file in your version of PowerPoint, then verify the saved deck. Do not assume an existing linked movie becomes embedded just because you saved the presentation again.

A linked arrangement may be appropriate when the event's workflow requires it, but the separate media must travel with the deck. Keep the deck and linked files together in a deliberate package and test the package after transfer. Avoid relying on a path that exists only on the founder's laptop.

Build a dependency map such as this fictional example:

| Presentation element | Media location | Delivery action | Offline test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main workflow movie | Embedded in the deck | Transfer saved presentation | Play from transferred deck |
| Extended detail clip | Separate local file | Include it in the agreed folder | Open through its actual link |
| Online customer video | Website | Replace with permitted local asset or omit | Do not count it as offline |
| Demo poster frame | Inside slide | Keep as visual context | Confirm it is not mistaken for playback |

Use the map to resolve dependencies before the event. It is not a substitute for testing the finished package.

## Set the start and ending behavior deliberately

Decide whether the movie begins when the slide appears, during the click sequence, or after a direct click on the video. Choose the behavior that matches the founder's actual cue. Test that behavior in slideshow mode rather than only pressing play in the editor.

Rehearse the preceding slide, entry to the movie, full playback, and return to the presentation. A clip that works when opened by itself may still start too early or consume an unexpected click when embedded.

Check what remains on screen after the movie ends. The last frame, poster frame, or next slide should match the explanation you intend to give. Do not leave the founder speaking about a result that has already disappeared.

Keep a note of the chosen start action beside the event copy. If another person operates the deck, they should not need to discover whether “next” means begin the movie or advance past it. This is planned playback preparation, not an emergency recovery routine.

## Run a cold offline test after transferring the package

Copy the final presentation and any required linked files to the intended playback computer or a representative test computer. Close the editing session and media players. Disconnect network access through the device's ordinary controls, then launch the local presentation from its delivery location.

Start from the slide before the demo and perform the exact planned cue. Watch the entire movie, listen to its audio, and check the transition afterward. Do not reconnect halfway through and still mark the test as offline.

If the movie fails, identify the missing dependency. Is the slide calling a website, pointing to an absent local path, or opening an unavailable cloud file? A local file that exists but does not play may have a separate compatibility problem. Record that distinction rather than changing several things at once.

Repeat the test after the repair. If a receiving event computer is not yet available, say the test covers only your current machine. A successful local test is useful evidence, but it does not establish compatibility with an unknown venue setup.

## Hand over the tested package without changing its assumptions

Deliver the exact deck and media arrangement that passed the test. Give the package an unmistakable version name and identify the movie's starting slide and cue. Avoid leaving several similarly named decks for the operator to choose between.

If the event asks for a different format or transfer method, treat that as a new delivery path. Confirm whether media stays embedded, linked files remain available, and the local copy is complete. Recheck playback after conversion or transfer.

Keep your original source movie privately so you can make a legitimate revised export if needed. Do not modify the tested presentation after handover without communicating that a replacement exists and which version should be used.

The finished work product is a dependency map and a transferred deck that plays its planned movie while disconnected. It proves that playback arrangement under tested conditions, not that every future computer will behave identically.
