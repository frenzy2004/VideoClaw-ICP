Prioritize portfolio media requests by separating work that is ready from work that is merely urgent, then apply explicit rules to the ready queue. Consider real deadlines, dependencies and the cost of delaying other companies. Record exceptions and tie-breaks so personal access to a partner does not become the scheduling system.

## Separate requests from commitments

A founder asking for a video review has created a request, not a production reservation. Keep that distinction visible from the beginning. Your register should distinguish received requests, requests awaiting information, accepted work and completed work. A single undifferentiated backlog makes every entry look equally actionable.

Use one work item for a bounded result. “Support our launch” may contain a profile correction, an interview review and a distribution request, each with different dependencies. Split the work only when those parts can be delivered independently. Dividing one inseparable edit into many tickets would make the queue appear more productive without changing what a company receives.

[Atlassian's marketing service template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/templates/marketing-service-management) treats request capture, assignment and tracking as connected activities. Apply that connection across company boundaries: the person answering a founder must be able to see whether work has actually been accepted.

Give each item a company, requested result, needed-by date, deadline reason, supplied materials and next decision. Keep the requester's preferred priority as context, but do not let a self-selected “urgent” label determine the sequence.

## Apply readiness before ranking

A request is ready when the next worker can make useful progress with the information available. That does not require every later step to be settled. It does require the immediate task to be defined.

For an interview-outline review, the audience and proposed participants may be enough. For a factual correction, the changed fact and its confirmation are necessary. For a release decision, an unfinished draft cannot be treated as equivalent to a completed package. Define readiness separately for these kinds of work.

Move missing-input requests into a visible waiting state with a precise return condition. “Waiting for the company” is too broad; “waiting for the company to confirm which product screenshot is current” gives someone an action. Tell the requester whether their original date remains feasible once that dependency is resolved.

The [Kanban Guide](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/) calls for explicit workflow policies and control of work in progress. In this portfolio application, the readiness rule prevents incomplete requests from occupying active slots merely because they arrived first.

Do not use waiting as a way to hide uncomfortable commitments. If the platform itself owes the missing input, record that ownership honestly.

## Choose a rule that explains the next decision

A useful ordering rule answers three questions. Is there a fixed external deadline? Would delaying this work block another accepted commitment? Can the requested result be completed within the available scope?

Use those questions to form a small number of understandable priority classes. For example, correcting an already-public material error may require an interruption. A dated event asset may have a fixed opportunity. An undated improvement can enter the normal queue. These are editorial operating choices, not a universal ranking formula.

Within a class, choose a tie-break in advance. Oldest ready request is easy to explain. A deliberate rotation across companies can also work when access has become concentrated. If you use rotation, describe which companies are eligible and when a turn is considered used.

Avoid an elaborate numerical score unless the team can explain each input and its consequence. Multiplying uncertain urgency by guessed revenue creates a precise-looking answer from weak information. A short decision note is often more defensible than a weighted total.

Also identify who can authorize an interruption. That authority should change the queue visibly, including the displaced item and its new communication plan.

## Work through a fictional queue

Imagine a platform has capacity to start two bounded editorial tasks. The following eight requests are fictional; their order is an example of applying rules, not a recommended service level.

| Company | Request | Readiness | Relevant condition | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder | Correct a public company description | Ready | Confirmed factual error | Start first |
| Beacon | Review a conference profile | Ready | Fixed organizer deadline | Start second |
| Cobalt | Improve an evergreen interview outline | Ready | Oldest normal request | Next normal slot |
| Delta | Review a product clip | Waiting | Current screen recording missing | Return when supplied |
| Ember | Assess a fund-channel feature | Ready | No fixed publication date | Normal queue |
| Finch | Check a release package | Waiting | Company approver unnamed | Clarify first |
| Grove | Review an event introduction | Ready | Event is later than Beacon's | Hold sequence |
| Harbor | Rework an undated company summary | Ready | Recently received | Normal queue |

Alder goes first because the existing publication needs correction, not because Alder is the largest company. Beacon receives the second slot because its outside deadline is confirmed. Cobalt remains visibly next among normal requests; an urgent insertion does not silently erase its age.

Delta's founder might say the clip is more important than Beacon's profile. That may be true for Delta, but the missing recording still prevents useful work. Offer a narrower task if one can proceed, such as checking the intended claim, and record that as a separate accepted result.

## Handle exceptions without creating a second queue

When a partner asks staff to move a company ahead, request the same factual explanation used for every other exception. What changed? What external dependency exists? Which accepted work would move? This keeps the conversation about the task rather than the influence of the messenger.

Record a short exception note with the reason, authorizer, displaced work and notification. Do not record speculative company valuations or sensitive investor discussions in a shared production board. Most scheduling decisions need far less information.

Tell affected companies early when their accepted sequence changes. Explain the updated next step and offer a narrower deliverable if that would preserve their purpose. Avoid inventing a new delivery date before checking the actual work.

If interruptions recur, investigate the pattern. Perhaps companies do not know lead-in requirements, the service menu is vague, or the fund has promised more distribution support than its channels can absorb. Treat repeated exceptions as operating evidence rather than normalizing constant emergency work.

## Revisit the policy using actual cases

At the end of a working cycle, review a few decisions rather than only the number of completed items. Examine a request that moved quickly, one that aged and one that was interrupted. Ask whether the policy explained the outcome to someone outside the platform team.

Look for systematic disadvantage. A company with a less experienced marketer may submit incomplete requests repeatedly. The solution may be a clearer example or an intake conversation, rather than leaving that company permanently outside the ready queue. Fair access includes helping people understand readiness.

Track how often work is returned for the same missing input, how often exceptions displace normal work and whether ready requests remain untouched. These observations support policy changes; they do not measure company quality or employee value.

Publish the resulting rules where founders request support. Keep individual sensitive details private, but make the sequence understandable. A credible queue lets the platform say both “this matters” and “this is when we can act” without pretending every request can be first.
