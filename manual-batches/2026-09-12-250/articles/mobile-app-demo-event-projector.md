Show a live mobile app on an event screen by testing the complete signal path from the actual phone to the venue's selected display input. Verify orientation, readable interaction, sound if needed, and who controls the source. A working connection at your desk does not establish that the stage setup is ready.

## Identify the phone and the venue input before rehearsal

Ask the event contact what connection the presentation position provides, which display it feeds, and who selects that input. A cable on the lectern may connect through a switcher rather than directly to the projector. Record the actual path instead of guessing from the connector's shape.

Identify the exact phone model, operating system, application build, and required orientation. Confirm whether the app needs network access, an authenticated test account, or another device during the demonstration. Display connectivity and product connectivity are separate requirements.

For an iPhone cable example, [Apple documents connecting through an appropriate adapter and selecting the display's input](https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/iphone/iph93af723b9/ios). Other phones have their own output capabilities and requirements; do not infer support solely because a device has a similar port.

Keep this a setup decision, not a shopping comparison. The useful question is whether the equipment already planned for this phone and venue can show the actual app task clearly.

## Draw the signal path and assign source control

Write the path in the order the picture travels. A typical wired rehearsal record might read:

Phone with the demo app → compatible display adapter → tested video cable → venue switcher input → projector → audience screen.

For each connection, name the actual component used in the test. Add power and network as separate notes rather than treating them as part of the video cable. If the adapter supports simultaneous charging and you intend to use it, test that arrangement too.

Identify who can change the source. The founder may control the phone while an AV operator controls the switcher. Agree on the cue that brings the phone image to the screen and the cue that returns to the deck.

If the venue proposes wireless mirroring, record the actual receiver and connection method instead of writing only “cast.” Confirm its current requirements with the relevant device documentation and the venue. Do not assume that a successful home-network test covers the venue's network or receiver.

## Connect the actual app, not just the home screen

A mirrored home screen proves only that an image reached the display. Open the demo app and perform the planned task. Watch for a blank region, a different external-display layout, or a view that changes when the app enters full screen.

[Apple's adapter guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108399) notes that app compatibility matters and that VGA does not carry audio. Test the product's actual behavior and any required sound rather than treating the cable connection as the whole rehearsal.

Keep the phone in its intended operating position. A device held freely in one hand may rotate or pull on the cable differently from one resting on the desk. Arrange the cable so ordinary tapping does not strain the connector or create a trip hazard.

Do not disable important product behavior simply to make mirroring appear successful. If the app presents a special external view, evaluate whether that view still demonstrates the claim. A different interface may be useful, but it should not be confused with the ordinary phone experience.

## Test orientation and readability through the whole task

Choose portrait or landscape based on the app's real workflow. A portrait app will often occupy a narrow portion of a wide event screen. Stretching it to fill the screen can distort the interface and does not create more useful evidence.

Stand at a realistic audience position and inspect the critical detail: the selected item, the action, and the result. If you cannot read the decisive state, simplify the demonstrated path or use an in-app display adjustment that genuinely exists and remains representative.

Walk through every screen transition. A keyboard, permission prompt, camera view, or modal may change the visible area. A clean starting screen is not enough if the result becomes tiny or partially hidden.

Test rotation deliberately, including any point where the app changes orientation. Decide whether to lock a supported orientation for the demo or to preserve the transition as part of the actual experience. Rehearse the chosen behavior rather than relying on how the phone happened to be held during setup.

## Keep a phone-to-screen rehearsal record

Use a compact record that describes the tested configuration and the task, not just the equipment inventory. The following fictional example concerns a mobile appointment app.

| Item | Rehearsal entry | Evidence of readiness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone and app | Exact device and demo build | Planned task opens correctly |
| Signal route | Adapter, cable, switcher input and projector | Actual app reaches the audience screen |
| Orientation | Portrait throughout the task | No unintended rotation or distortion |
| Critical detail | Selected appointment and confirmation state | Readable from the tested audience position |
| Network | Required venue connection or permitted alternative | App completes the intended interaction |
| Audio | Needed or intentionally absent | Correct output path verified if used |
| Source control | AV operator selects the phone input | Start and return cues rehearsed |

Record unresolved items plainly. “Projector unavailable; tested on a monitor” is useful information. It is not equivalent to an event-screen pass.

Keep the exact cable and adapter together after testing. A visually similar replacement can change the setup. If the event changes a component, repeat the relevant end-to-end check.

## Rehearse the stage interaction and the return to slides

Begin from the same presentation state the audience will see before the phone demo. Give the agreed cue, wait for the phone image to appear, and then start the task. Do not tap through the decisive action while the operator is still selecting the input.

Narrate what the audience can see, not just what appears on your handset. If the venue offers a confidence monitor, use it to confirm the public view without turning away from the audience for every tap.

At the end, hold the result long enough to inspect. Stop interacting with the phone before asking the operator to return to the deck. That prevents an unrelated home screen, notification, or account switch from becoming the final public image.

Repeat the whole sequence after any significant change to the phone, app build, signal path, or event display. The finished work product is a tested phone-to-screen path and a brief rehearsal record. It demonstrates readiness for this setup, not universal device compatibility.
