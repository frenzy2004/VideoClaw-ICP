Make a partnership announcement useful by explaining the customer job, each company's contribution, who can use the offer, and what is available now. Distinguish a signed agreement from delivered functionality, name important exclusions, and show one concrete customer path. The announcement should help someone act, not leave them interpreting two logos and matching expressions of excitement.

## Identify the kind of partnership before writing the headline

Partnership is a broad word. It can describe a product integration, a referral arrangement, a service relationship, or access to a program. Those arrangements create different expectations for customers.

[Notion's first-party account distinguishes technology, consulting, startup, and affiliate partnerships](https://www.notion.com/blog/how-notion-builds-partnerships-using-notion-and-how-you-can-too). The relevant lesson is to name the actual contribution rather than treating every relationship as the same kind of collaboration.

Write a plain description before choosing promotional wording: “Company A provides the workflow software; Company B offers optional implementation support.” That is more informative than “Two leaders join forces to transform work.”

If the relationship is only an agreement to explore something, say so. A customer should not have to discover that the promised experience is still a plan.

Confirm who has authority to approve the description on both sides. A founder's enthusiasm does not authorize claims about the partner's product, customer access, or commitments.

## Build a partnership value map

Create six fields: shared customer job, your contribution, partner contribution, eligible audience, availability, and exclusions. Add the customer response owner and the current written reference.

For a fictional pairing, imagine Reviewlane provides review software and Workshop North provides optional workflow-setup sessions. The shared job is helping a customer establish a review process. Reviewlane supplies the product; Workshop North supplies the human service. The eligible audience is a defined customer group, and the sessions require separate scheduling.

The exclusion is important: the partnership does not mean every customer receives consulting automatically or that the software includes a new automated workflow. Those would be different claims.

Fill the map with verified facts, not desired outcomes. “Customers can request a setup session” is a capability statement. “Customers will become more productive” is an outcome claim that requires separate support.

Ask whether the announcement remains useful when the partner names are removed. If the customer job disappears, the message may be relying too heavily on brand association.

## Verify the route from announcement to actual use

Walk through the experience as the intended customer. Can they identify eligibility, find the next step, understand any separate arrangement, and reach the right person with a question?

[Notion's Slack integration documentation describes concrete actions, setup, and permission conditions](https://www.notion.com/help/slack). It illustrates the difference between saying two products work together and explaining what a user can actually do. Do not assume those product details apply to your own partnership.

For your offer, test each step with the responsible team. If an application is required, explain what happens after submission. If access is limited, do not make the headline sound universally available. If the partner owns a service, identify that responsibility clearly.

Keep planned functionality separate from current functionality. A signed agreement, a private trial, and a generally available feature are different states. Your announcement should use the correct one.

Do not publish a next step that leads to a generic form nobody recognizes. The receiving team should know what the customer was told.

## Write a complete fictional customer path

In the fictional Reviewlane example, a workspace owner wants help defining who reviews and who approves work. The announcement tells them that optional setup sessions are available to a specified customer group through Workshop North.

The owner checks the eligibility note, follows the established request route, and receives scheduling information from the partner. Reviewlane remains responsible for its software support; Workshop North handles the session arrangement. The customer is told where to ask which kind of question.

The announcement also says what does not happen: requesting a session does not change workspace permissions automatically, and the service is not included for every account. Any actual pricing or contractual terms would need to be stated in the verified offer, not invented for this example.

Now test a poor-fit reader. A customer outside the eligible group should be able to recognize that fact without completing a long application. Give them a clear alternative or say that no alternative is currently available.

A complete path makes exclusions visible as part of the offer rather than as disappointment at the end.

## Give the founder a specific role in the announcement

The founder's contribution is to explain why the relationship makes sense for customers. Use a quote or short video to connect the shared job with the complementary contributions.

An original fictional line might be: “Our software organizes the review, but some teams need help deciding who owns each step. This arrangement gives the eligible customer group a separate route to that setup support.” It explains the boundary instead of promising a transformed business.

If both founders appear, assign different questions. One explains the product's role; the other explains the service or integration contribution. Avoid two versions of the same celebration.

Have both sides approve the factual meaning, named roles, and availability conditions. Check that a short quote remains accurate without the rest of the announcement.

Keep the visual truthful. A joint logo image should not imply an acquisition, endorsement of every product, or completed integration unless that is what the relationship actually means.

## Align release details and maintain the explanation

Before release, compare both companies' versions. The audience, availability, exclusions, and next step should agree. A customer should not receive a broader promise from one side than the other is prepared to honor.

Name a contact for factual changes. If availability moves, update the announcement and the response instructions together. If a partner's page remains outdated, flag the mismatch rather than silently accepting two conflicting public descriptions.

Prepare a short answer sheet for likely questions: who qualifies, what each company provides, what is not included, and where current details live. Keep commercial or legal terms with their authoritative owners.

After publication, review incoming questions for confusion. Do not treat clicks or congratulatory comments as proof that the partnership delivers the intended value. They show attention, not a completed customer outcome.
