In the first 48 hours after Demo Day, log every investor request, assign an answer owner, clear the required evidence and privacy decisions, send the smallest useful response, and schedule the next conversation or evidence delivery. Use three operating windows: zero to four hours for capture and acknowledgment, four to twenty-four hours for routing and review, and twenty-four to forty-eight hours for completed responses and next decisions.

Those windows are editorial targets, not universal investor expectations or accelerator rules. A direct request made during the event may deserve an earlier answer. A sensitive metric, customer reference, security question, or new product video may take longer. Speed should make ownership visible; it should not turn an unverified answer into a deadline.

## Build the board before Demo Day starts

Do not wait until the event ends to decide where names and questions go. Prepare a board with one row per investor interaction and these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Contact and firm | Identifies the recipient without relying on memory |
| Source and time | Records where and when the interaction occurred |
| Exact request | Preserves the investor's wording |
| Decision stage | Introductory, exploratory, diligence, partner discussion, or another known state |
| Response type | Email answer, deck, memo, demo, data-room item, reference, or meeting |
| Evidence owner | Confirms the claim, number, product state, or source |
| Disclosure owner | Reviews privacy, confidentiality, rights, or fundraising context |
| Response owner | Writes and sends the answer |
| Next decision | Defines what the follow-up should accomplish |
| Due and status | Makes the operating target and blocker visible |

Use the investor's exact words before translating them into an internal task. “Send more information” is not enough. Ask or record which part mattered: product, market, team, traction, business model, financing, or next meeting. A more precise request produces a smaller and safer response.

Set four statuses: captured, needs clarification, in review, and sent or scheduled. Add blocked as a separate visible state rather than hiding a delay inside “in review.” A blocked row should name the missing source or decision and the owner who can resolve it.

## Zero to four hours: capture, classify, and acknowledge

The first window is for memory preservation and routing. It is not a race to send every asset.

### Capture the interaction

For every relevant conversation:

1. Record the person's name, firm, and reliable contact path.
2. Write the exact question or request.
3. Note what the founder promised to send or schedule.
4. Identify any other team member who heard the conversation.
5. Mark information that appeared sensitive, uncertain, or new.
6. Attach the approved event notes rather than copying facts from memory.

If the team cannot confirm what was requested, send a short clarification instead of guessing. “You asked about our product review workflow; would a two-minute recorded walkthrough or a live product session be more useful?” is better than sending both a general deck and a long video.

### Assign one response owner

Each row needs one person accountable for the next action. Contributors can supply evidence or review, but the investor should not receive separate, inconsistent replies from a founder, accelerator mentor, and team member.

The response owner should be able to answer:

- What is the request?
- Which format best answers it?
- Who must approve the facts or access?
- What is the next investor decision?
- What will we send if the preferred asset is blocked?

### Acknowledge without pretending the answer is complete

An acknowledgment can confirm the request and set a realistic next step:

> Thank you for the conversation. I captured your question about [specific topic]. [Owner] is checking the current [source or product state], and I will send [the agreed item] or confirm the next session by [time or date].

Do not call the answer “on the way” if no owner or source exists. Do not attach an event deck by default simply to make the reply feel fast.

## Four to twenty-four hours: route the question and clear evidence

The second window converts requests into approved responses. Start with the smallest suitable format.

| Request class | Default route | Evidence or review gate |
|---|---|---|
| Company overview | Current approved deck or recap | Version, date, fundraising wording, and distribution |
| Product workflow | Narrow recorded walkthrough or live session | Product state, data, claims, rights, and fallback |
| Metric definition | Sourced written answer or controlled diligence item | Definition, population, period, owner, and access |
| Strategic reasoning | Short memo or meeting | Facts versus interpretation, confidentiality, and assumptions |
| Customer evidence | Permissioned source, reference process, or controlled item | Customer permission, scope, and exact wording |
| Security or privacy | Authorized owner and controlled response | Approved policy, system boundary, recipient, and disclosure path |

Y Combinator's [seed fundraising guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/) supports a focused fundraising process built around investor conversations. The routing table and hour windows are editorial operations added by this article; YC does not prescribe this 48-hour board.

### Check every objective statement

For a number or factual claim, attach:

- the exact approved wording;
- the source record;
- the owner;
- the date or measurement period;
- the population, product state, or scope;
- the audience and access decision;
- the trigger that would make it stale.

If the source does not support the sentence, narrow the sentence or remove it. Do not create a new chart or video merely to make an unsupported answer look more complete.

### Review visible data before producing media

A post-event product video can expose more than the speaker notices: customer names, email addresses, internal URLs, support tickets, file paths, browser history, account roles, notifications, or unreleased interfaces. Review the complete frame, not only the feature being demonstrated.

The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework) provides a risk-management framework for identifying and managing privacy risk. It does not certify a specific recording or dictate these time windows. The editorial application here is to add an explicit privacy owner and a no-send state when visible data or use context has not been cleared.

Use controlled fictional data when appropriate, and label the product state accurately. Do not imply that a prepared example is a customer result.

## Twenty-four to forty-eight hours: send the response and secure the next decision

By the final window, each high-priority row should end in one of four outcomes:

1. **Sent:** the requested answer and smallest useful asset were delivered.
2. **Scheduled:** a live discussion or demonstration was booked with an agenda.
3. **Clarified:** the investor refined the request, creating a new owned response.
4. **Blocked with a stated next date:** the team explained that a source, permission, or review was needed and named the follow-up owner.

“Sent” is not the final business outcome. It means the team completed the agreed response accurately. Do not mark a row complete merely because an automated email sequence fired.

### Write for forwarding

Assume the response may reach another member of the investor's team. Include enough context that the asset does not become misleading when separated from the live conversation.

A useful response contains:

- one sentence reconnecting to the exact question;
- a direct answer or clear statement of what remains open;
- the selected asset with version or product-state context;
- any important access or forwarding boundary;
- one proposed next decision, meeting, or evidence delivery;
- a named person who can answer the reply.

Do not rely on a vague subject line or an unlabeled link. A filename such as “final-final-demo-2” does not tell a forwarded reader the date, product state, or purpose.

### Schedule the decision, not a generic catch-up

If the next step is a meeting, name its purpose. Examples include:

- verify product fit against a specific portfolio thesis;
- walk through one product workflow;
- resolve a metric definition;
- introduce another partner or domain expert;
- review the next diligence materials;
- decide whether both sides should continue the process.

An agenda helps the team prepare the right owner and evidence. It also prevents a follow-up call from becoming a repetition of the Demo Day pitch.

Techstars' [pitch guidance](https://toolkit.techstars.com/master-your-pitch) supports clear, prepared communication. The decision-centered meeting language is editorial guidance, not a promise about investor progression.

## Prioritize requests without inventing investor intent

When the board fills quickly, prioritize based on explicit signals and response dependencies rather than prestige or guesswork.

| Priority input | Responsible interpretation |
|---|---|
| A specific request with a date | Meet or renegotiate the stated commitment |
| A scheduled partner or diligence discussion | Prepare the exact decision materials |
| A request requiring another person's permission | Start the permission process early |
| A sensitive or material question | Route to the authorized reviewer before responding |
| A generic compliment with no request | Thank the person and ask whether a next step is useful |
| Social engagement without direct contact | Do not infer investor interest; treat it as public engagement |

Do not label someone “high intent” because they viewed a page, liked a post, or attended the event. Use the request, meeting, and stated process as evidence of the next action.

If two responses compete for the same evidence owner, protect accuracy first. Send a concise acknowledgment to the other recipient and state when the answer can be reviewed. Hidden delays damage accountability more than a transparent review gate.

## Build a safe fast-response asset shelf

Preparation can reduce follow-up time without pre-writing answers to questions nobody asked. Before Demo Day, keep a small approved shelf:

- current company overview with owner and date;
- narrow product sequences with product-state labels;
- metric definitions linked to source records;
- approved team and company facts;
- common privacy, security, and rights routing contacts;
- a blank investor-response record;
- a meeting-agenda template;
- an explicit list of information that requires controlled access or review.

Each item needs a refresh trigger. A product release, changed metric, withdrawn customer permission, fundraising update, or modified availability statement can make an approved asset stale.

The shelf is not a mass-send package. It shortens the path from a real question to a reviewed response. The response owner still selects only what the recipient requested.

## The 48-hour board in practice

Use this example with fictional facts:

| Window | Request | Owner action | Gate | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 hours | “Show the approval handoff” | Capture wording and ask whether recorded or live is preferred | None for clarification | Investor requests a live session |
| 4–24 hours | Live session | Product owner selects current test environment and sequence | Product state and data review | Agenda and demo path approved |
| 24–48 hours | Session scheduling | Founder offers two times and names the decision | Access and attendee check | Product workflow meeting scheduled |

The example does not describe a VideoClaw customer, outcome, or product performance. It illustrates how one request moves through the board without creating an unnecessary public video.

## Final operating checklist

Before the 48-hour window closes, verify:

- Every relevant interaction has an exact request or a clarification action.
- Every row has one response owner and one evidence owner where needed.
- Sensitive or material questions are with the authorized reviewer.
- The selected format is the smallest accurate response.
- Product media has product-state, data, rights, and claim review.
- Sent assets carry version and context for forwarding.
- Meetings have a decision-centered agenda.
- Blocked items name the missing source, owner, and next date.
- No engagement signal has been presented as investor intent.
- Asset refresh triggers are recorded.
