An application video introduces the founders in the format requested by a program; a product demo proves one workflow; a Demo Day pitch creates enough investor interest for a next conversation; and a follow-up video answers a question from someone already engaged. Reusing one finished file for all four usually gives the wrong audience, evidence emphasis, context, or CTA—even when the raw footage overlaps.

Choose the format by the viewer's decision, not by which edit is already on the team's drive. Then follow the exact instructions of the accelerator, application, event, or recipient.

## The four formats at a glance

| Format | Primary audience | Main question | Evidence emphasis | Typical next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Application video | Accelerator selection team | Who are these founders, and how do they communicate? | Founder presence and concise company context, within the program's rules | Continue evaluating the application |
| Product demo | Reviewer, investor, customer, or technical stakeholder | Does this workflow or capability work as described? | Clean product action and observable result | Request technical or product follow-up |
| Demo Day pitch | Invited investor or event audience | Is this company worth a conversation now? | Memorable company case with selected proof | Book or continue a meeting |
| Follow-up video | Known person with a specific question | Can the company resolve this remaining question? | Narrow, context-aware evidence | Continue diligence, product review, or discussion |

This table describes editorial jobs, not universal program definitions. An accelerator can ask for a differently named or combined asset. Its current instructions control the submission.

## Application video: show the founders in the requested context

An accelerator application video is not automatically a fundraising pitch recording. The [Y Combinator application-video page](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/) distinguishes the founder video from a product demo and gives program-specific instructions. That distinction matters: the selection team may be evaluating the founders' communication, relationship, and understanding rather than asking for a polished product advertisement.

For an application video:

- read the current program instructions immediately before recording;
- include the required people and information;
- use the requested duration, orientation, file, and submission method;
- speak naturally without adding unrequested production layers;
- keep the company description accurate and consistent with the written application;
- do not insert a product tour merely because one already exists;
- review the exact submitted file and link.

The [Techstars application preview](https://www.techstars.com/application-preview) shows that an accelerator application can collect team, company, deck, and product information in separate fields or materials. That is a useful reminder: one video does not need to duplicate every part of the application.

**Do not reuse automatically:** a public launch video may expose information inappropriate for an application; a Demo Day pitch may assume the company has already been selected; a product demo may reveal little about the founders. Reuse source takes only after checking the new requirement.

## Product demo: prove one workflow without hiding the boundary

A product demo should answer a product question. It begins with a clean state, shows one meaningful action, and holds on an observable result. The founder or narrator explains what the viewer is seeing and why it matters.

A useful product-demo brief includes:

1. **User situation:** who is trying to accomplish what?
2. **Starting state:** what does the viewer need to recognize before the action?
3. **Action:** which product behavior will be shown?
4. **Result:** what changes on screen?
5. **Boundary:** is this a live environment, controlled example, prototype, or edited sequence?
6. **Next question:** what should the viewer examine after the demo?

Protect the recording environment. Remove personal data, customer records, credentials, notifications, hidden tabs, and unreleased material. Use synthetic or explicitly approved sample data. Record the build or state shown so the claim owner can confirm that the narration matches it.

**Do not reuse automatically:** a Demo Day audience may need less technical detail and more company context. An application may explicitly separate product material. A follow-up recipient may need a narrower answer than the broad demo provides.

## Demo Day pitch: make the company memorable enough for follow-up

A Demo Day pitch is a time- and context-bound event asset. The audience may be seeing many companies. The pitch must communicate the customer, important problem, company change, selected proof, and next step without becoming a complete diligence process.

The [Techstars pitch toolkit](https://toolkit.techstars.com/master-your-pitch) frames pitch work around the audience, message, and outcome. Translate that into a recording or event video by asking:

- What does this audience already know from the event materials?
- Which three or four ideas must survive one viewing?
- Which fact or product moment deserves screen time?
- What wording has been approved for the event audience?
- What should an interested viewer do next?
- What backup preserves the pitch if playback fails?

The Demo Day video may be a recording of the pitch, a supporting product clip, a prerecorded pitch, or another event-specific asset. Follow the organizer's terminology and technical requirements. Do not assume a public release is permitted merely because the video was shown to an invited audience.

**Do not reuse automatically:** application language may be outdated; event-only references may confuse a later viewer; customer or investor statements may have limited permission; fundraising context can change; a Demo Day CTA may expire.

## Follow-up video: answer the next question, not the first one again

A follow-up video has the narrowest audience and the most context. The recipient has already seen some combination of the application, deck, pitch, demo, or meeting. Repeating the general pitch wastes that context.

Start the brief with the actual question:

> After **[previous interaction]**, **[recipient or role]** needs to understand **[specific issue]** before **[next decision]**.

Then include only the evidence needed for that issue. Examples:

- a technical stakeholder asks to see one workflow with clearer narration;
- an investor asks how a product result is produced;
- an accelerator mentor asks for a revised customer explanation;
- a prospective design partner asks what an onboarding sequence looks like;
- a team member needs an approved version to share internally.

Keep the distribution boundary visible. A narrow response may contain context or evidence unsuitable for a public page. Name the intended recipient class, link destination, owner, and expiry or review condition.

**Do not reuse automatically:** a follow-up answer often assumes prior knowledge. It may be too detailed, too confidential, or too narrow for a broad audience.

## A decision tree for choosing the format

Use these questions in order:

1. **Did a program or recipient request a named asset?** Follow that requirement and verify the current instructions.
2. **Is the central question about the founders?** Use an application or founder-introduction format as requested.
3. **Is the central question whether a workflow works?** Use a product demo with a clean proof path.
4. **Is the central question whether to start an investor conversation?** Use the event's Demo Day pitch format.
5. **Has the viewer already engaged and asked something specific?** Use a follow-up video.
6. **Are two questions equally important?** Make separate chapters only if the delivery context allows it; otherwise make two assets.

Do not select “pitch video” simply because search results or software templates use that label. Write the viewer, question, evidence, and next step. The format should be obvious from those fields.

## What can be reused safely

Separate source reuse from finished-file reuse.

| Element | Often reusable after review | What must be rechecked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved company description | Yes | Current wording, audience, and claims |
| Founder introduction take | Sometimes | Program format, date, team changes, and tone |
| Product capture | Sometimes | Build, sample data, permissions, readability, and narration |
| Customer or metric statement | Only with controls | Source date, wording, permission, audience, and approver |
| Captions and transcript | Yes as a starting point | New edit timing, names, numbers, and CTA |
| Music, logos, and third-party visuals | Only within recorded rights | Territory, channel, duration, audience, and license scope |
| CTA | Rarely without change | Destination, owner, availability, and intended action |

Create a source pack with scene IDs, approved wording, permissions, and usage notes. Mark each item “reusable as approved,” “review before reuse,” or “restricted to original context.” That is more reliable than assuming the latest export is a universal master.

## Example: one startup across the accelerator journey

Consider a fictional US software startup in an accelerator. The example is illustrative and contains no customer or performance claim.

**Application:** Two founders explain who they are, the customer situation they observed, and why they are working on it, following the program's current instructions. A separate link contains the requested product material.

**Product demo:** A clean test account shows one user action and its visible result. The narration identifies the controlled environment and avoids implying unsupported production usage.

**Demo Day pitch:** The founder presents the customer, problem, company change, selected evidence, and invitation for an investor follow-up. A brief product sequence supports one point rather than replacing the pitch.

**Follow-up:** An interested viewer asks how the demonstrated result is produced. The team records a focused explanation using approved technical material and sends it through the agreed channel.

The company may reuse the approved one-sentence description, founder source takes, and product capture. The edit, context, evidence emphasis, and CTA change because the viewer's decision changes.

## Format selection checklist

- [ ] The current requester and instructions are recorded.
- [ ] One viewer and one question define the asset.
- [ ] The chosen format matches that question.
- [ ] Required duration and technical constraints come from the actual program or recipient.
- [ ] Every factual statement has a source and approver.
- [ ] Product footage uses a clean, permitted environment.
- [ ] The CTA has one owner and working destination.
- [ ] Captions, transcript, and playback have been reviewed.
- [ ] Distribution and reuse boundaries are written down.
- [ ] The exact submitted or released version has final approval.
